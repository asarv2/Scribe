\underline{\text{Separation Theorem}} \\
\text{THEOREM 10.4. Let } P \text{ and } \bar{P} \text{ be two disjoint nonempty polyhedra in } \mathbb{R}^n \text{. Then} \\
\text{there exist disjoint half-spaces } H \text{ and } \bar{H} \text{ such that } P \subset H \text{ and } \bar{P} \subset \bar{H}\text{.} \\
\{ x : v^T x \le c \} \qquad \text{intersection of finitely many half spaces} \quad \{ x : Ax \le b \}

<DESCRIPTION>This slide presents the Separation Theorem (Theorem 10.4) for Linear Programming.  The theorem states that given two disjoint nonempty polyhedra, $P$ and $\bar{P}$, in $\mathbb{R}^n$, there exist two disjoint half-spaces, $H$ and $\bar{H}$, such that $P \subset H$ and $\bar{P} \subset \bar{H}$.  The notation $\{x : v^Tx \le c\}$ represents a half-space, and $\{x : Ax \le b\}$ represents a polyhedron as an intersection of finitely many half-spaces. Two figures illustrate the theorem: the first shows a simple case where a line separates two polyhedra, and the second shows a more complex case where intersecting lines separate two polyhedra.  The theorem is fundamental in linear programming, often used in duality theory and algorithm design. The underlining emphasizes the key components of the theorem.  The figures visually represent the concept of separating disjoint polyhedra using hyperplanes.</DESCRIPTION>

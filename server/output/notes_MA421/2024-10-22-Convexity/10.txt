\underline{\text{Separation Theorem}} \\
\text{THEOREM 10.4. Let } P \text{ and } \bar{P} \text{ be two disjoint nonempty polyhedra in } \mathbb{R}^n \text{. Then} \\
\text{there exist disjoint half-spaces } H \text{ and } \bar{H} \text{ such that } P \subset H \text{ and } \bar{P} \subset \bar{H}\text{.} \\
\{ x : v^T x \le c \} \qquad \text{intersection of finitely many half spaces} \quad \{ x : Ax \le b \}
</LATEX>

<DESCRIPTION>This slide presents the Separation Theorem (Theorem 10.4) in the context of Linear Programming. The theorem states that if $P$ and $\bar{P}$ are two disjoint nonempty polyhedra in $\mathbb{R}^n$, then there exist two disjoint half-spaces, $H$ and $\bar{H}$, such that $P$ is a subset of $H$ and $\bar{P}$ is a subset of $\bar{H}$.  The slide illustrates this with two figures. The first figure shows a simple case with two polyhedra separated by a single hyperplane (line in 2D). The second figure depicts a more complex scenario where two polyhedra are separated by the intersection of multiple hyperplanes. The notation $\{x: v^Tx \le c\}$ represents a half-space defined by a hyperplane, and $\{x: Ax \le b\}$ represents the intersection of multiple half-spaces, which forms a polyhedron.  The underlining emphasizes key terms and conditions in the theorem statement. The context of linear programming is crucial because the separation theorem is a fundamental result used in various algorithms for solving linear programs, particularly those involving duality and optimality conditions.</DESCRIPTION>

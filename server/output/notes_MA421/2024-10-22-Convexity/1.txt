\underline{\text{Def 1}} \quad S \subseteq \mathbb{R}^m \text{ is } \underline{\text{convex}} \text{ if} \\
\text{for any } z_1, z_2 \in S \implies tz_1 + (1-t)z_2 \in S \\
\qquad 0 < t < 1 \\
\underline{\text{Def 2}} \text{ Convex combination of } z_1, z_2, \dots, z_n \in \mathbb{R}^m \\
t_1 z_1 + t_2 z_2 + \dots + t_n z_n \quad t_i \ge 0 \\
\sum_i t_i = 1
</LATEX>

<DESCRIPTION>This slide introduces two definitions fundamental to convex analysis in the context of linear programming.  Definition 1 defines a convex set $S$ in $\mathbb{R}^m$ as a set where, for any two points $z_1$ and $z_2$ in $S$, the line segment connecting them ($tz_1 + (1-t)z_2$ for $0 < t < 1$) is entirely contained within $S$.  Definition 2 defines a convex combination of points $z_1, z_2, \dots, z_n$ in $\mathbb{R}^m$ as a weighted sum $t_1z_1 + t_2z_2 + \dots + t_nz_n$, where the weights $t_i$ are non-negative and sum to 1.  The figures illustrate examples of convex combinations for 2, 3, and 4 points, visually representing the concept of a weighted average within the convex hull of the points. The underlining emphasizes key terms and conditions in the definitions. The context of linear programming is crucial because convex sets are essential for characterizing feasible regions and ensuring the existence and uniqueness of optimal solutions in many linear programming problems.</DESCRIPTION>

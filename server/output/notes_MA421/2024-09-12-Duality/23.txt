Slide 23

\textbf{Complementary Slackness}
\newline
$\mathcal{P} \ \sum_{j} c_j x_j = \sum_{j} (\sum_{i} a_{ij} y_i) x_j$
\newline
$0 = \sum_{j} (\sum_{i} a_{ij} y_i - c_j) x_j$
\newline
$\implies \sum_{j} (\sum_{i} a_{ij} y_i - c_j) x_j \ge 0$
\newline
$\implies \sum_{j} z_j x_j \ge 0$
\newline
$\implies z_j x_j = 0$

\textbf{Description:}

This slide continues the discussion of complementary slackness in linear programming.  It shows how the equality of the primal and dual objective functions at the optimal solution is related to the complementary slackness conditions.  The equations demonstrate that the difference between the primal objective function and the dual objective function is zero at the optimal solution.  This is a crucial step in the proof, as it demonstrates the equality of the optimal values of the primal and dual objective functions.  The slide likely follows previous ones demonstrating simplex iterations and now highlights the characteristics of the tableau at the optimal solution for the dual problem.  The notation $x_j$, $y_i$, $w_i$, and $z_j$ represent the primal and dual variables and their corresponding slack variables.  The equations show how the constraints ($Ax \le b$ and $A^T y \ge c$) are incorporated into the objective function values.

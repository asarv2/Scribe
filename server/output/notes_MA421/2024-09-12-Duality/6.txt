Slide 6

\textbf{Neg. Transpose Property is Preserved in each Simplex Step}

[V.J] p.64

\begin{itemize}
    \item[(P)]
    \begin{align*}
        \zeta &= +4x_1 + 1x_2 + 3x_3 \\
        w_1 &= 1 - x_1 - 4x_2 \\
        w_2 &= 3 - 3x_1 + x_2 - x_3
    \end{align*}
    \begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|}
    \hline
    $\zeta$ & $x_1$ & $x_2$ & $x_3$ &  \\ \hline
    $w_1$ & $-1$ & $-4$ & $0$ &  \\ \hline
    $w_2$ & $-3$ & $1$ & $-1$ &  \\ \hline
    \end{tabular}
    $x_3$ enters, $w_2$ leaves (greatest increase rule)
    \item[(D)]
    \begin{align*}
        -\zeta &= -1 - 1y_1 - 3y_2 \\
        z_1 &= -4 + y_1 + 3y_2 \\
        z_2 &= -1 + 4y_1 - y_2 \\
        z_3 &= -3
    \end{align*}
    \begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|}
    \hline
    $-\zeta$ & $y_1$ & $y_2$ &  \\ \hline
    $z_1$ & $1$ & $3$ & $-4$ \\ \hline
    $z_2$ & $4$ & $-1$ & $-1$ \\ \hline
    $z_3$ & $0$ & $0$ & $-3$ \\ \hline
    \end{tabular}
    $z_3$ leaves, $y_2$ enters
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Description:}

This slide demonstrates the application of the negative transpose property in a simplex method step.  It shows a linear programming problem in (P) and its dual (D) in tabular form.  The tables represent the coefficients of the variables in the objective function and constraints.  The slide illustrates how the coefficients in the dual problem are obtained by taking the negative transpose of the primal problem's coefficient matrix.  The step shown is a simplex iteration, where $x_3$ enters the basis in the primal problem and $w_2$ leaves, and in the dual problem, $y_2$ enters the basis and $z_3$ leaves.  The "greatest increase rule" is mentioned in the context of the primal problem, suggesting a specific rule for selecting the entering variable.  The slide emphasizes that the negative transpose property is maintained throughout the simplex iterations.

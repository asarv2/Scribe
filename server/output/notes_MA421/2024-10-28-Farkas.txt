\textbf{Farkas Lemma}

\textit{LEMMA 10.5.} The system $Ax \le b$ has no solutions if and only if there is a $y$ such that

\begin{align*}
A^T y &= 0 \\
y &\ge 0 \\
b^T y &< 0.
\end{align*}

(10.8)

\textbf{PROOF.} Consider the linear program

\begin{align*}
\label{eq:1}
(P) \quad \text{maximize} \quad 0 \\
\text{subject to} \quad Ax \le b
\end{align*}
and its dual

\begin{align*}
(D) \quad \text{minimize} \quad b^T y \\
\text{subject to} \quad A^T y = 0 \\
y \ge 0.
\end{align*}

\end{document}

\textbf{Description:}

This slide introduces Farkas Lemma (Lemma 10.5), a fundamental result in linear programming.  It states a condition for the system $Ax \le b$ to have no solutions.  The condition involves the existence of a vector $y$ satisfying certain constraints.  The slide then outlines a proof by considering the primal linear program (P) and its dual (D).  The primal (P) is to maximize 0 subject to $Ax \le b$, and the dual (D) is to minimize $b^T y$ subject to $A^T y = 0$ and $y \ge 0$. \textbf{Farkas Lemma (Other Versions)}

6.4.1 Proposition (Farkas lemma). Let $A$ be a real matrix with $m$ rows
and $n$ columns, and let $b \in \mathbb{R}^m$ be a vector. Then exactly one of the following
two possibilities occurs:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item[(F1)] There exists a vector $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$ satisfying $Ax = b$ and $x \ge 0$.
    \item[(F2)] There exists a vector $y \in \mathbb{R}^m$ such that $y^T A \ge 0^T$ and $y^T b < 0$.
\end{enumerate}

\end{document}

\textbf{Description:}

This slide presents an alternative formulation of Farkas' Lemma (Proposition 6.4.1).  It's a different, but equivalent, way to express the condition for the system $Ax = b$ to have a non-negative solution ($x \ge 0$).  Instead of the previous formulation involving $Ax \le b$, this version focuses on the case where $Ax = b$.  Crucially, it introduces two mutually exclusive possibilities (F1 and F2).  (F1) states that if a solution $x$ exists satisfying $Ax = b$ and $x \ge 0$, then (F2) cannot hold. Conversely, if (F1) does not hold, then (F2) must hold.  This is a key result in linear programming, often used to determine the feasibility of certain systems of equations. The notation $y^T A$ and $y^T b$ represents the transpose of $y$ multiplied by $A$ and $b$ respectively.  The slide number (2 of 11) indicates this is part of a larger discussion on Farkas' Lemma.

6.4.3 Proposition (Farkas lemma in three variants). Let $A$ be a real
matrix with $m$ rows and $n$ columns, and let $b \in \mathbb{R}^m$ be a vector.

(i) The system $Ax = b$ has a nonnegative solution $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$

(iii) The system $Ax \le b$ has a solution $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$ if and only if every $y \in \mathbb{R}^m$ with $y^T A = 0^T$ also satisfies $y^T b \ge 0$.

Slide 1

\textbf{Duality - Interpretations}

\begin{align*}
\text{max } \sum_{i=1}^n c_i x_i &= c^T x \\
\text{s.t. } \quad &AX \le b \\
&x \ge 0
\end{align*}

\begin{align*}
\text{min } \sum_{j=1}^m b_j y_j &= b^T y \\
\text{s.t. } \quad &A^T y \ge c \\
&y \ge 0
\end{align*}

\textbf{Description:}

This slide introduces the concept of duality in linear programming.  It shows the primal problem (maximization) and its corresponding dual problem (minimization).  The primal problem is to maximize a linear objective function subject to linear constraints (AX ≤ b and x ≥ 0). The dual problem is to minimize a different linear objective function subject to different constraints (A<sup>T</sup>y ≥ c and y ≥ 0).  The slide highlights the relationship between the two problems using arrows to visually connect them.  The label "Dual" is circled, indicating that the bottom problem is the dual of the top problem.  This is a foundational slide for understanding the duality theorem in linear programming.  The variables $x$ and $y$ represent vectors of decision variables, $c$ is a vector of coefficients for the objective function in the primal problem, and $b$ is a vector of coefficients for the objective function in the dual problem.  $A$ is the constraint matrix.  The notation $c^T x$ and $b^T y$ represent the dot products of the vectors.  The inequality signs (≤ and ≥) are crucial to understanding the relationship between the primal and dual problems.

\textbf{Thm 10.1} \quad $S$ is convex $\iff$ it contains all conv. comb. of points in $S$ \\
$pf$ "$\implies$" Suppose $S$ is convex. \\
$n=2$: $z_1, z_2 \in S \implies t_1 z_1 + t_2 z_2 \in S$, $t_1, t_2 \ge 0$, $t_1 + t_2 = 1$ \\
$n=3$: $z_1, z_2, z_3 \in S \implies t_1 z_1 + t_2 z_2 + t_3 z_3 = (t_1 + t_2) \left( \frac{t_1}{t_1 + t_2} z_1 + \frac{t_2}{t_1 + t_2} z_2 \right) + t_3 z_3 \in S$ \\
$t_1 + t_2 + t_3 = 1$, $t_1, t_2, t_3 \ge 0$
%
\subsection{Description}%
This slide continues the proof of Theorem 10.1, showing that if a set $S$ is convex, then it contains all convex combinations of its points.  The proof proceeds by induction.  The base case ($n=2$) is shown: if $z_1$ and $z_2$ are in $S$, then any convex combination $t_1 z_1 + t_2 z_2$ (where $t_1, t_2 \ge 0$ and $t_1 + t_2 = 1$) is also in $S$ by the definition of convexity.  The inductive step ($n=3$) is then demonstrated.  It shows that if $z_1, z_2, z_3$ are in $S$, then a convex combination $t_1 z_1 + t_2 z_2 + t_3 z_3$ can be rewritten as a convex combination of a convex combination of $z_1$ and $z_2$ and $z_3$.  Since the inner convex combination is in $S$ (by the base case), and the outer convex combination is also in $S$ (by the definition of convexity), the entire expression is in $S$.  The notation $t_1, t_2, t_3 \ge 0$ and $t_1 + t_2 + t_3 = 1$ indicates the non-negative weights and their sum to 1, which are crucial for convex combinations. The context of linear programming is relevant because convex sets are fundamental to optimization problems in linear programming.%

QUESTION 1
Consider the primal problem (P) in Slide 2.  The dual simplex method is employed because (P) is not primal feasible. What specific characteristic of the primal problem necessitates the use of the dual simplex method, and why is the dual problem (D) initially feasible?
A. The objective function of (P) has all positive coefficients.
B. The right-hand side of at least one constraint in (P) is negative.
C. The dual problem (D) has an unbounded objective function.
D. The constraints in (P) are inconsistent.
E. The objective function of (P) is unbounded.

ANSWER: B

EXPLANATION:
A. A positive objective function coefficient is not a condition for using the dual simplex method. The dual simplex method is used when the primal problem is infeasible.
B. The dual simplex method is used when the primal problem is infeasible, specifically when at least one constraint has a negative right-hand side.  This is because the standard simplex method requires an initial feasible solution, which is not available in this case. The dual problem is initially feasible because the origin (all dual variables equal to zero) satisfies the dual constraints.  The negative right-hand sides in the primal constraints translate to positive constants in the dual constraints, ensuring feasibility at the origin.
C. The dual problem's unboundedness is a consequence, not a cause, of primal infeasibility. The dual simplex method addresses primal infeasibility.
D. Inconsistent constraints in the primal problem would make both the primal and dual problems infeasible. The dual simplex method is used when the primal is infeasible but the dual is feasible.
E. An unbounded primal objective function would imply an infeasible dual problem, not a situation where the dual simplex method is applicable.

B. The dual dictionary is obtained by transposing the coefficient matrix of the primal dictionary and changing the signs of the constants; the origin is dual-feasible because the constants in the dual constraints are non-negative.
C. The dual dictionary is obtained by inverting the coefficient matrix of the primal dictionary; the origin is dual-feasible because the objective function is minimized.
D. The dual dictionary is identical to the primal dictionary; the origin is dual-feasible because the dual problem is always feasible.
E. The dual dictionary is obtained by taking the transpose of the primal dictionary and changing the signs of the objective function coefficients; the origin is dual-feasible because the dual problem is always feasible.

ANSWER: B

EXPLANATION:
A. Negating the primal dictionary does not produce the dual dictionary. The dual is obtained by a transpose and sign changes.
B. The dual dictionary is derived from the primal dictionary by taking the negative transpose of the coefficient matrix of the constraints and changing the signs of the constants. The origin is dual-feasible because the constants in the dual constraints (the right-hand sides of the $z_i$ equations) are non-negative. The origin is not primal-feasible because the constants in the primal constraints (the right-hand sides of the $w_i$ equations) are not all non-negative.
C. Inverting the coefficient matrix does not yield the dual dictionary. The dual is obtained by a transpose and sign changes.
D. The primal and dual dictionaries are not identical. The dual is obtained by a transpose and sign changes.
E. While the transpose is correct, changing the signs of the objective function coefficients is not part of the transformation. The constants in the constraints are what determine feasibility.

Consider the primal problem (P) in Slide 2. The dual simplex method is employed because (P) is not primal feasible. What specific characteristic of the primal problem necessitates the use of the dual simplex method, and why is the dual problem (D) initially feasible?
A. The primal problem (P) has negative entries in the right-hand side vector $b$, making it infeasible at the origin. The dual problem (D) is initially feasible because the origin satisfies its constraints.
B. The primal problem (P) has an unbounded objective function. The dual problem (D) is initially feasible because it is a minimization problem.
C. The primal problem (P) has redundant constraints. The dual problem (D) is initially feasible because it has fewer constraints.
D. The primal problem (P) has an inconsistent constraint set. The dual problem (D) is initially feasible because it is always feasible.
E. The primal problem (P) has a non-linear objective function. The dual problem (D) is initially feasible because it is a linear problem.

ANSWER: A

EXPLANATION:
A. The correct answer is A. The dual simplex method is designed for situations where the primal problem is infeasible but the dual is feasible.  The presence of negative entries in the right-hand side vector $b$ of the primal problem indicates that the origin is not a feasible solution.  The dual problem, however, is constructed in such a way that the origin often satisfies its constraints, making it initially feasible. This allows the dual simplex method to start its iterations from a feasible point in the dual space and iteratively move towards a feasible and optimal solution for the primal problem.
B. Incorrect. An unbounded primal problem implies an infeasible dual problem, not the other way around. The dual simplex method is not directly related to the unboundedness of the objective function.
C. Incorrect. Redundant constraints in the primal problem do not necessarily imply that the dual simplex method is needed.  The dual simplex method addresses primal infeasibility, not redundancy.
D. Incorrect. An inconsistent constraint set in the primal problem would make both the primal and dual problems infeasible. The dual simplex method is used when the primal is infeasible but the dual is feasible.
E. Incorrect. The dual simplex method applies to linear programs.  Non-linearity would require different solution techniques.

QUESTION 10
Consider the primal problem (P) in Slide 2. The dual simplex method is employed because (P) is not primal feasible.  What specific characteristic of the primal problem necessitates the use of the dual simplex method, and why is the dual problem (D) initially feasible?
A. The primal problem (P) has negative entries in the right-hand side vector $b$, making it infeasible at the origin. The dual problem (D) is initially feasible because the origin satisfies its constraints.
B. The primal problem (P) has an unbounded objective function. The dual problem (D) is initially feasible because it is a minimization problem.
C. The primal problem (P) has redundant constraints. The dual problem (D) is initially feasible because it has fewer constraints.
D. The primal problem (P) has an inconsistent set of constraints. The dual problem (D) is initially feasible because it is always feasible.
E. The primal problem (P) has a non-linear objective function. The dual problem (D) is initially feasible because it is a linear problem.

ANSWER: A

EXPLANATION:
A. The correct answer is A. The presence of negative entries in the right-hand side vector $b$ of the primal problem's constraints indicates that the origin (all variables equal to zero) is not a feasible solution.  The dual problem, however, is constructed in such a way that the origin is feasible.  This is because the dual constraints are inequalities of the form $A^T y \ge c$, and setting $y = 0$ satisfies these constraints as long as $c$ has non-positive entries (which is often the case when the primal is a maximization problem). The dual simplex method leverages this initial dual feasibility to iteratively move towards primal feasibility and optimality.
B. The primal problem being unbounded does not directly necessitate the dual simplex method.  The dual simplex method is used when the primal is infeasible at the origin.  The dual problem being a minimization problem is not sufficient to guarantee initial feasibility.
C. Redundant constraints in the primal problem do not automatically imply the need for the dual simplex method.  The dual simplex method is specifically designed for cases where the primal is infeasible at the origin.
D. An inconsistent set of constraints in the primal problem would make both the primal and dual problems infeasible. The dual simplex method is used when the primal is infeasible at the origin but the dual is feasible.
E. The dual simplex method applies to linear programs.  A non-linear objective function would require different solution techniques.

QUESTION 1
Consider the general linear program (GLP) presented in Slide 2: Maximize $c^T x$ subject to $Ax \le b$ and $x \le u$.  The Lagrangian is formed, incorporating Lagrange multipliers $y$ and $z$. If, during the process of finding the dual, we determine that $c^T - y^T A - z^T \ne 0$, what can we definitively conclude about the dual objective function $\xi(y, z)$, and why?
A. The dual objective function $\xi(y, z)$ is unbounded below.
B. The dual objective function $\xi(y, z)$ is unbounded above.
C. The dual objective function $\xi(y, z)$ is bounded and has a finite minimum value.
D. The dual objective function $\xi(y, z)$ is bounded and has a finite maximum value.
E. We cannot conclude anything definitive about the dual objective function without further information.

ANSWER: A

EXPLANATION:
A. The condition $c^T - y^T A - z^T \ne 0$ implies that the dual objective function is unbounded below.  In Slide 6, the Lagrangian is minimized with respect to $x$. If $c^T - y^T A - z^T \ne 0$, the term $(c^T - y^T A - z^T)x$ can be made arbitrarily small (approaching $-\infty$) by choosing an appropriate $x$, resulting in an unbounded dual objective function.
B. The dual problem is a minimization problem.  An unbounded dual objective function would be unbounded below, not above.
C. If the condition $c^T - y^T A - z^T \ne 0$ holds, the dual objective function is unbounded, so it cannot have a finite minimum value.
D. The dual problem is a minimization problem, so it cannot have a finite maximum value.
E. The condition $c^T - y^T A - z^T \ne 0$ directly impacts the unboundedness of the dual objective function, as explained in Slide 6.


QUESTION 2
Referring to Slide 1, we have the primal linear program (P): Maximize $\zeta = c^T x$ subject to $Ax \le b$ and $x \ge 0$. The Lagrangian is given by $L(x, y) = c^T x - y^T(Ax - b)$, where $y$ is the Lagrange multiplier. Explain the significance of the non-negativity constraint $y \ge 0$ in the context of the Lagrangian and the derivation of the dual problem.
A. The constraint $y \ge 0$ ensures the Lagrangian is convex.
B. The constraint $y \ge 0$ is arbitrary and does not affect the dual problem.
C. The constraint $y \ge 0$ ensures the dual problem is a minimization problem.
D. The constraint $y \ge 0$ arises from the inequality constraints $Ax \le b$ in the primal problem and is crucial for ensuring weak duality.
E. The constraint $y \ge 0$ is necessary for the Lagrangian to be differentiable.

ANSWER: D

EXPLANATION:
A. The Lagrangian's convexity is not directly determined by the non-negativity of $y$.
B. The constraint $y \ge 0$ is not arbitrary; it's fundamental to the derivation and interpretation of the dual problem.
C. The dual problem being a minimization problem is a consequence of the primal problem being a maximization problem, not directly due to $y \ge 0$.
D. The non-negativity constraint $y \ge 0$ is directly linked to the inequality constraints $Ax \le b$ in the primal problem.  When deriving the dual, we consider the inequality $y^T(Ax - b) \le 0$, which holds only if $y \ge 0$. This constraint is essential for establishing weak duality, ensuring that the objective function value of any feasible solution to the primal problem is less than or equal to the objective function value of any feasible solution to the dual problem.
E. The Lagrangian's differentiability is not directly related to the non-negativity of $y$.


QUESTION 3
Slide 3 shows the derivation of the dual problem from the Lagrangian.  The inequality $(y^T A + z^T)x^+ + (-y^T A - z^T)x^- \le y^T b + z^T u$ is derived.  Explain the significance of the subsequent inequalities $\ge c^T$ and $\ge -c^T$ added under this inequality.
A. These inequalities are arbitrary and do not affect the final dual problem.
B. These inequalities ensure the dual problem is feasible.
C. These inequalities are necessary conditions for strong duality to hold.
D. These inequalities represent lower bounds on the expression, ensuring that the dual objective function is bounded below.
E. These inequalities are derived from the objective function of the primal problem and are crucial for deriving the constraints of the dual problem.

ANSWER: E

EXPLANATION:
A. These inequalities are not arbitrary; they are essential for the derivation.
B. Feasibility of the dual problem is determined by the constraints, not these inequalities directly.
C. Strong duality is a consequence of the relationship between the primal and dual optimal values, not a direct result of these inequalities.
D. These inequalities do not directly ensure the dual objective function is bounded below; that is ensured by the constraints of the dual problem.
E. The inequalities $\ge c^T$ and $\ge -c^T$ are not arbitrary additions. They are derived from the primal objective function, $c^T x = c^T (x^+ - x^-)$.  To ensure that the inequality derived from the Lagrangian remains valid for all feasible $x^+$, $x^-$, we must have the left-hand side bounded below by $c^T$ (when $x^+ > 0$ and $x^- = 0$) and above by $-c^T$ (when $x^+ = 0$ and $x^- > 0$). This is crucial for deriving the constraints $A^T y + z \ge c$ in the dual problem.

It indicates the marginal increase in the objective function (total profit) if the production capacity limit for product $i$ were increased by one unit.

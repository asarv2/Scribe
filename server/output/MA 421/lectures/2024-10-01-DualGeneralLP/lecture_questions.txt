QUESTION 1
Given the primal problem (P) $\max \quad \zeta = c^T x$ subject to $Ax \le b$ and $x \ge 0$, and its dual problem (D) $\min \quad \xi = b^T y$ subject to $A^T y \ge c$ and $y \ge 0$, as shown in Slide 1, what is the expression for the Lagrangian function $L(x, y)$ used in the derivation of the dual problem?
A. $L(x, y) = c^T x + y^T (Ax - b)$
B. $L(x, y) = c^T x - y^T (Ax - b)$
C. $L(x, y) = c^T x + y^T (b - Ax)$
D. $L(x, y) = b^T y - x^T (A^T y - c)$
E. $L(x, y) = b^T y + x^T (A^T y - c)$

ANSWER: B

EXPLANATION:
A. This is incorrect. The signs are reversed in the constraint term. The Lagrangian should be minimized when the constraints are satisfied.
B. The correct expression for the Lagrangian function is $L(x, y) = c^T x - y^T (Ax - b)$.  The Lagrangian incorporates the objective function and the constraints, with $y$ as Lagrange multipliers for the inequality constraints $Ax \le b$. The negative sign ensures that the Lagrangian is minimized when the constraints are satisfied.
C. This is incorrect. The signs are reversed in the constraint term. The Lagrangian should be minimized when the constraints are satisfied.
D. This is incorrect. This expression represents a Lagrangian related to the dual problem, not the primal problem.
E. This is incorrect. This expression represents a Lagrangian related to the dual problem, not the primal problem, and the signs are reversed.


QUESTION 2
Explain the significance of introducing the variables $x^+$ and $x^-$ in Slide 2 when transforming the general linear program (MI).
A. To ensure the objective function remains linear.
B. To handle unbounded variables and guarantee the existence of a solution.
C. To simplify the constraint matrix A and make the problem easier to solve.
D. To convert inequality constraints into equality constraints.
E. To allow the use of the simplex method directly without further transformations.

ANSWER: B

EXPLANATION:
A. While the transformation maintains linearity of the objective function, it's not the primary significance. The main purpose is to handle variables without inherent non-negativity constraints.
B. The introduction of $x^+ = \max(x, 0)$ and $x^- = \max(-x, 0)$ allows us to represent any real-valued variable $x$ as the difference of two non-negative variables ($x = x^+ - x^-$). This is crucial because the standard form of a linear program requires non-negativity constraints.  Without this transformation, the duality theory presented in the subsequent slides would not directly apply to the general linear program (MI) which does not have the non-negativity constraint on $x$.
C. The transformation doesn't directly simplify the constraint matrix A. The focus is on the variables, not the matrix itself.
D. The transformation doesn't directly convert inequality constraints to equality constraints. That happens later in the derivation of the dual.
E. The transformation is a necessary step before applying duality theory and the simplex method, not a direct application of the simplex method itself.

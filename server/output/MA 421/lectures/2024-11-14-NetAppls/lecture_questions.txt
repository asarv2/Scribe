QUESTION 1
In the transportation problem with inequality constraints (Slides 1-2), if the total supply $\sum_i s_i$ exceeds the total demand $\sum_j d_j$, what modification is necessary to transform the problem into a standard transportation problem solvable with equality constraints, and why is this modification needed?
A. Introduce artificial variables to the supply constraints.
B. Introduce a dummy destination node with a demand equal to the difference between total supply and total demand.
C. Introduce a dummy source node with a supply equal to the difference between total demand and total supply.
D. Relax the equality constraints to inequalities.
E. Adjust the supply and demand values proportionally to achieve balance.

ANSWER: B

EXPLANATION:
A. Introducing artificial variables to the supply constraints would not address the imbalance between supply and demand.  Artificial variables are typically used to find an initial feasible solution in the simplex method, not to handle unbalanced supply and demand in a transportation problem.
B. A dummy destination node is introduced to absorb the excess supply.  This transforms the inequality constraints ($\sum_j x_{ij} \le s_i$ and $\sum_i x_{ij} \ge d_j$) into equality constraints, allowing the application of standard transportation algorithms which require balanced supply and demand.  Without this modification, the standard algorithms would not function correctly.
C. Introducing a dummy source node would not correctly address the imbalance.  The excess supply needs to be absorbed, not created.
D. Relaxing the equality constraints to inequalities would not solve the problem; it would simply change the nature of the problem, not make it solvable by standard algorithms.
E. Proportionally adjusting supply and demand would alter the original problem, potentially leading to an inaccurate solution.

D. Ignore the inequality constraints and solve the problem as if it were a standard transportation problem with equality constraints.
E. Introduce a dummy demand node with a demand equal to $\sum_i s_i - \sum_j d_j$ and zero-cost arcs connecting it to all supply nodes.

ANSWER: A

EXPLANATION:

The zero-cost arcs ensure that the additional supply does not affect the optimal solution's cost. This approach transforms the inequality-constrained problem into an equality-constrained problem solvable by standard algorithms.
B. Answer B is incorrect. Proportionally increasing the supply might lead to an unrealistic or infeasible solution, as it alters the original supply constraints.
C. Answer C is incorrect. Proportionally decreasing the demand might lead to an unrealistic or infeasible solution, as it alters the original demand constraints.
D. Answer D is incorrect. Ignoring the inequality constraints will likely lead to an infeasible solution, as the supply will not be sufficient to meet the demand.

QUESTION 10
In the transportation problem with inequality constraints (Slides 1-2), if the total supply $\sum_i s_i$ is strictly less than the total demand $\sum_j d_j$, what modification is necessary to transform the problem into a standard transportation problem solvable with equality constraints, and why is this modification needed?
A. Introduce a dummy source node with a supply equal to $\sum_j d_j - \sum_i s_i$, and connect this dummy node to all demand nodes with zero-cost arcs.
B. Introduce a dummy demand node with a demand equal to $\sum_i s_i - \sum_j d_j$, and connect all supply nodes to this dummy node with zero-cost arcs.
C. Increase the supply at each source node proportionally to make the total supply equal to the total demand.
D. Decrease the demand at each demand node proportionally to make the total demand equal to the total supply.
E. The problem is already solvable as is; no modification is needed.

ANSWER: A

EXPLANATION:
A. Answer A is correct.  Since the total supply is less than the total demand, there will be unmet demand.  Introducing a dummy source node with a supply equal to the difference between total demand and total supply allows us to model the unmet demand.  Connecting this dummy node to all demand nodes with zero-cost arcs represents the fact that this unmet demand incurs no cost. This transforms the problem into a balanced transportation problem with equality constraints, which can be solved using standard algorithms.
B. Answer B is incorrect.  Introducing a dummy demand node with a demand equal to the difference between total supply and total demand would not solve the problem.  The problem is that there is not enough supply to meet the demand.  A dummy demand node would not address this issue.
C. Answer C is incorrect.  Proportionally increasing the supply at each source node would change the original problem statement and is not a valid approach to solving the inequality constraint problem.
D. Answer D is incorrect.  Proportionally decreasing the demand at each demand node would change the original problem statement and is not a valid approach to solving the inequality constraint problem.
E. Answer E is incorrect.  Standard transportation problem algorithms require equality constraints (supply equals demand).  The inequality constraints make the problem unsolvable using standard methods without modification.

QUESTION 1
The Klee-Minty cube demonstrates the simplex method's worst-case performance.  Considering the largest-coefficient rule, what characteristic of the Klee-Minty problem leads to an exponential number of iterations, and how does this relate to the structure of the feasible region?
A. The highly symmetric structure of the feasible region causes the algorithm to cycle.
B. The objective function's coefficients are all equal, leading to arbitrary pivoting choices.
C. The skewed geometry of the feasible region, combined with the largest-coefficient rule, forces the algorithm to visit almost every vertex.
D. The constraints are highly redundant, causing the algorithm to explore unnecessary solutions.
E. The problem is inherently ill-conditioned, leading to numerical instability and increased iterations.

ANSWER: C

EXPLANATION:
A. While cycling can occur in the simplex method, it's not the primary reason for the exponential behavior in the Klee-Minty problem. The problem's structure is the key factor.
B. The objective function coefficients in the Klee-Minty problem are carefully chosen powers of 2, not all equal. This specific weighting is crucial to the problem's exponential behavior.
C. The Klee-Minty problem's skewed geometry, coupled with the largest-coefficient rule's myopic focus on the largest coefficient in the objective function, forces the simplex method to traverse a path visiting nearly all vertices of the feasible region. This path's length grows exponentially with the number of variables, resulting in exponential time complexity.
D. The constraints in the Klee-Minty problem are not redundant; they define a highly specific, skewed feasible region. Redundancy would not necessarily lead to exponential complexity.
E. The Klee-Minty problem is not inherently ill-conditioned; its exponential complexity arises from its geometric structure and the choice of pivot rule, not numerical instability.

QUESTION 1
In the context of the Max Flow Min Cut theorem, what is the significance of defining a cut (C) in the network graph, and how does the capacity of this cut relate to the maximum flow?
A. A cut is an arbitrary partition of the nodes; its capacity is irrelevant to the maximum flow.
B. A cut separates the source and sink nodes; its capacity provides an upper bound on the maximum flow.
C. A cut is a path from source to sink; its capacity is equal to the maximum flow.
D. A cut is a cycle in the graph; its capacity has no relation to the maximum flow.
E. A cut is a subset of nodes containing the source; its capacity is always less than the maximum flow.

ANSWER: B

EXPLANATION:
A. A cut is not arbitrary; it must separate the source and sink. Its capacity is crucial for determining the maximum flow.
B. A cut in the Max Flow Min Cut theorem is a partition of the nodes that separates the source and sink nodes. The capacity of this cut represents the sum of capacities of edges going from the source side to the sink side.  The Max Flow Min Cut theorem states that the maximum flow in the network is equal to the minimum capacity among all possible cuts. Therefore, the capacity of any cut provides an upper bound on the maximum flow.
C. A cut is not a path; it's a partition of the nodes.  While the maximum flow uses paths, the cut capacity provides a bound, not an equality.
D. A cut is not a cycle; it's a partition of the nodes. Cycles are irrelevant to the Max Flow Min Cut theorem.
E. A cut doesn't need to contain the source; it only needs to separate the source and sink. The capacity of a cut can be equal to the maximum flow (the minimum cut).


QUESTION 2
The Ford-Fulkerson algorithm iteratively increases the flow in a network.  Explain the concept of an "augmenting path" and its role in this iterative process.
A. An augmenting path is a cycle in the network; it's used to reduce the flow.
B. An augmenting path is a path from the source to the sink with available capacity; it's used to increase the flow.
C. An augmenting path is a path from the sink to the source; it's used to decrease the flow.
D. An augmenting path is any path in the network; it's used to randomly adjust the flow.
E. An augmenting path is a cut in the network; it's used to determine the maximum flow.

ANSWER: B

EXPLANATION:
A. Augmenting paths are not cycles; they are paths from source to sink.  Cycles are not used to increase flow.
B. An augmenting path is a path from the source node to the sink node in a network flow graph that has available capacity along each edge.  The Ford-Fulkerson algorithm finds such paths and increases the flow along them until no more augmenting paths exist, at which point the maximum flow is achieved.
C. Augmenting paths go from source to sink, not sink to source.  The direction is crucial for increasing flow.
D. Augmenting paths are not arbitrary; they must have available capacity along each edge to increase the flow systematically.
E. An augmenting path is not a cut; a cut is a partition of the nodes. Cuts are used to determine a bound on the maximum flow, not to increase it iteratively.


QUESTION 3
In the linear programming formulation of the maximum flow problem, why is it necessary to introduce a fictitious arc connecting the sink to the source?
A. The fictitious arc is needed to ensure the problem is feasible.
B. The fictitious arc is needed to make the problem unbounded.
C. The fictitious arc is needed to represent the total flow from source to sink in the objective function.
D. The fictitious arc is needed to simplify the constraint matrix.
E. The fictitious arc is not necessary; the problem can be formulated without it.

ANSWER: C

EXPLANATION:
A. The feasibility of the problem is determined by the capacities and the network structure, not the fictitious arc.
B. The fictitious arc does not make the problem unbounded; the capacity constraints prevent unboundedness.
C. The fictitious arc, with a cost of -1 and infinite capacity, allows the total flow from source to sink to be directly represented in the objective function.  Minimizing the negative of the flow on this arc is equivalent to maximizing the total flow in the network.
D. While it simplifies the formulation slightly, the primary reason is to represent the total flow in the objective function.
E. The problem can be formulated without it, but it makes the formulation less direct and elegant. The fictitious arc simplifies the objective function.


QUESTION 4

A. The maximum flow is always greater than the minimum cut capacity.
B. The maximum flow is always less than the minimum cut capacity.
C. The maximum flow is always equal to the minimum cut capacity.
D. There is no relationship between the maximum flow and the minimum cut capacity.
E. The relationship between maximum flow and minimum cut capacity is problem-specific and cannot be generalized.

ANSWER: C

EXPLANATION:
A. The maximum flow cannot be greater than the minimum cut capacity; the minimum cut capacity provides an upper bound on the maximum flow.
B. The maximum flow cannot be less than the minimum cut capacity; it is always less than or equal to the capacity of any cut.
C. The Max-Flow Min-Cut theorem states that the maximum flow that can be sent from the source to the sink in a network is equal to the minimum capacity among all cuts separating the source and the sink. This fundamental theorem provides a powerful tool for solving network flow problems.
D. The Max-Flow Min-Cut theorem establishes a direct and fundamental relationship between the maximum flow and the minimum cut capacity.
E. The relationship is not problem-specific; it's a general theorem applicable to all network flow problems.

<LATEX>
\underline{\text{Simplex in Matrix Notation}} \\
\begin{array}{|c|}
\hline
\text{max } f(x) = c^T x \\
\text{s.t. } Ax + w = b \\
x, w \ge 0 \\
\hline
\end{array}
\quad \begin{array}{c} x \in \mathbb{R}^n \\ c \in \mathbb{R}^n \\ A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n} \\ b \in \mathbb{R}^m \\ w \in \mathbb{R}^m \end{array}
\begin{array}{c} m \times (n+m) \\ \begin{bmatrix} A & I \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ w \end{bmatrix} = b \end{array} \\
\xrightarrow{\text{Simplex iterations}} \\
\begin{array}{|c|}
\hline
\text{max } f(x) = \overline{j} + \overline{c}^T x \\
\text{s.t. } \overline{A}x + \overline{w} = \overline{b} \\
x, \overline{w} \ge 0 \\
\hline
\end{array}
\quad \begin{array}{c} m \times (n+m) \\ \begin{bmatrix} \overline{A} & I \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ \overline{w} \end{bmatrix} = \overline{b} \end{array}
</LATEX>

<DESCRIPTION>This slide illustrates the simplex method's iterative process using matrix notation within the context of linear programming. The title "Simplex in Matrix Notation" is underlined.  The slide presents two equivalent formulations of a linear program. The first is a standard maximization problem: maximize $f(x) = c^Tx$ subject to $Ax + w = b$ and $x, w \ge 0$, where $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$ are the decision variables, $c \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is the cost vector, $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ is the constraint matrix, $b \in \mathbb{R}^m$ is the right-hand side vector, and $w \in \mathbb{R}^m$ are the slack variables. This is then represented in matrix form as $\begin{bmatrix} A & I \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ w \end{bmatrix} = b$. The second formulation represents a later iteration of the simplex method, where the objective function and constraints have been modified. The objective function is now $\overline{j} + \overline{c}^T x$, and the constraints are $\overline{A}x + \overline{w} = \overline{b}$, with the same non-negativity constraints. This is also represented in matrix form as $\begin{bmatrix} \overline{A} & I \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ \overline{w} \end{bmatrix} = \overline{b}$. The arrow indicates the iterative nature of the simplex algorithm, transforming the initial problem into a modified one during each iteration. The dimensions of the matrices are explicitly stated. The slide emphasizes the use of matrix notation to represent and solve linear programs efficiently.</DESCRIPTION>

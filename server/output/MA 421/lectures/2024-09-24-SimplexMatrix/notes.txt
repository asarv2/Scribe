\textbf{Simplex in Matrix Notation} \\
$\begin{pmatrix}
\max & f(x) = c^T x \\
\text{s.t.} & Ax \le b \\
& x \ge 0
\end{pmatrix}$
\qquad $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$, $c \in \mathbb{R}^n$ \\
\qquad $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$, $b \in \mathbb{R}^m$ \\
$\begin{pmatrix}
\max & f(x) = c^T x \\
\text{s.t.} & W = b - Ax \\
& x, W \ge 0
\end{pmatrix}$
\qquad $W \in \mathbb{R}^m$
</LATEX>

<DESCRIPTION>This slide introduces two equivalent formulations of a linear program in matrix notation, relevant to a Linear Programming course. The top formulation is a standard linear program: maximize $f(x) = c^T x$ subject to $Ax \le b$ and $x \ge 0$.  The variables are defined: $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is the decision variable vector, $c \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is the cost vector, $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ is the constraint matrix, and $b \in \mathbb{R}^m$ is the right-hand side vector. The bottom formulation introduces slack variables $W \in \mathbb{R}^m$, transforming the inequality constraints into equality constraints: $W = b - Ax$, with the additional non-negativity constraint $W \ge 0$.  Both formulations aim to solve the same optimization problem, but the second one is often preferred for algorithmic purposes (like the simplex method) because it deals with equality constraints. The curved arrow indicates the equivalence between the two formulations. The context of the slide is clearly within the topic of linear programming, specifically showing how to represent a linear program using matrix notation, a crucial step for applying algorithmic solutions.</DESCRIPTION>

\textbf{Simplex Iterations in Matrix Form} \\
(1) $BX_B + NX_N = b \implies X_B = B^{-1}b - B^{-1}NX_N$ \\
(2) $J = C_B^T X_B + C_N^T X_N$ \\
$= C_B^T (B^{-1}b - B^{-1}NX_N) + C_N^T X_N$ \\
$= C_B^T B^{-1}b - (C_B^T B^{-1}N - C_N^T)X_N$ \\
$= C_B^T B^{-1}b - ((B^{-1}N)^T C_B - C_N)^T X_N$ \\
$= \bar{J} + \bar{C}^T X_N$
</LATEX>

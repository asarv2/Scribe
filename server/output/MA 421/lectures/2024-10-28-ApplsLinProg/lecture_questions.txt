C. It represents the number of temporary employees needed in month $i$.
D. It represents the number of hours of labor handled by temporary employees in month $i$.
E. It is a redundant variable and does not affect the linearization.

ANSWER: D

EXPLANATION:
A. The number of regular employees is represented by the variable $x$.
B. The number of hours of labor handled by regular employees is represented by the variable $x$.

D. The auxiliary variable $y_i$ represents the number of hours of labor handled by temporary employees in month $i$.  By introducing this variable, the $\max(a_i - x, 0)$ term, which is non-linear, is replaced by a linear constraint $a_i - x \le y_i$, effectively linearizing the problem.
E. The variable $y_i$ is crucial for linearizing the objective function, making it solvable using linear programming techniques.


QUESTION 4
In the Markowitz portfolio selection model (Slide 12), how is the non-linear variance term handled to obtain a linear programming formulation, and what is the significance of the newly introduced variable?
A. The variance term is ignored to simplify the problem.
B. The variance is approximated using a linear function of the returns.
C. A new variable is introduced to represent the absolute deviation from the expected return, linearizing the variance term.
D. The problem is reformulated as a quadratic program, not a linear program.
E. The variance term is replaced by the standard deviation.

ANSWER: C

EXPLANATION:
A. Ignoring the variance term would significantly simplify the problem but would not accurately reflect the risk associated with the portfolio.
B. While approximations are sometimes used, this specific formulation uses absolute deviations to linearize the problem.
C. The non-linear variance term is linearized by introducing a new variable, $y_t$, which represents the absolute deviation from the expected return at time $t$. This allows the quadratic programming problem to be approximated as a linear program.
D. The slide explicitly shows a linear programming (LP) formulation.
E. Replacing the variance with the standard deviation would still result in a non-linear problem.


QUESTION 5
In the ice cream production planning problem (Slides 1-6), what is the economic interpretation of the two components in the objective function: $50 \sum_{i=1}^{12} (y_i + z_i)$ and $20 \sum_{i=1}^{12} S_i$?  Explain how these costs influence the optimal production plan.
A. The first term represents the cost of raw materials, and the second term represents the cost of labor.
B. The first term represents the cost of changing production levels between months, and the second term represents the cost of storing surplus ice cream.
C. The first term represents the fixed costs of production, and the second term represents the variable costs of production.
D. The first term represents the cost of transportation, and the second term represents the cost of spoilage.
E. The first term represents the profit from sales, and the second term represents the loss from unsold ice cream.

ANSWER: B

EXPLANATION:
A. Raw materials and labor costs are important in production planning, but they are not directly represented by these terms in the given objective function.  The objective function focuses on the costs associated with production changes and storage.
B. The first term, $50 \sum_{i=1}^{12} (y_i + z_i)$, represents the cost associated with changing production levels from one month to the next.  The variables $y_i$ and $z_i$ are introduced to linearize the absolute value of the change in production.  A higher cost (50 per unit change) incentivizes a more stable production plan. The second term, $20 \sum_{i=1}^{12} S_i$, represents the cost of storing surplus ice cream.  The variable $S_i$ represents the surplus inventory at the end of month $i$. A higher storage cost (20 per unit) incentivizes producing closer to demand to avoid excess inventory.  These costs together influence the optimal production plan by balancing the trade-off between stable production (minimizing changes) and minimizing storage costs. The optimal plan will find a balance between these two competing objectives.
C. While fixed and variable costs are relevant in production, the given objective function does not explicitly separate them. The costs are related to changes in production and storage, not fixed versus variable costs.
D. Transportation and spoilage costs are relevant in production planning, but they are not explicitly included in the given objective function. The model focuses on the costs of production changes and storage.
E. The objective function aims to minimize costs, not maximize profits.  While unsold ice cream represents a loss, it is already incorporated into the storage cost term.

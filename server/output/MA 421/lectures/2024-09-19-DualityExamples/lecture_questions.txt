QUESTION 1

C. This is incorrect. It only considers the second element of the vector $b$.

QUESTION 2

EXPLANATION:

B. The dual problem, as presented in Slides 7 and 8, is interpreted as a profit maximization problem. The constraints $\sum_i y_i a_{ij} \le c_j$ ensure that the cost of pills to replace the nutrients in food $j$ is less than or equal to the cost of food $j$ itself. This makes economic sense because no one would buy the pills if they cost more than the food.

QUESTION 3
Considering the primal (diet) problem and its dual (pill-selling) problem, what is the significance of the Weak Duality Theorem in this context?
A. The Weak Duality Theorem guarantees that the optimal cost of the diet will always be less than the maximum profit from selling pills.
B. The Weak Duality Theorem states that the optimal solution to the primal problem is always equal to the optimal solution to the dual problem.
C. The Weak Duality Theorem is irrelevant to the diet problem and its dual because they are not related.
D. The Weak Duality Theorem guarantees that the optimal cost of the diet will always be greater than or equal to the maximum profit from selling pills.
E. The Weak Duality Theorem only applies to problems with equality constraints, not inequality constraints like the diet problem.

ANSWER: D

EXPLANATION:
A. This statement is incorrect. The Weak Duality Theorem states that the primal objective function value is less than or equal to the dual objective function value for any feasible solutions.
B. This describes the Strong Duality Theorem, which states that the optimal objective function values of the primal and dual problems are equal if both problems have optimal solutions. The Weak Duality Theorem is a weaker statement that applies to any feasible solutions.
C. The Weak Duality Theorem is fundamental to duality theory and applies directly to the relationship between the primal diet problem and its dual pill-selling problem.
D. The Weak Duality Theorem states that the objective function value of any feasible solution to the primal problem is always less than or equal to the objective function value of any feasible solution to the dual problem. In the context of the diet problem, this means the minimum cost of the diet will always be greater than or equal to the maximum profit from selling the equivalent pills. This relationship holds for any feasible solutions, not just the optimal ones.
E. The Weak Duality Theorem applies to problems with both equality and inequality constraints.  The diet problem has inequality constraints, and the theorem still holds.

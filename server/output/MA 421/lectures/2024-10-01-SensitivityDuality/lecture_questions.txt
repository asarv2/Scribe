QUESTION 1
In the linear program presented in Slide 1, if the right-hand side of the second constraint ($10x_1 + 30x_2 \le 4000$) is increased by $t$, how does this affect the optimal values of $x_1$, $x_2$, and $\zeta$?  What is the maximum allowable increase $t$ before the current optimal solution becomes infeasible?
A. $x_1$ decreases, $x_2$ increases, $\zeta$ increases; $t \le 1000$
B. $x_1$ increases, $x_2$ decreases, $\zeta$ increases; $t \le 3000$
C. $x_1$ decreases, $x_2$ increases, $\zeta$ increases; $t \le 3000$
D. $x_1$ increases, $x_2$ decreases, $\zeta$ decreases; $t \le 1000$
E. $x_1$ decreases, $x_2$ increases, $\zeta$ remains unchanged; $t \le 1000$

ANSWER: A

EXPLANATION:
A. From Slide 7, increasing the right-hand side of the second constraint by $t$ leads to the updated optimal solution $x_1^* = 25 - \frac{t}{40}$ and $x_2^* = 75 + \frac{t}{40}$.  Thus, $x_1$ decreases and $x_2$ increases. The objective function becomes $\zeta^* = 6250 + 0.75t$, indicating an increase in $\zeta$. For feasibility, we require $x_1^* \ge 0$ and $x_2^* \ge 0$, which implies $25 - \frac{t}{40} \ge 0$ and $75 + \frac{t}{40} \ge 0$.  Solving these inequalities gives $t \le 1000$ and $t \ge -3000$. Therefore, the maximum allowable increase is $t \le 1000$.
B. The direction of change in $x_1$ and $x_2$ is incorrect.  Slide 7 shows that $x_1$ decreases and $x_2$ increases.
C. The maximum allowable increase is incorrect.  The constraint $x_1^* \ge 0$ limits the increase in $t$ to 1000.
D. The direction of change in $\zeta$ is incorrect. Slide 8 shows that $\zeta$ increases with $t$.
E. The objective function does change with $t$. Slide 8 shows that $\zeta$ increases with $t$.


QUESTION 2
Using complementary slackness (Slide 3), verify the optimality of the solution $x_1^* = 25$, $x_2^* = 75$ for the primal problem in Slide 1.  What are the optimal values of the dual variables $y_1^*$ and $y_2^*$, and how do they relate to the optimal objective function value?
A. $y_1^* = 32.5$, $y_2^* = 0.75$; Strong duality holds, $\zeta^* = 6250 = 100y_1^* + 4000y_2^*$.
B. $y_1^* = 0$, $y_2^* = 0$; Strong duality does not hold.
C. $y_1^* = 25$, $y_2^* = 75$; Strong duality holds, $\zeta^* = 6250 = 100y_1^* + 4000y_2^*$.
D. $y_1^* = 40$, $y_2^* = 70$; Strong duality does not hold.
E. $y_1^* = 70$, $y_2^* = 40$; Strong duality holds, $\zeta^* = 6250 = 100y_1^* + 4000y_2^*$.

ANSWER: A

EXPLANATION:
A. Since $x_1^* > 0$ and $x_2^* > 0$, complementary slackness implies that the corresponding dual slack variables are zero: $y_1 + 10y_2 = 40$ and $y_1 + 50y_2 = 70$. Solving this system yields $y_1^* = 32.5$ and $y_2^* = 0.75$.  Substituting these values into the dual objective function gives $100(32.5) + 4000(0.75) = 6250$, which equals the optimal primal objective function value $\zeta^*$, confirming strong duality.
B. This solution violates complementary slackness since $x_1^* > 0$ and $x_2^* > 0$ imply $y_1 + 10y_2 = 40$ and $y_1 + 50y_2 = 70$.
C. These values are the primal optimal solutions, not the dual optimal solutions.
D. These values do not satisfy the dual constraints or complementary slackness.
E. These values do not satisfy the dual constraints or complementary slackness.

This is evident from Slide 10, where $\Delta \zeta^* = (\Delta b)^T y^*$.

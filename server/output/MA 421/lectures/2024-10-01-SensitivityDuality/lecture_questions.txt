QUESTION 1
In Slide 7, the optimal solution is given by $x_1^* = 25 - \frac{t}{40}$ and $x_2^* = 75 + \frac{t}{40}$.  If we want to maintain feasibility ($x_1^*, x_2^* \ge 0$), what is the range of values for $t$?
A. $-3000 \le t \le 1000$
B. $-750 \le t \le 1000$
C. $0 \le t \le 1000$
D. $-3000 \le t \le 0$
E. $-1000 \le t \le 3000$

ANSWER: A

EXPLANATION:
A. The correct answer is A.  To maintain feasibility, we need $25 - \frac{t}{40} \ge 0$ and $75 + \frac{t}{40} \ge 0$. Solving these inequalities gives $t \le 1000$ and $t \ge -3000$, respectively. Therefore, the range of $t$ is $-3000 \le t \le 1000$.
B. Incorrect. This range only considers the constraint $x_1^* \ge 0$.
C. Incorrect. This range only considers the constraint $x_1^* \ge 0$ and ignores the lower bound.
D. Incorrect. This range only considers the constraint $x_2^* \ge 0$ and ignores the upper bound.
E. Incorrect. This range has the bounds reversed.

QUESTION 3
Based on Slides 2 and 3, how does the concept of complementary slackness help verify the optimality of the solution found in Slide 1?
A. Complementary slackness proves that the primal and dual objective functions are equal at the optimal solution.
B. Complementary slackness ensures that the constraints of both the primal and dual problems are satisfied.
C. Complementary slackness establishes a relationship between positive primal variables and zero dual slack variables (and vice versa), allowing for verification of the optimal solution.
D. Complementary slackness guarantees the existence of an optimal solution for both the primal and dual problems.
E. Complementary slackness is not directly relevant to verifying the optimality of the solution in Slide 1.

ANSWER: C

EXPLANATION:
A. While complementary slackness is related to strong duality (the equality of primal and dual objective values at optimality), it doesn't directly prove it. Strong duality is verified by comparing the objective function values obtained from the primal and dual optimal solutions.
B. Complementary slackness doesn't directly ensure that all constraints are satisfied.  Feasibility is checked separately by verifying that the constraints are satisfied by the optimal solution.
C. Slide 3 uses complementary slackness. Because $x_1^* > 0$ and $x_2^* > 0$, the corresponding dual slack variables $z_1$ and $z_2$ must be 0. This leads to a system of equations that can be solved to find the optimal dual variables, which are then used to verify strong duality.
D. Complementary slackness doesn't guarantee the existence of an optimal solution.  The existence of an optimal solution is determined by the properties of the linear program itself (e.g., boundedness and feasibility).
E. Complementary slackness is a crucial tool for verifying optimality in linear programming.

Slide 10 explicitly shows that for small changes in the right-hand side, the change in the optimal objective function value is given by $\Delta \zeta^* = (\Delta b)^T y^*$, which is the dot product of the change in the right-hand side vector and the optimal dual solution vector. This demonstrates the crucial role of the dual variables (shadow prices) in sensitivity analysis.

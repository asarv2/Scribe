QUESTION 1
In the context of Farkas' Lemma, what is the fundamental implication of the existence of a vector $y$ satisfying $A^T y = 0$, $y \ge 0$, and $b^T y < 0$?
A. The system $Ax \le b$ has a unique solution.
B. The system $Ax \le b$ has at least one solution.
C. The system $Ax \le b$ has no solution.
D. The system $Ax \le b$ is unbounded.
E. The system $Ax \le b$ is infeasible and unbounded.

ANSWER: C

EXPLANATION:
A. Incorrect. Farkas' Lemma doesn't address uniqueness; it focuses on existence.
B. Incorrect. The conditions on $y$ indicate the *absence* of a solution to $Ax \le b$.
C. The existence of such a vector $y$ directly implies that the system $Ax \le b$ has no solution. This is the core statement of Farkas' Lemma.
D. Incorrect. Unboundedness is a different property related to the objective function in optimization problems, not directly to the feasibility of the constraints.
E. Incorrect.  The conditions imply infeasibility, but not necessarily unboundedness.

Let's try $y = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$. Then $A^T y = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix} \ne 0$.  If we let $y = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$, then $A^T y = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix} \ne 0$.

QUESTION 1
In the context of the B\&N method, what is the primary motivation for transforming a general linear program into a form with only equality constraints and non-negativity restrictions on the variables?
A. To simplify the objective function.
B. To facilitate the application of the simplex method directly.
C. To make the problem easier to visualize geometrically.
D. To ensure the existence of a feasible solution.
E. To readily apply the Lagrangian method for deriving the dual problem.

ANSWER: E

EXPLANATION:
A. While simplifying the objective function might be a beneficial side effect, it's not the primary motivation for the transformation. The focus is on the constraints.
B. The simplex method can handle inequality constraints. The transformation is necessary for the Lagrangian approach, not for direct application of the simplex method.
C. Geometric visualization is helpful, but the transformation's main purpose is algebraic, to facilitate the Lagrangian method.
D. The transformation doesn't guarantee feasibility; feasibility is a property of the problem itself.
E. The B\&N method uses the Lagrangian to derive the dual problem.  Transforming the primal problem into a form with only equality constraints and non-negativity makes the application of the Lagrangian straightforward and allows for a cleaner derivation of the dual.

E. Weak duality is fundamental to the B\&N method and its derivation of the dual problem.

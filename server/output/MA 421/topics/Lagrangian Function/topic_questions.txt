QUESTION 1
What is the primary reason why it's difficult to provide a precise theoretical analysis of the average-case performance of the simplex method?
A. The simplex method's algorithm is inherently non-deterministic.
B. The worst-case scenarios for the simplex method are too frequent in practice.
C. It is challenging to define a realistic probability distribution over the space of all possible linear programming problems.
D. The simplex method's runtime is highly sensitive to the choice of pivot rule, making analysis complex.
E. The computational cost of simulating the simplex method on a large number of random instances is prohibitive.

ANSWER: C

EXPLANATION:
A. Answer A is incorrect. While the choice of pivot rule introduces some non-determinism, the core difficulty lies in modeling the input distribution, not the algorithm's inherent randomness.
B. Answer B is incorrect.  Worst-case scenarios are indeed a concern, but the average-case analysis aims to understand the typical performance, not just the worst-case behavior. The difficulty lies in defining "typical."
C. Answer C is correct.  A major hurdle in analyzing the average-case performance is the difficulty in creating a probability distribution that accurately reflects real-world linear programming problems.  The space of possible problems is vast and diverse, and it's hard to capture this diversity in a mathematically tractable way.
D. Answer D is incorrect.  The choice of pivot rule is a factor, but the fundamental problem is the difficulty in defining a representative probability distribution over the input space.
E. Answer E is incorrect. While simulations are computationally expensive, the core theoretical challenge is the difficulty in defining a realistic probability distribution for the input problems, not just the computational cost of analysis.

B. The Klee-Minty problem's feasible region is a highly skewed polytope. This skewed geometry forces the simplex method, under certain pivot rules, to traverse a path that visits an exponential number of vertices before reaching the optimal solution.  The specific structure of the constraints creates this unfavorable path.
C. The objective function, while specially constructed, is not the primary reason for the exponential complexity. The geometry of the feasible region is the key factor.
D. The ratio of variables to constraints is not the defining characteristic.  The problem's structure, leading to a highly skewed feasible region, is the crucial element.
E. The use of integer coefficients is not the primary cause. The specific structure of the constraints, creating the skewed feasible region, is the key factor.

B. $\mathcal{L}(x, y, \lambda) = x^2 + y^2 - \lambda(x + y - 1)$

B. This is incorrect because the constraint should be added, not subtracted.
C. This is incorrect; the objective function and constraint are reversed.
D. This is incorrect; both the objective function and constraint are reversed, and the constraint is subtracted.
E. This is incorrect; the constraint is incorrectly stated.

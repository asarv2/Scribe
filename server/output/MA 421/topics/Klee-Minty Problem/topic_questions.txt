QUESTION 1
The Klee-Minty problem demonstrates that the simplex method can exhibit exponential time complexity in the worst case. What is the fundamental characteristic of the Klee-Minty problem that leads to this behavior?
A. The presence of many degenerate vertices in the feasible region.
B. The use of a non-standard simplex method variant.
C. The highly skewed geometry of its feasible region, causing the simplex method to traverse many vertices before reaching the optimal solution.
D. The objective function's lack of linearity.
E. The problem's inherent non-convexity.

ANSWER: C

EXPLANATION:
A. While degeneracy can slow down the simplex method, it doesn't inherently cause exponential time complexity. The Klee-Minty problem's exponential behavior is due to the geometry, not degeneracy.
B. The Klee-Minty problem uses a standard simplex method. The exponential behavior is a property of the problem itself, not a flaw in a specific variant of the algorithm.
C. The Klee-Minty problem is specifically constructed to have a feasible region that is a highly distorted polytope.  The simplex method, following its rules, will visit a large number of vertices, resulting in exponential time complexity. The skewed geometry forces the algorithm to take a long path to the optimal solution.
D. The objective function in the Klee-Minty problem is linear. The exponential complexity arises from the structure of the constraints and the resulting geometry of the feasible region.
E. The feasible region of a linear program is always convex. The Klee-Minty problem is a linear program, and its feasible region is convex. The exponential complexity is a result of the specific shape of this convex region.

QUESTION 1
In the context of linear programming, what is the primary role of a slack variable, and how does its introduction affect the structure of the problem?
A. Slack variables are used to convert equality constraints into inequality constraints, simplifying the problem's structure.
B. Slack variables represent the difference between the left-hand side and right-hand side of an inequality constraint, transforming inequalities into equalities and allowing for easier application of the simplex method.
C. Slack variables are added to the objective function to improve its linearity and convexity, leading to a more efficient solution process.
D. Slack variables are used to eliminate redundant constraints, reducing the problem's size and complexity.
E. Slack variables are introduced to handle unbounded variables, ensuring that the problem remains well-defined.

ANSWER: B

EXPLANATION:
A. This is incorrect. Slack variables do the opposite; they convert inequalities to equalities.
B. Slack variables bridge the gap between inequality and equality constraints.  By representing the difference between the two sides of an inequality, they allow us to rewrite the inequality as an equality, a crucial step for algorithms like the simplex method which operate on systems of equations.
C. Slack variables are not added to the objective function; they are added to the constraints.
D. Slack variables do not eliminate constraints; they modify them.
E. Unbounded variables are handled through other techniques, not slack variables.


QUESTION 2

A. The constraint becomes $ax + by + s = c$, where $s$ is the slack variable, and this alteration is insignificant for solving the problem.
B. The constraint becomes $ax + by - s = c$, where $s$ is the slack variable, and this alteration is crucial for applying the simplex method.
C. The constraint becomes $ax + by + s \le c$, where $s$ is the slack variable, and this alteration simplifies the problem's structure.
D. The constraint becomes $ax + by = c$, where the slack variable is implicitly incorporated, and this alteration is necessary for graphical solution methods.
E. The constraint remains unchanged, as slack variables are only used for constraints of the form $ax + by \ge c$.

ANSWER: B

EXPLANATION:
A. While the equation is correct, the statement that the alteration is insignificant is false. The transformation to an equality is crucial.
B. Introducing a slack variable transforms the inequality into an equality, which is essential for the simplex method. The simplex method operates on a system of equations, and the slack variable allows us to represent the inequality constraint within this framework.
C. The slack variable should transform the inequality into an equality, not another inequality.
D. The slack variable is not implicitly incorporated; it's explicitly part of the equation.
E. Slack variables are used for both $\le$ and $\ge$ inequalities.


QUESTION 3
What is the geometric interpretation of a slack variable in a linear programming problem with two variables and one constraint of the form $ax + by \le c$?
A. The slack variable represents the distance from a point $(x, y)$ to the line $ax + by = c$.
B. The slack variable represents the distance from the origin (0,0) to the line $ax + by = c$.
C. The slack variable represents the area of the feasible region.
D. The slack variable represents the slope of the line $ax + by = c$.
E. The slack variable has no geometric interpretation in this context.

ANSWER: A

EXPLANATION:
A. The slack variable, $s = c - (ax + by)$, represents the vertical distance between a point $(x, y)$ satisfying the constraint and the boundary line $ax + by = c$.  If the point is on the line, $s = 0$. If the point is below the line, $s > 0$.
B. The slack variable is not the distance from the origin to the line; it's the distance from a point satisfying the constraint to the line.
C. The slack variable does not represent the area of the feasible region.
D. The slack variable does not represent the slope of the line.
E. The slack variable has a clear geometric interpretation.

C. Artificial variables are used to find an initial feasible solution, not to convert inequalities to equalities.

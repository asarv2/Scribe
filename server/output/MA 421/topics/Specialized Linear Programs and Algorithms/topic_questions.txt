QUESTION 1
In the context of the cutting stock problem, explain the trade-off between minimizing the number of rolls used and the complexity of generating cutting patterns. How does this trade-off influence the choice of algorithms and solution approaches?
A. Minimizing rolls always prioritizes simplicity in cutting patterns, regardless of problem size.
B. Increased pattern complexity always leads to fewer rolls used, making it the optimal strategy.
C. There's a trade-off: simpler patterns are easier to generate but might use more rolls, while complex patterns might save rolls but are computationally expensive to find.
D. The number of rolls and pattern complexity are independent factors; optimizing one doesn't affect the other.
E. The trade-off is insignificant in practice, as efficient algorithms easily find optimal solutions regardless of complexity.

ANSWER: C

EXPLANATION:
A. This is incorrect; minimizing rolls often requires exploring more complex patterns.
B. This is incorrect; while more complex patterns can reduce rolls, the computational cost to find them can outweigh the benefit.
C. This option correctly identifies the core challenge in the cutting stock problem.  Simpler patterns are easier to find but may not be optimal in terms of roll usage. More complex patterns can lead to better roll utilization but require more computational effort to discover.
D. This is incorrect; there is a direct relationship between pattern complexity and the number of rolls used.
E. This is incorrect; large-scale cutting stock problems are computationally challenging, and finding the optimal solution is often intractable.


QUESTION 2
In the Markowitz portfolio optimization model, explain how the risk aversion parameter influences the optimal portfolio composition. How does changing this parameter affect the balance between risk and return in the resulting portfolio?
A. The risk aversion parameter has no effect on portfolio composition; only expected returns and variances matter.
B. Increasing risk aversion always leads to a portfolio with higher risk and higher expected return.
C. Increasing risk aversion leads to a portfolio with lower risk and potentially lower expected return.
D. The risk aversion parameter only affects the expected return, not the risk of the portfolio.
E. The risk aversion parameter only affects the risk, not the expected return of the portfolio.

ANSWER: C

EXPLANATION:
A. The risk aversion parameter is a crucial component of the objective function, directly influencing the portfolio's composition.
B. This is incorrect; increasing risk aversion leads to a less risky portfolio, potentially sacrificing some return.
C. A higher risk aversion parameter implies the investor is more averse to risk. The model will then prioritize minimizing risk, even if it means accepting a lower expected return.
D. The risk aversion parameter directly impacts both risk and return by weighting them differently in the objective function.
E. Similar to D, the risk aversion parameter affects both risk and return in the optimization.


QUESTION 3
Describe the key differences between $L^1$, $L^2$, and $L^\infty$ regression in terms of their sensitivity to outliers and the computational challenges associated with each.  Which method is generally considered most robust to outliers?
A. $L^1$, $L^2$, and $L^\infty$ are equally sensitive to outliers.
B. $L^2$ is most robust to outliers, followed by $L^1$, then $L^\infty$.
C. $L^1$ is most robust to outliers, followed by $L^2$, then $L^\infty$.
D. $L^\infty$ is most robust to outliers, followed by $L^1$, then $L^2$.
E. $L^2$ and $L^1$ are equally robust to outliers, and $L^\infty$ is the least robust.

ANSWER: C

EXPLANATION:
A. Different norms have vastly different sensitivities to outliers.
B. This order of robustness is incorrect.
C. The $L^1$ norm is less sensitive to outliers than the $L^2$ norm, and the $L^\infty$ norm is the most sensitive.  Computationally, $L^2$ is easiest, followed by $L^1$ (linear programming), and $L^\infty$ is the most challenging.
D. This order of robustness is incorrect.
E. While $L^2$ and $L^1$ are both less sensitive than $L^\infty$, $L^1$ is generally considered more robust to outliers.

QUESTION 5
In the context of specialized linear programs and algorithms, explain the core differences between the cutting stock problem and the bin packing problem.  How do their objectives, constraints, and typical solution approaches differ?
A. Both problems are identical, focusing on minimizing waste when packing items into containers.
B. The cutting stock problem focuses on cutting items from larger stock, while bin packing focuses on fitting items into pre-existing bins, with different objective functions and constraints.
C. The cutting stock problem uses a greedy approach, while bin packing uses linear programming.
D. Bin packing is a special case of the cutting stock problem where the bin sizes are fixed.
E. Both problems are NP-hard, so there's no practical difference in their solution approaches.

ANSWER: B

EXPLANATION:
A. The problems have distinct objectives and constraints, leading to different solution strategies.
B. The cutting stock problem aims to minimize the number of rolls of material needed to fulfill orders of various lengths, while bin packing aims to minimize the number of bins needed to pack items of various sizes.  Cutting stock often uses linear programming with column generation, while bin packing frequently employs heuristics or approximation algorithms due to its NP-hard nature.  The constraints also differ significantly, reflecting the different ways items are packed or cut.
C. While heuristics are common for bin packing, linear programming is a common approach for cutting stock, and neither is exclusive to one problem.
D. While bin packing can be viewed as a restricted version of cutting stock, the core differences in objectives and solution approaches remain.
E. While both are NP-hard, this doesn't negate the significant differences in their formulations and solution strategies.

QUESTION 1
In $L^1$-regression, what is the primary objective of the optimization problem, and how does this objective differ from that of $L^2$-regression?
A. To minimize the sum of squared errors between the predicted and actual values, similar to $L^2$-regression.
B. To minimize the sum of absolute errors between the predicted and actual values, unlike $L^2$-regression which minimizes squared errors.
C. To maximize the sum of absolute errors between the predicted and actual values.
D. To minimize the maximum absolute error between any single predicted and actual value.
E. To find the model parameters that perfectly fit all data points, regardless of error.

ANSWER: B

EXPLANATION:
A. This describes the objective of $L^2$-regression, not $L^1$-regression.
B. The primary objective in $L^1$-regression is to minimize the sum of the absolute differences between the predicted and actual values. This contrasts with $L^2$-regression, which aims to minimize the sum of the *squares* of these differences.
C. The goal is to *minimize*, not maximize, the error.
D. This describes a different type of regression problem focused on minimizing the maximum error.
E. Perfect fitting is generally not possible and not the goal of regression; the aim is to find a good balance between fitting the data and avoiding overfitting.


QUESTION 2
How does the choice of the loss function ($L^1$ vs. $L^2$) affect the sensitivity of the regression model to outliers in the dataset?
A. Both $L^1$ and $L^2$ are equally sensitive to outliers.
B. $L^1$ is more sensitive to outliers than $L^2$.
C. $L^2$ is more sensitive to outliers than $L^1$.
D. $L^1$ is completely insensitive to outliers, while $L^2$ is highly sensitive.
E. The sensitivity to outliers depends on the specific dataset and not the loss function.

ANSWER: C

EXPLANATION:
A. This is incorrect; the $L^1$ and $L^2$ loss functions have different sensitivities to outliers.
B. This is the opposite of the correct answer.
C. The $L^2$ loss function (sum of squared errors) is more sensitive to outliers because squaring large errors amplifies their influence on the model parameters.  $L^1$ (sum of absolute errors) is more robust to outliers because it treats all errors linearly.
D. While $L^1$ is less sensitive, it is not completely insensitive to outliers.
E. The choice of loss function significantly impacts the sensitivity to outliers.


QUESTION 3
What is a key computational challenge associated with solving the $L^1$-regression problem compared to $L^2$-regression, and why does this challenge arise?
A. $L^1$-regression is computationally faster than $L^2$-regression due to its simpler loss function.
B. $L^1$-regression involves a non-differentiable loss function, making it more challenging to solve using gradient-based methods.
C. $L^1$-regression requires significantly more memory than $L^2$-regression.
D. $L^1$-regression is inherently unstable and prone to numerical errors.
E. There is no significant computational difference between $L^1$ and $L^2$ regression.

ANSWER: B

EXPLANATION:
A. The opposite is true; $L^1$ regression is computationally more challenging.
B. The $L^1$ loss function is not differentiable at zero, making it difficult to solve using standard gradient-based optimization techniques.  $L^2$ regression has a differentiable loss function, leading to simpler solutions via normal equations or gradient descent.
C. Memory requirements are not a primary distinguishing factor between the two methods.
D. Both methods can be numerically stable when implemented correctly.
E. There is a significant computational difference due to the differentiability of the loss function.


QUESTION 4
Describe the geometric interpretation of the solution to the $L^1$-regression problem, and how does it differ from the geometric interpretation of the $L^2$-regression solution?
A. The $L^1$ solution minimizes the sum of squared distances from the data points to the regression line, while the $L^2$ solution minimizes the sum of absolute distances.
B. The $L^2$ solution is the orthogonal projection of the data points onto the regression line, while the $L^1$ solution is not necessarily an orthogonal projection.
C. Both $L^1$ and $L^2$ solutions are always identical, regardless of the data.
D. The $L^1$ solution is always a point within the convex hull of the data points, while the $L^2$ solution may lie outside.
E. There is no meaningful geometric interpretation for either $L^1$ or $L^2$ regression solutions.

ANSWER: B

EXPLANATION:
A. This reverses the descriptions of $L^1$ and $L^2$ solutions.
B. In $L^2$-regression, the solution corresponds to the orthogonal projection of the data points onto the regression line.  In $L^1$-regression, the solution is found at the intersection of medians, and it's not necessarily an orthogonal projection.
C. The solutions are generally different, except in special cases.
D. While the $L^1$ solution often lies within the convex hull, this is not a defining characteristic that distinguishes it from $L^2$.
E. Both methods have meaningful geometric interpretations.

QUESTION 10
Given the data points (1, 2), (2, 4), (3, 5), and using the $L^1$-regression formulation from Slide 5, what is the objective function to minimize?
A. $ \min_{x_1, x_2} \sum_{i=1}^3 (y_i - (x_1 + x_2))^2$
B. $ \min_{x_1, x_2} \sum_{i=1}^3 |y_i - (x_1 + x_2)|$
C. $ \min_{x_1, x_2, t_1, t_2, t_3} (t_1 + t_2 + t_3)$ subject to $-t_i \le y_i - (x_1 + x_2) \le t_i$ for $i = 1, 2, 3$
D. $ \min_{x_1, x_2} \max_{i=1,2,3} |y_i - (x_1 + x_2)|$
E. $ \min_{x_1, x_2} \sum_{i=1}^3 (y_i - (x_1 + x_2))^2$

ANSWER: C

EXPLANATION:
A. This minimizes the sum of squared errors, which is the $L^2$-regression objective, not $L^1$.
B. While this represents the sum of absolute errors, it's not in a form suitable for linear programming. The linear programming formulation requires the introduction of slack variables.
C. The $L^1$-regression aims to minimize the sum of absolute errors.  Slide 5 shows this is achieved by introducing slack variables $t_i$ and minimizing their sum, subject to constraints ensuring each $t_i$ bounds the absolute error.
D. This minimizes the maximum absolute error, which is the $L^\infty$-regression objective.
E. This is the same as option A, minimizing the sum of squared errors ($L^2$-regression).

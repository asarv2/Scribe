QUESTION 1
In the context of linear programming duality, what does the Separating Hyperplane Theorem assert about the relationship between a primal problem with an empty feasible region and its dual problem?
A. The dual problem must also have an empty feasible region.
B. The dual problem must have an unbounded objective function.
C. There exists a hyperplane separating the feasible regions of the primal and dual problems.
D. The dual problem must have a unique optimal solution.
E. The dual problem's objective function value is always zero.

ANSWER: B

EXPLANATION:
A. The dual problem does not necessarily have an empty feasible region.  It could be feasible but unbounded.
B. If the primal feasible region is empty, the dual problem is unbounded.  The Separating Hyperplane Theorem, in this context, implies that there's no feasible solution to the primal problem, meaning the dual problem's objective function can be made arbitrarily large (for a maximization dual) or arbitrarily small (for a minimization dual), hence unbounded.
C. While the Separating Hyperplane Theorem deals with hyperplanes, it doesn't directly assert a hyperplane separating the feasible regions of the primal and dual problems when the primal is infeasible. The theorem applies to separating a point from a convex set, not necessarily two convex sets.
D. The dual problem does not necessarily have a unique optimal solution; it could have multiple optimal solutions or be unbounded.
E. The dual problem's objective function value is not always zero.  It can take on any value, or be unbounded, depending on the problem's structure.


QUESTION 2
Considering the primal and dual problems presented on Slide 1, how does the Separating Hyperplane Theorem provide a geometric interpretation of strong duality when both the primal and dual problems have optimal solutions?
A. It shows that the optimal objective function values are equal because the optimal solutions lie on the same hyperplane.
B. It demonstrates that there is always a hyperplane separating the feasible regions of the primal and dual problems.
C. It proves that if the primal problem is unbounded, the dual problem must be infeasible.
D. It implies that the optimal solutions of the primal and dual problems are always unique.
E. It suggests that the feasible regions of the primal and dual problems are always disjoint.

ANSWER: A

EXPLANATION:
A. Strong duality states that the optimal objective function values of the primal and dual problems are equal. The Separating Hyperplane Theorem, in this context, can be interpreted geometrically:  when both problems have optimal solutions, there is no hyperplane separating the optimal solutions; they essentially lie on the same hyperplane (or are very close to each other), reflecting the equality of their objective function values.
B. This statement is incorrect.  The Separating Hyperplane Theorem applies when there is a point outside a convex set.  In strong duality, we are concerned with the equality of optimal values, not necessarily a separation of feasible regions.
C. This is a consequence of weak duality, not a direct interpretation from the Separating Hyperplane Theorem.
D. The optimal solutions are not always unique.  Both primal and dual problems can have multiple optimal solutions.
E. The feasible regions are not always disjoint.  In fact, strong duality implies a close relationship between the optimal solutions of the primal and dual problems.

In linear programming, if one problem is infeasible (its feasible region is empty), then the other problem is either infeasible or unbounded.  The non-existence of a separating hyperplane reflects this situation where at least one problem is infeasible.

This is illustrated on Slide 1 by showing that if the constraints of the primal problem are inconsistent, leading to an empty feasible region, then the dual problem's objective function can be made arbitrarily small (for a minimization problem) or large (for a maximization problem).

A. The Separating Hyperplane Theorem is a fundamental result in convex analysis that has significant implications for linear programming duality. The theorem provides a geometric interpretation of the duality relationship, showing how the infeasibility of one problem implies the unboundedness of the other.  This is a crucial link between the geometry of the feasible regions and the behavior of the objective functions in primal and dual problems.
B. This option incorrectly states that the Separating Hyperplane Theorem is irrelevant to linear programming duality.

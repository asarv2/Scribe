Slide 4

\textbf{Elementary Row Operations} (to eliminate variables)

(1) Interchange 2 rows (equations)

\begin{itemize}
    \item $( \leftrightarrow R_i \leftrightarrow R_j )$
\end{itemize}

(2) Multiply 1 row (eqn) by a non-zero no.

\begin{itemize}
    \item $( \leftrightarrow R_i \times q \quad (q \neq 0) \leftrightarrow q R_i )$
\end{itemize}

(3) Add a multiple of 1 row (eqn) to another

\begin{itemize}
    \item $( \leftrightarrow R_i \leftrightarrow R_i + \alpha R_j \leftrightarrow R_i + \alpha R_j )$
\end{itemize}

\textit{Description:}

This slide details the three elementary row operations that can be performed on a matrix (in this context, the augmented matrix representing a system of linear equations).  These operations are fundamental to Gaussian elimination, a method for solving systems of linear equations.  The operations are: (1) swapping two rows, (2) multiplying a row by a non-zero constant, and (3) adding a multiple of one row to another.  These operations are crucial because they do not change the solution set of the system of equations.  The slide is part of a larger presentation likely covering Gaussian elimination and its application to linear programming problems.  The use of the notation $R_i$ and $R_j$ to represent rows is standard in linear algebra.  The slide is likely to be followed by examples of how these operations are applied to solve systems of linear equations.

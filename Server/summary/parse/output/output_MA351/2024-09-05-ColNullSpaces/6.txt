\textbf{Simplex Tableau Example}

\vspace{0.5cm}

\begin{tabular}{c|ccccc|c}
\textbf{} & $x_1$ & $x_2$ & $s_1$ & $s_2$ & $z$ & \textbf{RHS} \\
\hline
$s_1$ & 1 & 2 & 1 & 0 & 0 & 10 \\
$s_2$ & 3 & 1 & 0 & 1 & 0 & 15 \\
\hline
$z$ & -2 & -3 & 0 & 0 & 1 & 0
\end{tabular}

\end{document}

\vspace{0.5cm}

\textbf{Description:}

This slide presents a simple example of a simplex tableau.  The tableau is a tabular representation of a linear programming problem in standard form, specifically for a maximization problem with two variables ($x_1$ and $x_2$) and two constraints.  The columns represent the variables ($x_1$, $x_2$, slack variables $s_1$ and $s_2$, and the objective function $z$).  The rows represent the constraints and the objective function.  The numbers in the tableau are the coefficients of the variables in the constraints and the objective function. The "RHS" column represents the right-hand side values of the constraints and the objective function.  This is a crucial step in the simplex method for solving linear programming problems.  The slide likely follows the previous ones, which introduced linear programming, standard form, and the conversion process.

\textbf{Example: Pivot Operation}

\vspace{0.5cm}

\textbf{Tableau:}

\begin{tabular}{c|cccc|c}
\textbf{} & $x_1$ & $x_2$ & $s_1$ & $s_2$ & \textbf{RHS} \\
\hline
$s_1$ & 1 & 2 & 1 & 0 & 10 \\
$s_2$ & 3 & 1 & 0 & 1 & 15 \\
\hline
$z$ & -2 & -3 & 0 & 0 & 0
\end{tabular}

\vspace{0.5cm}

\textbf{Entering Variable: } $x_2$ (most negative coefficient in the objective row)

\textbf{Leaving Variable: } $s_1$ (smallest non-negative ratio: 10/2 = 5)

\textbf{Pivot Element: } 2

\vspace{0.5cm}

\textbf{Resulting Tableau (after pivot operation): }

\begin{tabular}{c|cccc|c}
\textbf{} & $x_1$ & $x_2$ & $s_1$ & $s_2$ & \textbf{RHS} \\
\hline
$x_2$ & 1/2 & 1 & 1/2 & 0 & 5 \\
$s_2$ & 5/2 & 0 & -1/2 & 1 & 5 \\
\hline
$z$ & -1 & 0 & 3/2 & 0 & 15
\end{tabular}

\end{document}

\vspace{0.5cm}

\textbf{Description:}

This slide provides a step-by-step example of a pivot operation in the simplex method.  It starts with a simplex tableau for a linear programming problem.  The slide explicitly identifies the entering variable ($x_2$) based on the most negative coefficient in the objective row.  It then calculates the ratios of the RHS values to the corresponding coefficients of the entering variable in each constraint row to determine the leaving variable ($s_1$).  The pivot element is identified as 2, located at the intersection of the leaving row ($s_1$) and entering column ($x_2$).  The slide then shows the resulting tableau after performing the pivot operation.  This updated tableau is ready for the next iteration of the simplex method.  The example is crucial for understanding the practical application of the pivot operation in the simplex algorithm.

\textbf{Diagonalization of a matrix}

Given $A^{nxn}$ (assume diagonalizable, $g_i = m_i$ for all)

$A x_i = \lambda_i x_i$, $i=1,2,\dots,n$

$[AX_1 \quad AX_2 \quad \dots \quad AX_n] = [\lambda_1 x_1 \quad \lambda_2 x_2 \quad \dots \quad \lambda_n x_n]$

$A[x_1 \quad \dots \quad x_n] = [\lambda_1 x_1 \quad \dots \quad \lambda_n x_n]$

$A = QDQ^{-1}$

\textbf{Ex 1}

$A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$,

$\begin{bmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} = 1 \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$

$\begin{bmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = 2 \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$

\textbf{Ex 2}

$A = \begin{bmatrix} 5 & -2 \\ 3 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$,

$\begin{bmatrix} 5 & -2 \\ 3 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix} = 2 \begin{bmatrix} 5 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}$

$\begin{bmatrix} 5 & -2 \\ 3 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} = 3 \begin{bmatrix} 5 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}$

This slide is about diagonalization of a matrix. It shows the general formula for diagonalization $A = QDQ^{-1}$, where $Q$ is the matrix of eigenvectors and $D$ is the diagonal matrix of eigenvalues.  Two examples (Ex 1 and Ex 2) are provided to illustrate the process.  The examples show how to find the eigenvalues and eigenvectors for a given matrix $A$.  The slide also shows the matrix multiplications involved in verifying the diagonalization.  The notation and formatting are typical of lecture notes on linear algebra.  The use of square brackets denotes matrices, and the use of $\lambda_i$ and $x_i$ indicates eigenvalues and eigenvectors, respectively.  The examples are presented in a step-by-step manner, showing the intermediate calculations.

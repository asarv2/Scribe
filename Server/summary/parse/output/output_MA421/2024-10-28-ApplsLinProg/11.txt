Slide 11

\textbf{Portfolio Selection (Markowitz Model)}

\newline
\textit{[LV] ch. 13}

\newline
\textbf{Estimation of $ER_j$ and $E[\sum_{j} X_j R_j]$}

\newline
Given historical data, $R_j(t)$, $t=1,\dots,T$

\newline
(1) $ER_j = \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^T R_j(t)$  \hspace{0.5cm} ($= \overline{r_j}$)

\newline
(2) $E[\sum_{j} X_j R_j] = \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^T \left| \sum_{j} X_j (R_j(t) - \overline{r_j}) \right|$


\textbf{Description:}

This slide details how to estimate the expected return of an investment ($ER_j$) and the expected return of a portfolio ($\sum_{j} X_j R_j$) given historical data.  The expected return of an individual investment $ER_j$ is calculated as the average of its historical returns.  The expected return of a portfolio is calculated as the average of the absolute value of the difference between the historical returns and the average return for each investment, weighted by the proportion of the portfolio invested in that investment.  This slide is a crucial step in the Markowitz portfolio selection model, providing the necessary calculations to quantify expected return and risk based on historical data.

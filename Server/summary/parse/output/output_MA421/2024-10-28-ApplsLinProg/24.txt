\textbf{Integer Programming}

\newline
\textit{[EMG] p. 201} \hspace{1cm} Knapsack Problem

\newline
23.1 Knapsack Problem. Consider a picnicker who will be carrying a knapsack that holds a maximum amount $b$ of "stuff". Suppose that our picnicker must decide what to take and what to leave behind. The $j^{th}$ thing that might be taken occupies $a_j$ units of space in the knapsack and will bring that amount of "enjoyment". The knapsack problem then is to maximize enjoyment subject to the constraint that the stuff brought must fit into the knapsack:

\newline
maximize $\sum_{j=1}^n c_j x_j$

\newline
subject to $\sum_{j=1}^n a_j x_j \le b$

\newline
$x_j \in \{0, 1\}$, $j = 1, 2, \dots, n$

\newline
In the usual notation, the knapsack problem is recorded as:

\newline
maximize $\sum_{j=1}^n c_j x_j$

\newline
subject to $\sum_{j=1}^n a_j x_j \le b$

\newline
$x_j \in \{0, 1\}$, $j = 1, 2, \dots, n$

\newline
$x_j$ = nonnegative integer ($i = 1, 2, \dots, m$)


\textbf{Description:}

This slide presents the classic 0-1 knapsack problem, a fundamental problem in integer programming.  The problem describes a scenario where a person has a knapsack with a limited capacity ($b$) and a set of items ($j = 1, 2, \dots, n$) each with a weight ($a_j$) and a value ($c_j$).  The goal is to select a subset of items to maximize the total value ($c_j x_j$) while ensuring that the total weight of the selected items does not exceed the knapsack's capacity ($\sum_{j=1}^n a_j x_j \le b$).  The variables $x_j$ are binary, indicating whether item $j$ is included in the knapsack (1) or not (0).  This problem is a classic example of a combinatorial optimization problem that can be solved using integer programming techniques.

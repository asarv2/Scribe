\textbf{Description:}

This slide presents a log-log plot of empirical data on the performance of the Simplex method.  The x-axis represents the minimum of the number of constraints (m) and the number of variables (n), plotted on a logarithmic scale. The y-axis represents the number of pivots (iterations) required to solve the linear programming problem, also plotted on a logarithmic scale.  The plot shows a scatter of data points, with different symbols (+ for problems with optimal solutions, x for unbounded problems).  A trend line is drawn through the data points, suggesting a power-law relationship between the problem size and the number of pivots.  The text notes that the number of pivots is approximately proportional to 0.15 times the minimum of m and n raised to the power of 1.70 for problems with optimal solutions, and approximately proportional to 1.80 times the minimum of m and n raised to the power of 1.70 for unbounded problems.  This visual representation provides empirical evidence of the computational complexity of the Simplex method.

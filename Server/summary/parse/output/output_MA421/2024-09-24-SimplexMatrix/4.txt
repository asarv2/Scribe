\section*{Basic and Non-Basic Variables}

$\mathbf{x} = \begin{pmatrix}
x_1 \\
\vdots \\
x_n \\
w_1 \\
\vdots \\
w_m
\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}
x_1 \\
\vdots \\
x_n \\
x_{n+1} \\
\vdots \\
x_{n+m}
\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}
\mathbf{x}_B \\
\mathbf{x}_N
\end{pmatrix}$

$\mathbf{c}^T \mathbf{x} = \sum_{j=1}^n c_j x_j = \sum_{j \in B} c_j x_j + \sum_{j \in N} c_j x_j = \mathbf{c}_B^T \mathbf{x}_B + \mathbf{c}_N^T \mathbf{x}_N$

\textbf{Description:}

This slide introduces the concept of basic and non-basic variables in the context of the simplex method.  It shows how the vector $\mathbf{x}$ can be partitioned into two sub-vectors: $\mathbf{x}_B$ (basic variables) and $\mathbf{x}_N$ (non-basic variables).  The notation suggests that the basic variables are a subset of the original variables, and the non-basic variables are the remaining ones.  The equation $\mathbf{c}^T \mathbf{x} = \sum_{j=1}^n c_j x_j = \sum_{j \in B} c_j x_j + \sum_{j \in N} c_j x_j = \mathbf{c}_B^T \mathbf{x}_B + \mathbf{c}_N^T \mathbf{x}_N$ demonstrates how the objective function can be expressed in terms of basic and non-basic variables.  This is a crucial step in the simplex algorithm, as it allows for efficient calculation of the objective function and identification of the next iteration.  The notation $j \in B$ and $j \in N$ indicates that the summation is over the indices of the basic and non-basic variables, respectively.  The slide is likely part of a lecture explaining the simplex method's underlying structure.

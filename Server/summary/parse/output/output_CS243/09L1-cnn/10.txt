# Why convolution? Not fully connected?

Local Receptive Field Leads to Sparse Connectivity (affects less)

**Top Left:**

* **Sparse connections:**  The text "Sparse connections due to small convolution kernel" is displayed.  This text explains that convolution leads to fewer connections between neurons compared to a fully connected network.  This is because the convolution kernel (filter) operates on a small local region of the input data.

* **Diagram (Top):** A diagram of a network is shown.  It depicts a layer of neurons (circles) connected to another layer of neurons.  The connections between the neurons are not fully connected; instead, each neuron in the top layer is connected to only a few neurons in the bottom layer.  The connections are shown as lines.  This diagram visually represents the sparse connectivity characteristic of convolution.  The neurons in the top layer are labeled s1, s2, s3, s4, and s5.  The neurons in the bottom layer are labeled x1, x2, x3, x4, and x5.  The connections between the neurons are shown as lines, and each line has a weight associated with it.  The diagram is positioned in the top half of the slide.

**Bottom:**

* **Dense connections:** The text "Dense connections" is displayed.  This text explains that a fully connected network has many connections between neurons.

* **Diagram (Bottom):** A diagram of a network is shown.  It depicts a layer of neurons (circles) connected to another layer of neurons.  The connections between the neurons are fully connected; each neuron in the top layer is connected to every neuron in the bottom layer.  The connections are shown as lines.  This diagram visually represents the dense connectivity characteristic of a fully connected network.  The neurons in the top layer are labeled s1, s2, s3, s4, and s5.  The neurons in the bottom layer are labeled x1, x2, x3, x4, and x5.  The connections between the neurons are shown as lines, and each line has a weight associated with it.  The diagram is positioned in the bottom half of the slide.


**Description:**

This slide, part of a lecture on AI Basics, explains why convolution is used in neural networks, specifically in convolutional neural networks (CNNs).  It contrasts the sparse connectivity of convolution with the dense connectivity of a fully connected network.  The diagrams visually represent the difference in connectivity patterns between the two types of networks.  The slide uses the terms "sparse connections" and "dense connections" to highlight the key difference in the way the networks are structured.  The slide is designed to help the audience understand the rationale behind using convolution in CNNs.

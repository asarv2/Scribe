# Slide 14

**Discrimination in online ad delivery**

Sweeney observed in 2013 that black-identifying names turned out to be much more likely than white-identifying names to generate ads that including the word "arrest" (60 per cent versus 48 per cent).

Google uses a learning algorithm to place ads that are most often clicked on.

Likely to be a reflection of people clicking on those ads more for 'black' names

Source: Lawrence Hunter, Colorado School of Mines


---

**Description:**

This slide discusses a specific example of bias in online advertising.  It describes research by Latanya Sweeney showing that in 2013, ads containing the word "arrest" were disproportionately shown to individuals with names associated with Black individuals.  The slide also notes that Google's ad placement algorithm, which prioritizes frequently clicked ads, may be amplifying this bias.  The overall message is that algorithms can inadvertently perpetuate existing societal biases if trained on data containing those biases.  The source is clearly attributed, which is a standard practice in academic presentations.  The slide is part of a larger discussion on bias in AI and the potential for algorithms to reflect and amplify existing societal inequalities.

**Group Fairness**

Equalize two groups S, T at the level of outcomes

* For example, S=minority, T=everyone else
* P( outcome o | S ) = P( outcome o | T )

This is not strong enough as a notion of fairness

* Sometimes desirable, but can be abused
* Self-fulfilling prophecy: select smartest students in T, random students in S, then students in T will perform better

This slide discusses the concept of group fairness in the context of AI.  It defines group fairness as equalizing outcomes for two groups (e.g., a minority group S and a majority group T) at the level of outcomes.  The example given is that the probability of a particular outcome for group S should be equal to the probability of that same outcome for group T.  The slide notes that this definition of fairness isn't sufficient, as it can be misused or lead to unintended negative consequences, such as a self-fulfilling prophecy.  The example of selecting the smartest students for one group and random students for another illustrates how this approach can lead to unequal outcomes.

# Slide 31

**Gaussian pyramids [Burt and Adelson, 1983]**

**Idea:**

Represent an N x N image as a "pyramid" of 1 x 1, 2 x 2, 4 x 4, ..., 2<sup>k</sup> x 2<sup>k</sup> (assuming N = 2<sup>k</sup>)

**(Figure):**

A 3D pyramid-shaped diagram is shown.  The pyramid's base represents the original image (level 0).  The pyramid tapers upward, with progressively smaller levels (1, 2, etc.) representing successively smaller versions of the image.  Each level is a grid of squares, with the squares getting smaller as you move up the pyramid.  The lines connecting the squares in the different levels visually represent the hierarchical relationship between the levels.  The pyramid is positioned in the right-hand side of the slide.  The pyramid is a visual representation of how a Gaussian pyramid works, showing the different levels of resolution and how they are related to each other.


**Description of what you see:**

This slide introduces the concept of Gaussian pyramids in image processing.  The text explains that a Gaussian pyramid represents an image as a series of progressively smaller and blurred versions.  The figure is a 3D representation of this pyramid structure.  The base of the pyramid represents the original image, and the top represents the smallest, most highly compressed version.  The levels in between represent intermediate resolutions.  The slide is designed to illustrate the hierarchical structure of a Gaussian pyramid and how it represents an image at different scales.  The source is credited at the bottom.

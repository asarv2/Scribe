# Slide 13

**Characterizing edges**

An edge is a place of rapid change in the image intensity function

**(Figure):**

The slide presents three panels arranged horizontally.

* **Panel 1 (left):**  A grayscale image is shown.  It's a simple, vertical bar graph-like image.  The left side of the image is light gray, transitioning to dark gray, and then to black on the right side. A horizontal, light red line is drawn across the image, roughly in the middle of the transition. This image represents a sample image with a sharp edge. The purpose of this image is to visually illustrate the concept of an edge as a point of rapid change in intensity.


* **Panel 2 (middle):** A graph is shown.  It's a line graph, with the x-axis representing position (or horizontal scanline) and the y-axis representing intensity. The graph shows a smooth curve, representing the intensity function along a horizontal scanline of the image. The curve gradually changes from light to dark, illustrating a gradual change in intensity. The purpose of this graph is to visually represent the intensity function along a horizontal scanline of the image.


* **Panel 3 (right):** Another graph is shown.  It's a line graph, with the x-axis representing position (or horizontal scanline) and the y-axis representing the first derivative of the intensity function. The graph shows a sharp peak (positive) and a sharp valley (negative) in the middle of the graph.  These peaks and valleys represent the extrema of the derivative, which correspond to the edges in the image. The purpose of this graph is to visually represent the first derivative of the intensity function, highlighting the extrema that correspond to edges.


**Description of what you see:**

This slide explains the concept of edges in image processing.  It uses a visual representation to show how edges correspond to rapid changes in intensity.  The left panel shows a sample image with a sharp edge. The middle panel shows the intensity function along a horizontal scanline, illustrating a gradual change in intensity. The right panel shows the first derivative of the intensity function, highlighting the extrema that correspond to the edges in the image.  The slide is designed to illustrate the mathematical relationship between an image, its intensity function, and its derivative, which can be used to detect edges.

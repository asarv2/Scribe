# Slide 23

**Corner Detection: Basic Idea**

We should easily recognize the point by looking through a small window
Shifting a window in any direction should give a large change in intensity

**(Figure):**

The slide displays three panels arranged horizontally.

* **Panel 1 (left):** A small, teal-colored square is shown within a larger, gray area.  Four white arrows emanate from the center of the square, pointing in the cardinal directions (north, south, east, and west).  The teal square is positioned in the center of a larger, darker gray area.  This panel represents a "flat region." The arrows indicate that shifting the window in any direction will not cause a significant change in intensity.


* **Panel 2 (middle):** A light-teal colored square is shown within a larger, gray area.  Two white arrows emanate from the center of the square, pointing vertically (up and down).  The teal square is positioned in the center of a larger, darker gray area.  This panel represents an "edge." The arrows indicate that shifting the window in the direction perpendicular to the edge will not cause a significant change in intensity.


* **Panel 3 (right):** A light-teal colored square is shown within a larger, gray area.  Four white arrows emanate from the center of the square, pointing in the cardinal directions (north, south, east, and west).  The teal square is positioned in the center of a larger, darker gray area.  This panel represents a "corner." The arrows indicate that shifting the window in any direction will cause a significant change in intensity.


**Description of what you see:**

This slide visually explains the concept of corner detection.  The three panels illustrate how the appearance of a small window (or patch) changes when it's moved across an image.  The "flat region" shows no change in intensity when the window is shifted.  The "edge" shows no change in intensity when the window is shifted along the edge direction.  The "corner" shows a significant change in intensity when the window is shifted in any direction.  The slide is designed to illustrate the key difference in appearance between flat regions, edges, and corners when a small window is moved across an image.  The source is credited at the bottom.

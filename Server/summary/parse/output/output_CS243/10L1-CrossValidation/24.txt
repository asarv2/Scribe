**When to get more data vs. use different model**

The slide presents three scenarios, each with a graph illustrating the relationship between model complexity and accuracy for both the training and test sets.  These graphs are used to determine whether to increase the amount of data or use a different model to improve the model's performance.

*   **Underfitting:**
    *   **Graph:** The graph shows two curves, one light blue (training set) and one red (test set). Both curves are relatively flat and low, indicating that the model is not learning effectively from the data.  The training accuracy is slightly higher than the test accuracy, but both are low.  The x-axis is labeled "Model Complexity," and the y-axis is labeled "Accuracy."  The graph is positioned in the leftmost section of the slide.  The text "Underfitting" is above the graph.  The text "Increase complexity of model" is below the graph.

*   **Overfitting:**
    *   **Graph:** The graph shows two curves, one light blue (training set) and one red (test set). The training accuracy curve is high and steep, while the test accuracy curve is high initially, peaks, and then decreases.  The x-axis is labeled "Model Complexity," and the y-axis is labeled "Accuracy."  The graph is positioned in the middle section of the slide.  The text "Overfitting" is above the graph.  The text "Get more data and/or regularize during learning" is below the graph.

*   **Just right:**
    *   **Graph:** The graph shows two curves, one light blue (training set) and one red (test set). Both curves are relatively high and reach a plateau at a similar level of accuracy.  The x-axis is labeled "Model Complexity," and the y-axis is labeled "Accuracy."  The graph is positioned in the rightmost section of the slide.  The text "Just right" is above the graph.  The text "Ideal performance that is almost never observed" is below the graph.


**Description:**

The slide uses graphs to visually represent the concepts of underfitting, overfitting, and a "just right" model.  The graphs illustrate how the accuracy of a model on both the training and test sets changes as the model's complexity increases.  The different shapes of the curves in each scenario help to visually distinguish between these three cases.  The text below each graph provides guidance on how to address the issues of underfitting and overfitting, suggesting either increasing model complexity or obtaining more data and/or regularization techniques.  The overall purpose of the slide is to help learners understand how to diagnose and address these common issues in machine learning model development.

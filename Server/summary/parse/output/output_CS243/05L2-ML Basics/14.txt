# What if I fit this with a degree-9 polynomial?

**(Figure):**

The slide displays three subplots, each showing a scatter plot of data points (blue circles) and two fitted lines.  The x-axis is labeled "x" and the y-axis is labeled "t" in all three plots.

* **Left Subplot (M = 1):**  A red straight line is fitted to the data points.  The line is a poor fit, as it does not closely follow the overall trend of the data.

* **Middle Subplot (M = 3):** A red straight line and a green curved line are fitted to the data points. The green curve is a better fit than the red line, but still doesn't perfectly capture the data's trend.

* **Right Subplot (M = 9):** A red straight line and a green highly complex curved line are fitted to the data points. The green curve is a very close fit to the data points, almost perfectly following the data's trend.  However, the green curve exhibits significant oscillations and fluctuations, suggesting overfitting.


**Context:**

This slide continues the discussion of curve fitting and the potential for overfitting.  The three subplots demonstrate how increasing the degree of the polynomial (from M=1 to M=9) can lead to a better fit to the training data, but also to overfitting.  The red line in each subplot represents a simple linear fit, while the green line represents a polynomial fit of increasing degree.  The blue circles represent the data points.  The slide is designed to illustrate the trade-off between model complexity and the ability to generalize to unseen data.


**Description:**

The slide is designed to illustrate the concept of overfitting in machine learning.  The three subplots show how a higher-degree polynomial can fit the training data very closely, but may not generalize well to new, unseen data.  The red line in each subplot represents a simple linear fit, while the green line represents a polynomial fit of increasing degree.  The blue circles represent the data points.  The slide is a crucial part of the lecture, as it helps to illustrate the concept of overfitting and the trade-off between model complexity and the ability to generalize to unseen data.

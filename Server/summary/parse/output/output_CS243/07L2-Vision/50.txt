# Slide 50

**Filters: Blur**

* **Original (f):**  A grayscale image of a portion of a person's face, likely an eye and surrounding area. The image is pixelated, showing distinct blocks of color.  This image is presented as the original image with potential noise.

* **Kernel (k):** A 3x3 matrix is shown, with each cell containing the number 1.  The matrix is enclosed in a teal/turquoise box.  This matrix represents the kernel or filter used for blurring.  The values in the kernel are all 1, indicating a mean filter.  The division by 9 at the top of the slide indicates that the kernel's values are normalized by 9.  This is a visual representation of the filter used to smooth the image.

* **Blur (with a mean filter) (g):** A grayscale image of the same portion of a person's face as the original image.  However, the image is noticeably smoother and less pixelated.  The image is presented as the result of applying the mean filter to the original image.  This image demonstrates the effect of the blur filter on the original image.

* **Source:** "Source: D. Lowe" is displayed at the bottom of the slide, indicating the source of the image or the figure.


**Description of what you see:**

This slide demonstrates the effect of a blur filter, specifically a mean filter, on an image.  The original image (f) shows a pixelated image, likely with noise.  The kernel (k) is a 3x3 matrix of 1s, representing the filter used to smooth the image.  The blurred image (g) shows the result of applying the mean filter to the original image.  The blurred image is smoother and less pixelated, visually demonstrating the noise reduction effect of the filter.  The slide is designed to illustrate the concept of image blurring using a mean filter.  The source is attributed to D. Lowe.

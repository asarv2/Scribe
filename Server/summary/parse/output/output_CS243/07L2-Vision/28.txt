**Texture recognition**

Texture is characterized by the repetition of basic elements or textons.

Many times, it is the identity of the textons, not their spatial arrangement, that matters.

**(Image Description):**

The slide displays three grayscale images of different textures.  These images are arranged in a 3-column, 1-row format.

* **Column 1 (Left):**  A grayscale image of a repeating pattern of small, circular shapes.  The circles are tightly packed, creating a dense texture.  This image represents a texture with a repeating, simple pattern.

* **Column 2 (Center):** A grayscale image of a repeating pattern of squares.  The squares are arranged in a grid-like pattern, creating a more structured texture.  This image represents a texture with a more complex, but still repeating, pattern.

* **Column 3 (Right):** A grayscale image of a repeating pattern that resembles a snake skin or scales.  The pattern is more irregular and organic than the previous two.  This image represents a texture with a more complex, irregular, and organic pattern.

Below the three main images, there are three small, grayscale images.  These are smaller versions of the basic elements (textons) that make up the larger textures.  The small images are arranged in a horizontal row, directly below the corresponding larger texture images.  The small images are positioned to visually represent the basic elements that create the larger textures.

**Satisfiability of a CNF formula**

**Notations**

* Let C<sub>1</sub>, ..., C<sub>m</sub> be clauses over a finite set V of Boolean variables P<sub>1</sub>, ..., P<sub>n</sub>.
* Define φ = C<sub>1</sub> ∧ ... ∧ C<sub>m</sub>
* Let v be an assignment on V.

**Observations**

* The CNF formula φ is satisfiable by v if, and only if, the clause C<sub>i</sub>, for all i ∈ {1, ..., m}, is satisfiable by v.
* A clause C<sub>i</sub>, for some i ∈ {1, ..., m}, is satisfiable by v if, and only if, at least one of its literals evaluates to true by v.

**Consequences**

* To check whether or not v satisfies φ, we may not need to know the truth values of all literals in all clauses.
* For instance, if v(p) = true and v(q) = false, we can see that the formula (p∨q∨¬r)∧(¬q∨s∨q) is satisfied without considering v(r) and v(s).


**Description:**

This slide defines the concept of satisfiability for a Conjunctive Normal Form (CNF) formula.  It introduces the notation and key concepts related to CNF formulas, clauses, and variable assignments.  The slide is structured to explain the logical conditions for a CNF formula to be satisfiable by a given variable assignment.  The example at the end illustrates how knowing the truth values of some variables can determine the satisfiability of the formula without needing to consider all variables.  The slide is clear and concise, providing a foundational definition for the topic.

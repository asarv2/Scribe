III. Small sample CI for population mean under normal distribution and unknown 𝜎<sup>2</sup>

Suppose 𝑋<sub>1</sub>, …, 𝑋<sub>𝑛</sub> are random samples with unknown mean 𝜇 and unknown variance 𝜎<sup>2</sup>.

As we studied, 
(𝑥̅ − μ) / (S/√n) ≈ Z holds and the CI for μ presented in earlier sections is valid when the sample size is sufficiently large. The resulting interval can be used whatever the nature of the population distribution.

The CLT cannot be invoked, however, when n is small. Statisticians derive the exact distribution of (𝑥̅ − μ) / (S/√n) when all 𝑋’s are normal. In this section, we will focus on a CI for a normal population distribution.

Assumption: the population of interest is normal, so that 𝑋<sub>1</sub>, …, 𝑋<sub>𝑛</sub> constitutes a random sample from a normal distribution with both 𝜇 and 𝜎 unknown.

The result on which inferences are based introduces a new family of probability distributions called t distributions.

Theorem: When 𝑥̅ is the mean of a random sample of size n from a normal distribution with mean 𝜇, the rv

𝑇 = (𝑥̅ − 𝜇) / (𝑆/√𝑛)

has a probability distribution called a student-t distribution with n – 1 degrees of freedom (df).

We know that a normal distribution is governed by two parameters; each different choice of 𝜇 in combination with 𝜎 gives a particular normal distribution. Any particular t distribution results from specifying the value of a single parameter, called the number of degrees of freedom, abbreviated df.

Proposition of t distribution: Let 𝑡<sub>𝑣</sub> denote the t distribution with 𝑣 df.

* It has a bell shape and centers at 0.
* It is more spread out than normal.
* The df controls the variability of t distribution.

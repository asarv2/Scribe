**Description of Slide 5:**

This slide, part of a lecture on Statistical Methods (STAT 511), continues the discussion of confidence intervals, focusing specifically on confidence intervals for population proportions (𝑝).  It builds upon the previous slides' discussion of confidence intervals for population means.  The slide presents different methods for constructing confidence intervals for a population proportion when the true proportion is unknown.  The slide introduces the concept of a Bernoulli sample and the sample proportion (𝑝̂).  It explains how the Central Limit Theorem (CLT) can be used to approximate the distribution of the sample proportion.  The slide details three approaches to calculating the confidence interval for the population proportion, each using different methods to estimate the variance of the sample proportion.  The formulas presented show how the sample proportion, critical value (z*), and sample size (n) are used to calculate the interval.  The slide also notes that for large sample sizes, the calculations simplify considerably.  There are no figures on this slide.

We will discuss 3 situations:

I. Large sample CI for population mean μ
II. Confidence Intervals for Proportions p
III. Small sample CI for population mean under normal distribution and unknown σ<sup>2</sup>


I. Large sample CI for population mean μ

Our CI formula in last section requires: 1) normality, 2) known standard deviation. CI for the population mean μ is,

(𝑥̅ − z∗∙σ/√n, 𝑥̅ + z∗∙σ/√n)

Now we consider if these conditions don’t hold. Suppose X<sub>1</sub>, …, X<sub>n</sub> are random sample with unknown mean μ and unknown variance σ<sup>2</sup>.

First of all, even they are not normal rv’s, by CLT, we still have the large sample approximation that

𝑥̅ ≈ N(μ, σ<sup>2</sup>/n) and  (𝑥̅−μ)/(σ/√n) ≈ Z

If furthermore, if we have a good estimation σ̂ for σ, we can plug in this estimator, then we obtain

(𝑥̅−μ)/(σ̂/√n) ≈ Z

One popular estimator for population standard deviation is sample standard deviation. Thus, it leads to the following large sample approximated confident interval with C confidence level:

(𝑥̅ − z∗∙S/√n, 𝑥̅ + z∗∙S/√n)


Note: Because S = √(1/(n-1) * Σ(X<sub>i</sub> − 𝑥̅)<sup>2</sup>), the margin of error here is a not fixed value, but data-dependent.

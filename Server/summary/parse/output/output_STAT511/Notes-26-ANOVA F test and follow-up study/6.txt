**Figure Description:**

The slide contains three figures, arranged horizontally.

1. **Boxplot (left):**  A boxplot is displayed, showing the distribution of a numerical variable (likely GPR) across three categories (e.g., smoking status: never smoked, former smoker, current smoker).  The boxes represent the interquartile range (IQR), the lines within the boxes represent the median, and the whiskers extend to the minimum and maximum values within 1.5 times the IQR.  Outliers are plotted as individual points.  The boxplots are side-by-side, allowing for a visual comparison of the distributions across the categories.  This is used to check for potential outliers and differences in the central tendency and spread of the data.

2. **Normal Q-Q Plot (middle):** A normal Q-Q plot is a scatter plot.  It plots the quantiles of a sample against the quantiles of a standard normal distribution.  If the data are normally distributed, the points on the plot will fall approximately along a straight line.  Deviations from a straight line indicate deviations from normality.  This plot is used to assess the normality assumption for the residuals in the ANOVA analysis.

3. **Boxplot (right):**  A boxplot is displayed, similar to the first boxplot, but it is likely a boxplot of the residuals from the ANOVA model.  This is used to check for outliers in the residuals, which is an important assumption for ANOVA.

**Sampling Distribution of the Sample Mean**

Yuan Qu

The previous simulation studies show that when *n* is sufficiently large, the sampling distribution of sample variance and sample mean is more and more normal. Actually, most of the statistics that are based on average have this property. We particularly study the sampling distribution of sample mean in this section.

Proposition: Let X<sub>1</sub>, X<sub>2</sub>, ..., X<sub>n</sub> be a random sample from a distribution with mean value μ and standard deviation σ. Then

1. E(X̄) = μ<sub>X̄</sub> = μ
2. Var(X̄) = σ<sub>X</sub><sup>2</sup>/n and σ<sub>X̄</sub> = σ<sub>X</sub>/√n

In addition, with T = X<sub>1</sub> + X<sub>2</sub> + ... + X<sub>n</sub>, the sample total: E(T) = nμ<sub>X</sub>, Var(T) = nσ<sub>X</sub><sup>2</sup> and σ<sub>T</sub> = √nσ<sub>X</sub>.

The standard deviation σ<sub>X̄</sub> = σ<sub>X</sub>/√n is often called the standard error of the mean; it describes the magnitude of a typical or representative deviation of the sample mean from the population mean.

Proposition: Let X<sub>1</sub>, X<sub>2</sub>, ..., X<sub>n</sub> be a random sample from a normal distribution with mean μ and standard deviation σ. Then for any *n*, X̄ is normally distributed (with mean μ and standard deviation σ/√n, as is T (with mean nμ and standard deviation √nσ).

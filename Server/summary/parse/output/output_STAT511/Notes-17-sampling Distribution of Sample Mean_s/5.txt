**Recall the fact that we can use normal to approximate a Binomial distribution.**

Yuan Qu

The CLT can be used to justify the normal approximation to the binomial distribution discussed earlier.

We know that a binomial variable X is the number of successes in a binomial experiment consisting of n independent success/failure trials with p = P(S) for any particular trial. Define a new rv X<sub>1</sub> by

X<sub>1</sub> = { 1, if the 1st trial results in a success
         0, if the 1st trial results in a failure

and define X<sub>2</sub>, X<sub>3</sub>, ..., X<sub>n</sub> analogously for the other n–1 trials. Each X<sub>i</sub> indicates whether or not there is a success on the corresponding trial.

When the X<sub>i</sub>’s are summed, an 1 is added for every Success that occurs and a 0 for every Failure, so X<sub>1</sub> + ... + X<sub>n</sub> = X. The sample mean of the X<sub>i</sub>’s is p̂ = X/n, the sample proportion of successes. That is, both X and p̂ are approximately normal when n is large.


Example 17.4 A poll is conducted in order to learn about the amount of support a candidate (say for president) has. Suppose 50% of the population support the candidate. Suppose the size of the sample/poll is n = 900 people. What is the probability that in the sample, the proportion of those supporting the candidate will be larger than 0.53 (i.e. proportion 0.5 of support in the population while the proportion in the sample is larger than 0.53)?

Let Y<sub>i</sub> = 1 if the i<sup>th</sup> sample supports the candidate and Y<sub>i</sub> = 0 if not. Then

T = Σ<sub>i=1</sub><sup>n</sup> Y<sub>i</sub> ~ Bi(n, p)

The proportion of supporters in the sample is the sample mean of Y<sub>i</sub>, that is,

p̂ = T/n = (1/n) Σ<sub>i=1</sub><sup>n</sup> Y<sub>i</sub> = Y̅

According to the CLT, the sample proportion is normally distributed, with a mean

μ<sub>p̂</sub> = E(p̂) = E(T/n) = (1/n)E(T) = (1/n) * np = p = 0.5

and standard deviation

σ<sub>p̂</sub> = √Var(p̂) = √Var(T/n) = √(1/n<sup>2</sup> * Var(T)) = √(1/n<sup>2</sup> * npq) = √(pq/n) = √(0.5 * 0.5 / 900) = 0.016

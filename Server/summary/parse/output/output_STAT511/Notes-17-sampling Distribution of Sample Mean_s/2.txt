**A very important statistical result: Central Limit Theorem (CLT)**

Let X<sub>1</sub>, X<sub>2</sub>, ..., X<sub>n</sub> be a random sample from a distribution with mean μ and variance σ<sup>2</sup>. Then if n is sufficiently large, X̄ has approximately a normal distribution with μ<sub>X̄</sub> = μ<sub>X</sub> and σ<sub>X̄</sub><sup>2</sup> = σ<sub>X</sub><sup>2</sup>/n, and T also has approximately a normal distribution with mean nμ<sub>X</sub> and variance nσ<sub>X</sub><sup>2</sup>. The larger the value of n, the better the approximation.

Note: if the underlying distribution is close to a normal density curve, then the approximation will be good even for a small n, whereas if it is far from being normal, then a large n will be required.

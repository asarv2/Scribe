Connection between CI and 2-sided test for population mean

Confidence interval is equivalent to the corresponding two-tail test.

Therefore, if

* the alternative hypothesis has a “≠” sign,
* the confidence level C and the significance level α add to 100%,

A level α two-sided significance test rejects H<sub>0</sub>: μ = μ<sub>0</sub> exactly when μ<sub>0</sub> falls outside a level 1 − α confidence interval for μ.


Example 23.5  Newspapers reported that within the county school corporation, the average time spent
per week by first graders doing HW is 50 minutes. Such claim started several discussions and before
taking any further steps the school corporation decided to initiate its own study.

A SRS of 40 first graders registered in that school corporation were selected, and their guardians were
asked to report the number of minutes per week that the student spent doing HW. The average
time/week out of that sample was 55 minutes/week. If σ=10 minutes/week, perform a significance test,
at the 10% significance level, to establish whether there is any evidence that the mean of the
population is different from 50 minutes/week.

n = 40, x̄ = 55, σ = 10, μ<sub>0</sub> = 50, α = 0.1

Hypothesis testing:

1. H<sub>0</sub>: μ = 50  H<sub>a</sub>: μ ≠50

2. z =  (x̄−μ<sub>0</sub>) / (σ/√n) = (55−50) / (10/√40) = 3.16

3. This is a two-sided hypothesis test: p-value = 2 × P(Z > |3.16|) = 2(0.0008) = 0.0016.

4. (1) P-value < α, so reject H<sub>0</sub>. (2) We have significant evidence that the population mean HW
time/week is not 50 minutes.


CI:
C = 1 −α = 0.9, z<sub>0.95</sub> = 1.645

margin of error = 1.645 × (10/√40) = 2.6

CI = (55 −2.6, 55 + 2.6) = (52.4, 57.6)

Test μ<sub>0</sub> = 50 ?

**Condition probability**

**Motivating example**
Suppose you want to calculate the probability a randomly chosen person is colorblind. In the US, 4.25% of the population is colorblind.
* P(colorblind) = 0.0425.
Now, suppose you have additional information. Namely, the person to be chosen is male – that means, we are restricting objects to a subset of the population.
* Since 8% of all men are colorblind, the probability has changed to 0.08.

When we have additional information, we must “condition” our probability based on this additional information or based on this sub-population. The probability adjusted for the additional information is called a conditional probability.
In this section, we examine how the information “an event B has occurred” affects the probability assigned to A.

**Notation**
* A = event of interest. P(A) as the original, or unconditional probability, of the event A.
* B = event that is known to occur. B is the “conditioning event.”
* In symbols this adjusted probability is written P(A|B). The vertical line in the formula immediately before B designates conditional probability.
* Wording: P(A|B) is called the conditional probability of A given that B has occurred.
* For our example, we’d say the probability a randomly chosen person is colorblind (A) given the person chosen is male (B) is 0.08.

**Definition**
Consider an equal probability case, for any two events A and B with P(B) > 0, the conditional probability of A given that B has occurred is defined by

P(A|B) = N(A∩B)/N(B) = N(A∩B)/N(B)/N

**General Formula:** When P(B) > 0, the conditional probability of A given B is

P(A|B) = P(A∩B) / P(B)

• A Venn diagram illuminates the relationship of the conditional probability and P(A ∩ B) and P(B).
• Given that B has occurred, the relevant sample space is no longer S, but consists of outcomes in B; A has occurred if and only if one of the outcomes in the intersection occurred, so the conditional probability of A given B is proportional to P(A ∩ B).
• The proportionality constant 1/P(B) is used to ensure that the probability P(B | B) of the new sample space B equals 1.

**Independence**

P(A|B) represents the conditional probability of A given that event B has occurred. Often the chance that A will occur or has occurred is not affected by knowledge that B has occurred, so that P(A|B) = P(A).

**Definition**

* Two events A and B are independent if P(A|B) = P(A)  or P(B|A) = P(B)
* Otherwise, they are dependent.

**Proposition**

* If A and B are independent, then A<sup>c</sup> and B are also independent, since P(A<sup>c</sup>|B) = 1 − P(A|B) = 1 − P(A) = P(A<sup>c</sup>)
* Because of the multiplication rule, this also implies that P(A ∩ B) = P(A|B) × P(B) = P(A) × P(B)

**Slide Description:**

This slide (slide 5) continues the discussion of hypothesis testing, focusing on the calculation of the test statistic and the p-value.  It introduces the concept of a large sample z-test, which is used when the sample size is large enough to assume the sampling distribution of the sample mean is approximately normal.  The slide provides a formula for the test statistic (Z<sub>TS</sub>) and explains how to calculate the p-value, which is the probability of observing a test statistic as extreme or more extreme than the one calculated from the sample data, assuming the null hypothesis is true.  The slide also includes a worked example (Example 21.7) demonstrating the application of the z-test to a real-world scenario involving sprinkler system activation temperatures.  The example clearly outlines the null and alternative hypotheses, the calculation of the test statistic, and the interpretation of the p-value in the context of rejecting or failing to reject the null hypothesis.  The slide is well-organized and provides a clear explanation of the concepts.  The formulas and calculations are presented in a clear and concise manner.  The example is relevant to the course's topic of statistical methods.

Nonnormal: strong non-linearity indicates a poor fit. Nonormal population distribution can often be placed in one of the following three categories:

1. It is symmetric and has “lighter tails” than does a normal distribution; that is, the density curve declines more rapidly out in the tails than does a normal curve.

    *Light-tailed data*
    *(Scatter plot:  A scatter plot of data points is shown. The points roughly follow a diagonal line, suggesting a possible normal distribution.)*

2. It is symmetric and heavy-tailed compared to a normal distribution.

    *Heavy-tailed data*
    *(Scatter plot: A scatter plot of data points is shown. The points deviate significantly from a straight line, forming a curve that resembles the letter "S," indicating a non-normal distribution.)*

3. It is left skewed.

    *(Plot: A plot is shown with a density curve (model) and a data curve. The data curve is skewed to the left, meaning the tail of the distribution extends further to the left than to the right.)*

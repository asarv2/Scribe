**Figure Descriptions:**

**Upper Left:**  A scatter plot, labeled "Points on the line," shows a series of plotted points. The points appear to roughly follow a diagonal line, suggesting a possible normal distribution. The x-axis is labeled "Points on the line," and the y-axis is labeled "Points on the line." The points are plotted in a way that suggests a linear relationship. The reason for this plot is to visually assess if the data points follow a straight line, which is a characteristic of a normal distribution.

**Upper Right:** A scatter plot, labeled "S" shape, shows a series of plotted points that deviate significantly from a straight line. The points form a curve that resembles the letter "S," indicating a non-normal distribution. The x-axis and y-axis are labeled similarly to the previous plot. The reason for this plot is to visually demonstrate a non-normal distribution.

**Upper Right (second plot):** A scatter plot, labeled "C" type shape, shows a series of plotted points that deviate significantly from a straight line. The points form a curve that resembles the letter "C," indicating a non-normal distribution. The x-axis and y-axis are labeled similarly to the previous plot. The reason for this plot is to visually demonstrate a non-normal distribution.

**Lower Left:** A histogram, labeled "A," displays a distribution of data values. The x-axis represents the data values, and the y-axis represents the frequency or count of data points in each bin. **Lower Right:** A scatter plot, labeled "Normally distributed data," shows a series of plotted points that closely follow a straight line.

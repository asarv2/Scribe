**Independence**

P(A|B) represents the conditional probability of A given that event B has occurred. Often the chance that A will occur or has occurred is not affected by knowledge that B has occurred, so that P(A|B) = P(A).

**Definition**

* Two events A and B are independent if P(A|B) = P(A)  or P(B|A) = P(B)
* Otherwise, they are dependent.

**Proposition**

* If A and B are independent, then A<sup>c</sup> and B are also independent, since P(A<sup>c</sup>|B) = 1 − P(A|B) = 1 − P(A) = P(A<sup>c</sup>)
* Because of the multiplication rule, this also implies that P(A ∩ B) = P(A|B) × P(B) = P(A) × P(B)

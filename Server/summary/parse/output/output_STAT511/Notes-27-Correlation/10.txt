**(Figure 1, Top Left):** A histogram is displayed. The x-axis is labeled "log10(mass)" and ranges from approximately -1 to 2.5. The y-axis is labeled "Frequency".  The histogram shows the distribution of log10(mass) values for the carnivore species.  This figure is used to visualize the distribution of the log-transformed body mass values.

**(Figure 2, Top Right):** A histogram is displayed. The x-axis is labeled "log10(abundance)" and ranges from approximately -0.5 to 3.5. The y-axis is labeled "Frequency". The histogram shows the distribution of log10(abundance) values for the carnivore species. This figure is used to visualize the distribution of the log-transformed abundance values.

**(Figure 3, Bottom Left):** A scatterplot is displayed. The x-axis is labeled "log10(mass)" and ranges from approximately -0.5 to 2.5. The y-axis is labeled "log10(abundance)" and ranges from approximately -0.5 to 3.5.  The scatterplot shows the relationship between the log-transformed body mass and the log-transformed abundance of the carnivore species.  This figure is used to visually assess the correlation between the log-transformed variables.  The points on the scatterplot generally trend downward, indicating a negative correlation.

**(Figure 4, Bottom Right):**  This is identical to Figure 3, a scatterplot showing the relationship between log10(mass) and log10(abundance).  The placement and purpose are the same as Figure 3.


**Text Content:**

This scatterplot shows a negative association. That is, bigger carnivores are less abundant. The form of the association between the log of carnivore body mass and the log of prey abundance is linear. The association is quite strong because the points don't deviate a great deal from the line. It is striking that animals from many different parts of the world should fit so simple a pattern.


**Description:**

This slide continues the analysis of the relationship between carnivore body mass and abundance, focusing on the log-transformed variables.  It presents histograms of the log-transformed data and a scatterplot showing the relationship between the log-transformed variables.  The text describes the observed negative association and the strength of the linear relationship.  The slide is designed to demonstrate the use of transformations (log transformations) to improve the linearity of the relationship between variables, a common practice in statistical analysis.

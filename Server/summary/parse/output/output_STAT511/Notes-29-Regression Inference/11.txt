**Data doesn't have equal variances**

In the example below, the responses for each X value are normal BUT the variances increase as X increases. As a result, the constant variance assumption is violated.

**(Figure 2: Normal P-P Plot)**

*   **Orientation:** Horizontal.
*   **Location:** Upper-center portion of the slide.
*   **Description:** A normal probability plot (P-P plot) of the residuals. The points are plotted against their expected values under normality. The points deviate significantly from a straight line, suggesting that the residuals are not normally distributed. This plot is used to check the assumption of normality in the regression model. The deviation from a straight line indicates a violation of the normality assumption.


**(Figure 3: Scatter Plot)**

*   **Orientation:** Horizontal.
*   **Location:** Upper-right portion of the slide.
*   **Description:** A scatter plot with points plotted on a graph. The x-axis is labeled "Regression Standardized Predicted Value" and the y-axis is labeled "Dependent Variable: Y". The points appear to form a wedge shape, with the spread of the points increasing as the fitted values increase. This plot is used to check the assumption of constant variance (homoscedasticity) in the regression model. The wedge shape indicates a violation of the constant variance assumption.


**(Figure 4: Scatter Plot)**

*   **Orientation:** Horizontal.
*   **Location:** Lower-left portion of the slide.
*   **Description:** A scatter plot showing the relationship between two variables. The x-axis is labeled "x" and the y-axis is labeled "y". The points appear to be scattered randomly, but the spread of the points increases as the x-values increase. This plot is used to illustrate a case where the model assumptions are violated.

**Description:**

This slide continues the theme of model diagnostics in linear regression. It focuses on illustrating cases where the assumptions of normality and constant variance are violated.  The figures are crucial for visually demonstrating how these violations manifest in the data.  The slide uses examples to show how non-constant variance can appear in a scatter plot and how a normal probability plot can reveal non-normality.  The slide is designed to help students recognize these violations when analyzing data.

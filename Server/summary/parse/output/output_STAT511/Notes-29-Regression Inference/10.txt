**Figures:**

**Figure 1 (Scatter Plot):**

*   **Orientation:** Horizontal.
*   **Location:** Lower-left portion of the slide.
*   **Description:** A scatter plot showing the relationship between two variables. The x-axis is labeled "Days after flowering" and the y-axis is labeled "Response". The points appear to be scattered randomly, suggesting a possible linear relationship between the variables. This plot is used to check the assumption of linearity in the regression model.


**Figure 2 (NQ Plot):**

*   **Orientation:** Horizontal.
*   **Location:** Center-left portion of the slide.
*   **Description:** A normal probability plot (NQ plot) of the residuals. The points are plotted against their expected values under normality.  The points roughly follow a straight line, suggesting that the residuals are approximately normally distributed. This plot is used to check the assumption of normality in the regression model.


**Figure 3 (Residual Plot):**

*   **Orientation:** Horizontal.
*   **Location:** Center-right portion of the slide.
*   **Description:** A scatter plot of residuals versus the fitted values. The x-axis is labeled "Regression Standardized Predicted Value" and the y-axis is labeled "Dependent Variable: Y". The points appear to be randomly scattered around a horizontal line near zero, indicating that the variance of the residuals is constant across the range of fitted values. This plot is used to check the assumption of equal variances (homoscedasticity) in the regression model.


**Figure 4 (Scatter Plot - Data Not Linear):**

*   **Orientation:** Horizontal.
*   **Location:** Lower-right portion of the slide.
*   **Description:** A scatter plot showing the relationship between two variables. The x-axis is labeled "Days after flowering" and the y-axis is labeled "Response". The points do not appear to follow a linear pattern, suggesting a lack of linearity in the relationship between the variables. This plot is used to illustrate a case where the model assumptions are violated.


**Description of the Slide:**

This slide continues the discussion of model diagnostics in linear regression. It focuses on visually inspecting residual plots to assess the assumptions of linearity, normality, and equal variances.  The slide presents examples of plots that satisfy these assumptions (Example 29.3) and plots that violate the assumption of linearity (Example 29.4).  The figures are crucial for understanding how to interpret residual plots and identify potential problems with the model.  The slide also provides guidance on how to check the assumptions of independence and outliers.

**Hypothesis tests about the one-sample population mean 8.2-8.3 (continued)**

Let X<sub>1</sub>, ..., X<sub>n</sub> represent a random sample of size n from some population.

* Null hypothesis H<sub>0</sub>: μ = μ<sub>0</sub>, where μ<sub>0</sub> is called null value.
* Three possible alternative hypotheses
    1. H<sub>a</sub>: μ > μ<sub>0</sub>, the population mean is greater than μ<sub>0</sub>
    2. H<sub>a</sub>: μ < μ<sub>0</sub>, the population mean is less than μ<sub>0</sub>
    3. H<sub>a</sub>: μ ≠ μ<sub>0</sub>, the population mean is not μ<sub>0</sub>

**Case 2: Normal distribution with unknown variance**

If X's follow normal distribution 𝑁(𝜇<sub>0</sub>,𝜎<sup>2</sup>) (i.e., the null hypothesis is true), then  
$\frac{\bar{X} - \mu_0}{s/\sqrt{n}}$ follows a t distribution with df n-1. Thus this statistic can be used for hypothesis testing, since it has a tractable sampling distribution under H<sub>0</sub>.

Test statistic:
TS = $\frac{\bar{X} - \mu_0}{s/\sqrt{n}}$ ~ t(n-1)

When H<sub>0</sub> is true, the test statistic has a t distribution with n – 1 degrees of freedom.

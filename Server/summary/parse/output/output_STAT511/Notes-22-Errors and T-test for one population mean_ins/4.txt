**Test procedure and calculation of type I and type II error probabilities (continued)**

**b.** Let 𝑋̅ denote the sample average compressive strength for 𝑛=20 randomly selected specimens. Consider a test procedure that rejects 𝐻<sub>0</sub> when 𝑋̅ ≥ 1331.26. What is the probability distribution of the test statistic when 𝐻<sub>0</sub> is true? What is the probability of a type I error for the test procedure?

𝑋̅ is normally distributed with mean 𝐸(𝑋̅) = 𝜇 and standard deviation  𝜎/√𝑛 = 60/√20 = 13.416. When 𝐻<sub>0</sub> is true, 𝐸(𝑋̅) = 1300. Thus,

𝛼 = 𝑃(𝑋̅ ≥ 1331.26  when 𝐻<sub>0</sub> is true)

= 𝑃(𝑍 ≥ (1331.26−1300)/13.416) = 𝑃(𝑍 ≥ 2.33) = 0.01

**c.** What is the probability distribution of the test statistic when µ=1350? Using the test procedure of part (b), what is the probability that the mixture will be judged unsatisfactory when in fact µ=1350 (a type II error)?

When µ = 1350, 𝑋̅ has a normal distribution with mean 1350 and standard deviation 13.416, so

𝛽(1350) = 𝑃(𝑋̅ < 1331.26  when µ=1350)

= 𝑃(𝑍 ≤ (1331.26−1350)/13.416) = 𝑃(𝑍 ≤ −1.40) = 0.0808


**Example 22.3** (Another example of calculating Type II error) The true average voltage drop from collector to emitter of insulated gate bipolar transistors of a certain type is supposed to be at most 2.5 volts. Consider a hypothesis test 𝐻<sub>0</sub>: µ = 2.5 versus 𝐻<sub>𝑎</sub>: µ > 2.5. An investigator selects a sample of 𝑛 = 10 such transistors to test the hypotheses. Assume normal distribution and the standard deviation of the voltage distribution is σ = 0.100. Let significance level α=0.05. How likely is it that 𝐻<sub>0</sub> will not be rejected when in fact µ = 2.6?

Since the distribution is normal and σ = 0.100 is known, we use a 𝑧-test for the hypotheses.

With α=0.05, the rejection region is

{𝑍 > 1.64} = { (𝑋̅−2.5)/0.100/√10 > 1.64} or {𝑋̅ > 2.5 + 1.64 * 0.100/√10}.

The probability of Type II error, or β is

𝛽(2.6) = 𝑃(𝑋̅ < 2.5 + 1.64 * 0.100/√10 | µ=2.6) = 𝑃((𝑋̅ −2.6)/0.100/√10 < 1.64 −√10)

It is 𝑃(𝑍 < −1.52) = 0.064.

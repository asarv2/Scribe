Definition: the rv’s X<sub>1</sub>, X<sub>2</sub>, ..., X<sub>n</sub> are said to form a (simple) random sample of size n if

1. The X<sub>i</sub>’s are independent rv’s.

2. Every X<sub>i</sub> has the same probability distribution.

In such case, we say X<sub>1</sub>, X<sub>2</sub>, ..., X<sub>n</sub> are independent and identically distributed (iid).


Note: this assumption of random sample provides a joint distribution for the sequence of X<sub>1</sub>, X<sub>2</sub>, ..., X<sub>n</sub>, and further defines a distribution of a statistic (a random variable).

**Description of the Slide:**

This is slide 2 of a 4-part lecture on Statistical Methods, specifically focusing on sampling distributions.  The slide introduces the concept of independent and identically distributed (iid) random variables, which are crucial for defining a random sample.  It then presents an example (Example 16.2) of tossing a die twice, where X<sub>1</sub> and X<sub>2</sub> represent the outcomes of the two tosses.  The slide asks two questions:

1. **Distribution of T = X<sub>1</sub> + X<sub>2</sub>:**  This part of the slide lays out the framework for calculating the probability mass function (pmf) of the sum of the two random variables.  It shows the table structure needed to calculate the pmf, but the actual probabilities are not filled in.  This is a key step in understanding how the sum of random variables behaves.

2. **Distribution of X̅:** This part of the slide introduces the concept of the sample mean (X̅) and asks for its distribution.  The table is set up to calculate the pmf of the sample mean, but again, the probabilities are missing.

The tables are crucial for understanding the sampling distribution of the sum and the sample mean.  The slide is designed to guide the student through the process of calculating these distributions, which is a fundamental concept in statistical inference.  The missing values in the tables are likely to be filled in during the lecture or as part of a subsequent exercise.

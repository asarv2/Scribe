**Yuan Qu**

* The sample space can be classified into two types:
    * Countable - If the elements of a sample space can be put into 1-to-1 correspondence with a subset of integers, the sample space is countable.
    * Uncountable - Otherwise, it is uncountable.

* Event – Any subset of outcomes contained in S, including S itself and empty set.
    * Simple event – An event that consists of one and only one outcome of S.
    * Compound event – An event that consists of more than one outcome of S.
    * Example: if the experiment consists of tossing THREE coins, the sample space contains all possible combinations of heads and tails, S ={ HHH, HHT, HTH, HTT, THH, THT, TTH, TTT }.
    * Event A={ HHH } means exactly three Heads occur. Simple or compound?
    * Event B={ HHH, HHT } means HHH or HHT occurs, in other words, the first two are both Heads. Simple or compound?

**Set operations on events**

* The complement of event A,
    * Denoted Ac or A’. Ac = S \ A.
    * The event consists of all outcomes that are NOT included in the outcomes of event A.

* The intersection of two events A and B,
    * Denoted 𝐴∩𝐵.
    * The event that both A AND B occur.

* The union of two events A and B
    * Denoted 𝐴∪𝐵.
    * The event that either event A OR B occurs.

**Description of the Slide:**

This slide, part of a lecture on Statistical Methods, continues the discussion of probability concepts.  It builds on the previous slide by defining and illustrating different types of events (simple and compound) and introducing set operations on events (complement, intersection, and union).  The example (Example 4.1) demonstrates how to apply these operations to specific sets.  The slide uses the notation S to represent the sample space, and the empty sets are left blank for the student to fill in the results of the set operations.  The example of tossing three coins is used to illustrate the concept of sample space and events.  The slide is organized logically, progressing from definitions to examples, and is designed to help students understand the fundamental concepts of probability.

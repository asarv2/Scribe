**Example 25.1 revisit** With 𝜇𝑖 denoting the true average compression strength for boxes of type 𝑖 (𝑖 = 1, 2, 3, 4), the null hypothesis is

𝐻0: 𝜇1 = 𝜇2 = 𝜇3 = 𝜇4

There is a substantial amount of overlap among observations on the first three types of boxes, but compression strengths for the fourth type appear considerably smaller than for the other types. This suggests that 𝐻0 is not true.

Can we just use the independent samples t test 6 times to test the following alternative hypotheses?

𝐻𝑎: 𝜇1 ≠ 𝜇2
𝐻𝑎: 𝜇1 ≠ 𝜇3
𝐻𝑎: 𝜇1 ≠ 𝜇4
𝐻𝑎: 𝜇2 ≠ 𝜇3
𝐻𝑎: 𝜇2 ≠ 𝜇4
𝐻𝑎: 𝜇3 ≠ 𝜇4

ANSWER IS NO: Because if 𝐻0 is true for each of the 6 different hypotheses given above (that is if there is no association between the type of shipping boxes and compression strength) then we’d have much more than 6 chances to make a overall type I error (the probability of at least one false rejection among 6 tests).

In general, we have “𝐼” groups that we wish to compare, then we could do 𝑘 = 𝐼(𝐼−1)/2 two-sample t tests. The overall type I error rate is

𝑃(𝑎𝑡 𝑙𝑒𝑎𝑠𝑡 1 𝑡𝑒𝑠𝑡 𝑐𝑜𝑚𝑚𝑖𝑡𝑠 𝑇𝑦𝑝𝑒 𝐼 𝑒𝑟𝑟𝑜𝑟) = 1 −(1 −𝛼)𝑘

| 𝐼 | 𝑘 | α = 0.05 | α = 0.01 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 1 | 0.05 | 0.01 |
| 3 | 3 | 0.1426 | 0.0297 |
| 4 | 6 | 0.2649 | 0.0685 |
| 5 | 10 | 0.4013 | 0.0956 |
| 6 | 15 | 0.5367 | 0.1399 |

It’s necessary to reduce the number of tests.

**Hypotheses:**

H<sub>0</sub>: there is no relationship between “type of shipping boxes” and “compression strength”; or
H<sub>0</sub>: group index has nothing to do with the mean of “compression strength”; or
H<sub>0</sub>: 𝜇<sub>1</sub> = . . . = 𝜇<sub>𝐼</sub> (constant)

H<sub>a</sub>: “type of shipping boxes” does influence the group mean of “compression strength”; or
H<sub>a</sub>: There are at least two groups that their group means are different; or
H<sub>a</sub>: 𝜇<sub>1</sub> = . . . = 𝜇<sub>𝐼</sub> doesn’t hold.

Note: H<sub>a</sub> is not the same statement as saying “all the group means are different.”


**Intuition of ANOVA: Sum of Square (variability) decomposition**

```
(𝑋<sub>𝑖𝑗</sub>−𝑋̅<sub>∙∙</sub>)       =        (𝑋̅<sub>𝑖∙</sub>−𝑋̅<sub>∙∙</sub>)        +        (𝑋<sub>𝑖𝑗</sub>−𝑋̅<sub>𝑖∙</sub>) 
```

```
total           =           between-group           +           within-group 
deviation       deviation           deviation 
```

Of course, the individual components 𝑋<sub>𝑖𝑗</sub>−𝑋̅<sub>∙∙</sub> sum to 0, so we must square them. It turns out that all cross-product terms cancel, and we have:

```
∑<sub>𝑖=1</sub><sup>𝐼</sup>∑<sub>𝑗=1</sub><sup>𝐽</sup>(𝑋<sub>𝑖𝑗</sub>−𝑋̅<sub>⋅⋅</sub>)<sup>2</sup> = ∑<sub>𝑖=1</sub><sup>𝐼</sup>∑<sub>𝑗=1</sub><sup>𝐽</sup>(𝑋<sub>𝑖𝑗</sub>−𝑋̅<sub>𝑖⋅</sub>)<sup>2</sup> + ∑<sub>𝑖=1</sub><sup>𝐼</sup>𝐽(𝑋̅<sub>𝑖⋅</sub>−𝑋̅<sub>⋅⋅</sub>)<sup>2</sup>
```

Total sum of square (SST) = treatment sum of square (SSTr) + error sum of squre (SSE)

Total variation in data = variation within each group + variation among groups

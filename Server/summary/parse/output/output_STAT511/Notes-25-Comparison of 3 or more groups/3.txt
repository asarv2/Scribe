**Comparing means of 3 or more groups (ANOVA)**

In previous chapter, we carry out the comparison between the means/proportions of two population means. Now we further extend the study to a comparison of more than two population or treatment means.

**Single Factor ANOVA**

Example 25.1 Consider an experiment in which several different types of shipping boxes were compared with respect to compression strength (lb).

| Type of Box | Compression Strength (lb) | Sample Mean | Sample SD |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 655.5 788.3 734.3 721.4 679.1 699.4 | 713.00 | 46.55 |
| 2 | 789.2 772.5 786.9 686.1 732.1 774.8 | 756.93 | 40.34 |
| 3 | 737.1 639.0 696.3 671.7 717.2 727.1 | 698.07 | 37.20 |
| 4 | 535.1 628.7 542.4 559.0 586.9 520.0 | 562.02 | 39.87 |

**How many groups are we comparing?**

1. Two variables are of interests:
    * **Explanatory:** Type of shipping boxes – categorical
        * A categorical explanatory variable is also called a factor.
        * The values that a factor can take are levels (Type 1, 2, 3, 4).
        * The combination of levels of factors are treatments (Treatment 1, 2, 3, 4).
    * **Response:** compression strength – numerical

We are interested in the relationship between the explanatory and response variable.

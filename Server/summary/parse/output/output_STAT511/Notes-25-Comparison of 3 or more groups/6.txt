**Figure Description:**

Below the text, there are two side-by-side boxplots, labeled (a) and (b).  Both boxplots display three boxes, each representing a different group (labeled "Center 1," "Center 2," and "Center 3").  The boxes are positioned horizontally along a number line.  Each box represents the distribution of values for that group.  The box itself shows the interquartile range (IQR), with a line inside representing the median.  Whiskers extend from the box to show the range of the data, excluding outliers.  The horizontal axis is labeled with values.  Boxplot (a) shows more spread in the data than boxplot (b).  The boxplots are positioned below the text to visually compare the distributions of the data for each group in the two scenarios.  The presence of these boxplots is crucial for understanding the concept of comparing means of multiple groups in the context of ANOVA.  The comparison of the spread in the boxplots (a) and (b) is used to illustrate the concept of between-group and within-group variation, which is central to the F-test in ANOVA.

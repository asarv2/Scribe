* The total sum of squares (SST) is a measure of the total variation in the data—the sum of all squared deviations about the grand mean. This total variation can be partitioned into two pieces.

* The error sum of squares (SSE) measures variation that would be present whether H<sub>0</sub> is true or false.

* The treatment sum of squares (SSTr) is the amount of variation that can be explained by possible differences in the 𝜇<sub>i</sub>’s. H<sub>0</sub> is rejected if the explained variation is large relative to unexplained variation.

* Fundamental Identity: SST = SSTr + SSE

* The significance of group difference depends on the comparison of between-group-variation (SSTr) vs within-group-variation (SSE)

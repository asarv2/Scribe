**Example 8.8** Consider a university having 15,000 students and let X = number of courses for which a randomly selected student is registered. The pmf of X follows:

| X | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| p(x) | 0.01 | 0.03 | 0.13 | 0.25 | 0.39 | 0.17 | 0.02 |
| Number registered | 150 | 450 | 1950 | 3750 | 5850 | 2550 | 300 |

μ = 1 ∙ p(1) + 2 ∙ p(2) + ... + 7 ∙ p(7)

If we think of the population as consisting of the X values 1, 2, ..., 7, then μ = 4.57 is the population mean.

**Example 8.9** Let X be the number of children born up to and including the first boy. Assume p is the probability of having a boy in each birth, then p(x) = (1 - p)<sup>x-1</sup> ∙ p for all positive integer x. Then the mean is E(X) = Σ x ∙ (1 - p)<sup>x-1</sup> ∙ p = 1/p

**NOTE:** one needs to be careful about infinite summation.

Sometimes interest will focus on the expected value of some function h(X) rather than on just E(X).
Define a new r.v. Y = h(X), then
E[h(X)] = E(Y) = Σ y ∙ P(Y = y) = Σ y ∙ P(X ∈ {x: h(x) = y}) = Σ h(x) ∙ p(x).

**Proposition:** the expected value of a function of discrete r.v. X is E[h(X)] = Σ h(x) ∙ p(x).

**Example 8.1 revisit:** Consider also the experiment of tossing a fair coin three times independently. Define the random variable X to be the number of heads obtained in the three tosses.

| x | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| p(x) | 1/8 | 3/8 | 3/8 | 1/8 |

What is E(3 - X)?

**Proposition:** E(a ∙ X + b) = a ∙ E(X) + b. (Or, using alternative notation, μ<sub>aX+b</sub> = a ∙ μ<sub>X</sub> + b)

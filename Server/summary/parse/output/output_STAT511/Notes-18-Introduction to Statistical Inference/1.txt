# Slide 1

**Toward Statistical Inference**

Remember: Our final aim is to estimate the value of a parameter from the entire population.

We can think of the true value of the population parameter as the bull's-eye on a target and of the variable/sample statistic as an arrow fired at the target. Bias and variability describe what happens when we take many shots at the target.

* Points on the target: (𝑋<sub>1</sub>, 𝑋<sub>2</sub>, … , 𝑋<sub>𝑛</sub>) or (𝑋̅<sub>1</sub>, 𝑋̅<sub>2</sub>, … , 𝑋̅<sub>𝑛</sub>)
* What do we observe?

Variability: response varies with each unit.

* Observations can be erroneous compared to “true” value.
* Observations can be imprecise.


Definition: Bias concerns the center of the sampling distribution.

A statistic used to estimate a parameter is unbiased if the mean of its sampling distribution is equal to the true value of the parameter being estimated. It is on target!


Definition: The variability of a statistic is described by the spread of its sampling distribution.

This spread is determined by the sampling design and the sample size n.
Statistics from larger probability samples have smaller spreads.

According to CLT, the variability of 𝑋̅ is proportional to 𝑛<sup>−1/2</sup>.

**Description of the Slide:**

This slide introduces the concept of statistical inference, specifically focusing on bias and variability in estimating population parameters.  The slide uses an analogy of a target and arrows to illustrate the concept.  The text explains that the true value of the parameter is the bullseye, and the sample statistic is like an arrow shot at the target.  The variability of the shots (arrows) around the bullseye represents the variability of the sample statistic.

Three figures are presented, each showing a target with various dots representing sample statistics.  The figures illustrate different scenarios:

* **Figure 1 (top):** Shows a cluster of dots centered around the bullseye. This represents an unbiased and precise estimate.
* **Figure 2 (middle left):** Shows a cluster of dots significantly off-center from the bullseye. This represents a biased estimate.
* **Figure 3 (middle right):** Shows a cluster of dots spread out around the bullseye, but centered on it. This represents an unbiased but imprecise estimate.

The figures are crucial for understanding the concepts of bias (centeredness) and variability (spread) of the sampling distribution.  The figures are placed strategically to visually demonstrate the difference between unbiased/biased and precise/imprecise estimates.  The labels "Unbiased," "Biased," "Precise," and "Imprecise" are used to clearly identify the characteristics of each scenario.  The text accompanying the figures further explains the definitions of bias and variability.

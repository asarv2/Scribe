**Example 14.5** When a certain method is used to collect a fixed volume of rock samples in a region, there are four resulting rock types. Let X₁, X₂, and X₃ denote the proportion by volume of rock types 1, 2, and 3 in a randomly selected sample (the proportion of rock type 4 is 1 − X₁ − X₂ − X₃, so a variable X₄ could be redundant). If the joint pdf of X₁, X₂, and X₃ is

𝑓(𝑥₁, 𝑥₂, 𝑥₃) = {𝑘𝑥₁𝑥₂(1 − 𝑥₃),  0 ≤ 𝑥₁, 𝑥₂, 𝑥₃ ≤ 1, 𝑥₁ + 𝑥₂ + 𝑥₃ ≤ 1
              0,                 otherwise


1) Verify/find k so that it is a valid joint pdf:
𝑓(𝑥₁, 𝑥₂, 𝑥₃) ≥ 0
and
∫<sub>0</sub><sup>1</sup>∫<sub>0</sub><sup>1−𝑥₁</sup>∫<sub>0</sub><sup>1−𝑥₁−𝑥₂</sup> 𝑘𝑥₁𝑥₂(1 − 𝑥₃) 𝑑𝑥₃ 𝑑𝑥₂ 𝑑𝑥₁ = 1
→ 𝑘 = 144


2) What is the probability that rocks of types 1 and 2 together account for at most 50% of the sample, that is, 𝑃(𝑋₁ + 𝑋₂ < 0.5)?
𝐴 = {0 ≤ 𝑥₁, 𝑥₂, 𝑥₃ ≤ 1, 𝑥₁ + 𝑥₂ + 𝑥₃ ≤ 1, 𝑥₁ + 𝑥₂ ≤ 0.5}

Independence among events: Probability of intersection = product of probabilities of each event.
Independence among rv's: Joint Distribution = product of marginal distributions.

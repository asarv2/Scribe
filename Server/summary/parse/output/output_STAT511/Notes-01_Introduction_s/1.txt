**Introduction to Statistics**

**Statistics: The Art and Science of Learning from Data**

**What is Statistics?**

* Questions Statistics Can Answer
    * Do people who eat a high fiber cereal for breakfast, on average, weight less that people who skip breakfast?

**Four Main Phases of Statistical Analysis:**

1. The Research Question - Any question about a population or process.
    * Example: is duct tape better than freezing with dry ice at making warts go away?

2. Design of the study and data collection – Plan for obtaining and analyzing the data to be used to answer the questions of interest.
    * First decide how to get people for the study and then decide what to measure on them.
    * In this study there were 51 volunteers, all with warts and all relatively young. They were randomly assigned to one of two treatments: Duct tape (treatment 1) or freezing with dry ice (treatment 2). After a reasonable amount of time, each subject was examined to determine whether they still had warts.

3. Descriptive statistics – Summaries of the data – both graphical and numerical summaries.
    * Of the 26 patients with duct tape, 85% got rid of their warts compared to only 60% of the patients whose warts were frozen.

4. Conclusions and Inferences – Inferences about a population or process based on the data..
    * In this study, it was concluded that there was strong statistical evidence that for all people (not just those in the study) duct tape will be more effective than freezing in removing warts.
    * Above, we inferred that the outcome in the sample generalizes to the general population of people. We can make this inference because getting a similar result with another 51 people is very unlikely if duct tape isn’t better at getting rid of warts.

**KEY POINT 1:** The focus of this course is on data analysis but not the research question, the design of study and data collection.

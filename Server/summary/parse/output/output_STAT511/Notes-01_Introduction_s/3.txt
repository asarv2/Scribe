**Parameter and Statistic**

* **Parameter:** A parameter is a numerical summary of the variable values taken by all the members of the population.

* **Statistic:** A statistic is a numerical summary of the variable values taken by the subjects in the study.  So, unlike a parameter whose value doesn't change (assuming a stable population), the value of a statistic is variable because its value depends upon which subjects are chosen for the study. A statistic is used to estimate a parameter value.

**Example:**

Parameter: proportion of all current Purdue students who regularly smoke.

Statistic: proportion of these 100 students who are regular smokers.

**Statistical Inference:** A statistical inference is a statement about a population based on measurements of a subset of the population.

**KEY POINT 2:** The subjects in the sample were selected from the population, and so the population is made up of similar individuals.

**KEY POINT 3:** The larger the sample size, the more information you have. The more information you have, the more willing people are to trust your results.

**KEY POINT 4:** Statistics concerns properties of populations. We cannot use statistics to make predictions about particular individuals.

**Learning Goals:**

* **Describe data:**
    * Recognize which charts, graphs, and statistics are appropriate for a given data set.
    * Learn how to interpret these graphs and statistics.
* **Understanding the role of chance in statistics:**
    * What we measure (variable values) varies between individuals in a population. We use chance to choose our subjects for the study and we get a sample that is representative of the population.
    * The consequence of this is that the statistic we calculate from our sample is based on chance. If we'd chosen another sample, we could get a different value for our statistic.
* **Make inferences about the population:**
    * Learn how statisticians have devised a non-subjective way for making inferences about a population based on data. Learn how statisticians take chance into account when they make inferences.
    * Learn how to make your own inferences about populations based on data.

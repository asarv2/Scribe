# Slide 1

**Random Variables**

In set 4-7 of the notes, we study the basic probability theory, where the outcome of an experiment can be anything. In order to make analytical analysis, one needs to transform the outcome of an experiment to numerical values.

**NOTE:** Categorical variables can also be represented by binary values.

**Random variable:**

* For a given sample space S of some experiment, a random variable (r.v.) is any rule that associates a number with each outcome in S. In mathematical language, a random variable is a function whose domain is the sample space and whose range is the set of real numbers.
* We usually let X, Y or Z stand for the random variables, lowercase letters x, y, or z denote the particular values of the corresponding random variables.

**Example 8.1** Consider the experiment of tossing a fair coin three times independently. Define the random variable X to be the number of heads obtained in the three tosses. A complete enumeration of the value of X for each point s in the sample space S is:

| s | HHH | HHT | HTH | THH | TTH | THT | HTT | TTT |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| X(s) | 3 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 0 |


**Example 8.2** Consider the experiment that we toss coin until first head shows up. Define the random variable to be the number of Tails before the first Head shows up.

| s | H | TH | TTH | TTTH | TTTTH | ... |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| X(s) | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | ... |


**Example 8.3** Consider the experiment of tossing a fair dice two times independently. Define the random variable X to be the summation of the two values.

**Example 8.4** Consider the experiment where we measure the chemical reaction time. We can define a random variable by an identity function.

**Two basic types of Random variable:**

* **Discrete** is a r.v. whose possible values are countable.
* **Continuous** is a r.v. whose possible values consists either of all numbers in a single interval on the number line or all numbers in a disjoint union of such intervals, and no possible value of the variable has positive probability, that is, P(X = c) = 0 for all possible c.

**Probability distribution of a discrete random variable**

**Terminology and Notation:**

* Capital letters, such as X, are used to represent random variables.
* Lower case letters, like x, refer to value taken by the random variable.

**Definition:**

For any real value x:

P(X = x) = P({s: X(s) = x}) = P(all s ∈ S: X(s) = x).

For any set B of real values:

P(X ∈ B) = P({s: X(s) ∈ B}) = P(all s ∈ S: X(s) ∈ B) = ΣP(X = x).

The probability mass function (pmf) of a discrete variable is defined as p(x) = P(X = x).

**NOTE:** The probability distribution of discrete r.v. also satisfies all the axioms.

**Example 8.1 revisit:** Consider also the experiment of tossing a fair coin three times independently. Define the random variable X to be the number of heads obtained in the three tosses. The r.v. is:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| X(s) | 3 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 0 |

The pmf is:

| x | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| p(x) | 1/8 | 3/8 | 3/8 | 1/8 |


**Example 8.2 revisit:** Consider the experiment that we toss coin until first head shows up. Define the random variable to be the number of Tails before the first Head shows up.

| s | H | TH | TTH | TTTH | TTTTH | ... |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| X(s) | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | ... |

The pmf is:

|---|---|---|---|---|

Sometimes interest will focus on the expected value of some function h(X) rather than on just E(X).

E[h(X)] = E(Y) = Σ y ∙ P(Y = y) = Σ y ∙ P(X ∈ {x: h(x) = y}) = Σ h(x) ∙ p(x).

# Slide 2

**Example 24.1 revisit**

**Design 2 - Matched Pairs Design:**

* Break students into pairs with similar characteristics.
* Randomly give one student in pair practice exam, other student no practice exam.
* Give students exam and compare outcomes within pairs.

**Design 3 - Matched Pairs Design:**

* Randomly assign students to
    * Either: first take one exam without practice exam, and then to take a second exam with practice exam.
    * Or: first take one exam with practice exam, and then to take a second exam without practice exam.
* Compare 2 outcomes of each student (within pair).

**Definition: paired data**

The data consists of *n* independently selected pairs (𝑋<sub>1</sub>, 𝑌<sub>1</sub>), (𝑋<sub>2</sub>, 𝑌<sub>2</sub>), … , (𝑋<sub>𝑛</sub>, 𝑌<sub>𝑛</sub>). Let 𝐷<sub>𝑖</sub> = 𝑋<sub>𝑖</sub> − 𝑌<sub>𝑖</sub>, so the 𝐷<sub>𝑖</sub>’s are the differences within pairs. Then the 𝐷<sub>𝑖</sub>’s are assumed to have the same distribution with mean value μ<sub>𝐷</sub> and variance σ<sub>𝐷</sub><sup>2</sup>.

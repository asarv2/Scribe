**Two sample paired test (paired data)**

By the definition of paired data, the problem of comparing two population means is equivalent to testing the population mean of D. Therefore, inference of paired data essentially is the inference of the one sample *D* data.

**Basic Assumptions: paired data**

1. The data consists of *n* independently selected pairs (𝑋<sub>1</sub>, 𝑌<sub>1</sub>), (𝑋<sub>2</sub>, 𝑌<sub>2</sub>), … , (𝑋<sub>𝑛</sub>, 𝑌<sub>𝑛</sub>) with 𝐸(𝑋<sub>𝑖</sub>) = μ<sub>1</sub>  and 𝐸(𝑌<sub>𝑖</sub>) = μ<sub>2</sub>.

2. Let 𝐷<sub>𝑖</sub>= 𝑋<sub>𝑖</sub>−𝑌<sub>𝑖</sub>, so the 𝐷<sub>𝑖</sub>’s are the differences within pairs.

3. Then the 𝐷<sub>𝑖</sub>’s are assumed to be normally distributed with mean value μ<sub>𝐷</sub> and variance σ<sub>𝐷</sub><sup>2</sup> (this is usually a consequence of the 𝑋<sub>𝑖</sub>’s and 𝑌<sub>𝑖</sub>′s themselves being normally distributed).

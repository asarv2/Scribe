**Two sample Z test/CI for mean comparison**

The inferences discussed in this section concern a difference 𝜇<sub>1</sub> – 𝜇<sub>2</sub> between the means of two population distributions.

* One such null hypothesis 𝐻<sub>0</sub> would state that

𝐻<sub>0</sub>: 𝜇<sub>1</sub> – 𝜇<sub>2</sub> = Δ<sub>0</sub> or 𝜇<sub>1</sub> = 𝜇<sub>2</sub> + Δ<sub>0</sub>

Three possible alternative hypotheses:

𝐻<sub>1</sub>: 𝜇<sub>1</sub> −𝜇<sub>2</sub> > 𝛥<sub>0</sub>
𝐻<sub>1</sub>: 𝜇<sub>1</sub> −𝜇<sub>2</sub> < 𝛥<sub>0</sub>
𝐻<sub>1</sub>: 𝜇<sub>1</sub> −𝜇<sub>2</sub> ≠𝛥<sub>0</sub>

* Alternatively, it may be appropriate to estimate 𝜇<sub>1</sub> – 𝜇<sub>2</sub> by computing a C level CI.


Basic Assumptions: independent data

1. 𝑋<sub>1</sub>, … , 𝑋<sub>𝑚</sub> is a random sample from a normal distribution with mean μ<sub>1</sub> and variance σ<sub>1</sub><sup>2</sup>
2. 𝑌<sub>1</sub>, … , 𝑌<sub>𝑛</sub> is a random sample from another normal distribution with mean μ<sub>2</sub> and variance σ<sub>2</sub><sup>2</sup>
3. The X and Y samples are independent of one another.


Theorem 1:

* 𝑋̅ −𝑌̅ is a point estimator for 𝜇<sub>1</sub> – 𝜇<sub>2</sub>.
* 𝐸(𝑋̅ −𝑌̅) = μ<sub>1</sub> −μ<sub>2</sub>, 𝑋̅ −𝑌̅ is unbiased.
* 𝑉𝑎𝑟(𝑋̅ −𝑌̅) =  σ<sub>1</sub><sup>2</sup>/𝑚 + σ<sub>2</sub><sup>2</sup>/𝑛.

* Furthermore,

o Case 1, if both X and Y have normal distributions, 𝑋̅ −𝑌̅ follows exactly normal distribution.

o Case 2, if X and Y are not normal, but both m and n are large enough, then 𝑋̅ −𝑌̅ follows approximately normal distribution.

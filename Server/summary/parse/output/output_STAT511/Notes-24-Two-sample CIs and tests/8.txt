Case 4: X and Y are normal with unknown with equal variances:
Pooled t procedure

If we can assume that not only that the two population distributions are normal but also that they have equal variances, we can use a pooled t test statistic for difference of the two samples.

Let σ<sup>2</sup> denote the common population variance. Then standardizing X̅ − Y̅ gives

Z<sub>TS</sub> = (X̅ − Y̅) − Δ<sub>0</sub>
√σ<sup>2</sup>/m + σ<sup>2</sup>/n  
= (X̅ − Y̅) − Δ<sub>0</sub>
σ√(1/m + 1/n)

which has a standard normal distribution.

The following weighted average of the two sample variances, called the pooled (i.e., combined) estimator of σ<sup>2</sup>, adjusts for any difference between the two sample sizes:

S<sub>p</sub><sup>2</sup> = ((m − 1)S<sub>1</sub><sup>2</sup> + (n − 1)S<sub>2</sub><sup>2</sup>) / (m + n − 2)

Note: This estimator combines the information of data X and Y together for the estimation. The first sample contributes m − 1 df to the estimator, and the second sample contributes n − 1 df, for a total of m + n − 2 df.

This pooled estimator of σ<sup>2</sup> will be used to substitute unknown population variance σ<sup>2</sup> in the above expression for Z.

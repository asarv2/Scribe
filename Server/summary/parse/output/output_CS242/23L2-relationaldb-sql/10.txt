**Description of the Slide:**

This slide, part of a lecture on Introduction to Data Science, discusses one-to-one relationships in relational databases.  The title "One-to-One Relationship" is displayed at the top.  The text explains that in a true one-to-one relationship, each row in one table corresponds to exactly one row in another table.  However, this type of relationship is not very common because a single column could often be used to achieve the same result.  The slide presents two tables side-by-side.  The table on the left, labeled "Person," contains data with columns for "ID," "Last Name," "First Name," and "RoleID."  The table on the right, labeled "PurdueID," contains data with columns for "ID" and "PurdueID."  The data in both tables is structured in rows and columns, with each row representing a record and each column representing an attribute.  The data includes sample information about individuals and their Purdue IDs.  The purpose of this slide is to illustrate the concept of a one-to-one relationship and its less frequent use compared to other relationships, as it can often be achieved with a single column.  The tables are formatted with clear column headers and data entries, making the one-to-one relationship between the tables easily visible.  The layout is designed to visually represent the concept of a one-to-one relationship and its potential redundancy.  The horizontal line connecting the "Person" and "PurdueID" boxes visually represents the relationship between the two tables.

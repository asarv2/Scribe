# Slide 27

**From Text to Table**

Remove Punctuation / Stop Words
Convert to same case
Identify unique words (stem to roots?)
Count the occurrences of each word
Add the document to your table
* Add new columns for new words
* Record frequencies for appearing words
* Record zeros for the rest
Repeat for the corpus

**Description of the Slide:**

This slide outlines the steps involved in transforming textual data into a structured table format, suitable for analysis.  The top section lists the preprocessing steps required: removing punctuation, converting text to lowercase, identifying unique words (potentially stemming them), counting word occurrences, and populating a table.  The bottom section shows a partially completed table, where the columns represent unique words (like "collar," "enjoy," "beowulf") and the rows represent different documents (like "Beowulf," "Frankenstein").  The empty cells indicate that the frequency counts for those words in those documents have not yet been determined.  The presence of the text from a portion of a literary work (Beowulf) suggests that the slide is part of a lecture on text analysis, specifically on how to prepare text data for quantitative analysis.  The table structure is a key component of the process, allowing for the creation of a Document-Term Matrix, which is a fundamental step in many text analysis tasks, such as TF-IDF calculations.  The slide is designed to illustrate the workflow from raw text to a structured table representation.

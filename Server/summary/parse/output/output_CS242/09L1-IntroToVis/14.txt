# Slide 14

**Example of visualization success story**

**(Image Description):**

A line graph, titled "Figure 2.1 The hockey stick chart," is presented.  The x-axis is labeled "Year" and spans from approximately 1000 to 2000. The y-axis is labeled "Departures in temperature (°C) from the 1961-1990 average" and ranges from -1.0 to 0.5.  A thick black line represents an average temperature trend.  A band of light gray shading represents the range of temperature variation based on multiple data sources.  A thinner blue line and a thinner red line are also visible, representing different data sets used to calculate the average temperature.  A small box, positioned near the upper right of the graph, contains the text "Data from thermometers (red) and from tree rings, corals, ice cores and historical records (blue)."  The graph is titled "NORTHERN HEMISPHERE" above the graph.  The graph is positioned centrally on the slide.  Below the graph, there is a caption that reads "Figure 2.1 The hockey stick chart. Summary For Policymakers of the 2001 Third Assessment Report of the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change."

**Description:**

This slide, part of an Intro to Data Science lecture, presents a visualization of historical temperature data.  The graph, a "hockey stick" chart, displays the average temperature departures from a baseline (1961-1990 average) over time.  The graph combines data from multiple sources (thermometers, tree rings, corals, ice cores, and historical records) to show a clear upward trend in temperature in recent centuries.  The visual representation of the data is intended to illustrate a significant finding in climate science, demonstrating the impact of human activity on global temperatures.  The inclusion of the caption provides context and attribution to the source of the data.

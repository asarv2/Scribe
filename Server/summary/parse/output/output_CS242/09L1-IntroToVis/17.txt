**(Figure 1. Cable Industry Expenditures):** A line graph is presented. The x-axis represents years, ranging from 1990 to 2013. The y-axis represents the amount of expenditure in billions of dollars.  A dark-blue line traces the trend of cable industry infrastructure expenditures over time.  Several vertical gridlines are visible, providing a visual reference for the data points.  Key points on the graph are labeled with annotations:

* **1990-1992:** Expenditure is relatively low.
* **1996:** Expenditure begins to increase.
* **1998:** Expenditure peaks.
* **2000:** Expenditure begins to decrease.
* **2002:**  The "Telecommunications Act is passed" annotation is positioned near the graph, indicating a significant event.
* **2002-2013:** Expenditure fluctuates, but generally remains below the peak of 1998.

Annotations are also present on the graph, indicating the years when the Cable Television Consumer Protection and Competition Act ("Regulation") and the Telecommunications Act ("Deregulation") were passed.  These annotations are positioned near the corresponding points on the graph.

**Description:**

This slide, part of an Intro to Data Science lecture, presents a line graph illustrating the trend of cable industry infrastructure expenditures over time.  The graph visually displays how expenditures changed in response to regulatory changes (regulation and deregulation).  The slide's purpose is to demonstrate how additional information (like the passage of regulatory acts) can alter conclusions drawn from a simpler analysis of the data.  The graph is designed to show the correlation between regulatory changes and infrastructure spending.  The annotations on the graph highlight the key events and their potential impact on the trend.

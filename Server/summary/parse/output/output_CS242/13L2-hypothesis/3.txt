# Slide 3

# Explaining Patterns in Data

A good explanation consists of:

* Claim that answers a question/problem
* Evidence from data
* Reasoning that connects evidence to claim, shows why data counts as evidence

---

**Description of the Slide:**

This slide, titled "Explaining Patterns in Data," outlines the components of a good explanation.  The slide is a light background with dark text.  The title is at the top.  Below that, a bulleted list describes the three key elements of a good explanation:

* **Claim:**  This is a statement that answers a question or solves a problem.
* **Evidence:**  This is data that supports the claim.
* **Reasoning:** This is the process that connects the evidence to the claim, demonstrating why the data supports the claim.

In the middle-right of the slide, there is a simple diagram.  It shows a concept map with "Claim + Evidence + Reasoning = Explanation" in the center.  Arrows connect this central concept to "What do you know?" and "How do you know that?".  Another arrow connects the central concept to "Why does your evidence support your claim?".  The arrows and text are in an orange/peach color.  This visual aids in understanding the interconnectedness of the three components in forming a complete explanation.  The overall design of the slide is clear and concise, focusing on the key elements of data explanation.

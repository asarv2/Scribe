# Slide 7

**Claims**

Claim: Men are nearly twice as likely to survive in the United States (82%) as in England (44%), based on a five-year survival rate after detection.

**Data**

Data: In a 2000 study, 49 British men per 100,000 were diagnosed with prostate cancer, of which 28 died within five years. Thus 21/49 survived, about 43%.

But screening for prostate cancer is different in England than in the United States. The US country relies heavily on a PSA test, which can sometimes detect cancer earlier. But the test is not widely used in England. Thus, the detection, and the five-year survival rate, spans a different time in the lives of patients in the two countries.

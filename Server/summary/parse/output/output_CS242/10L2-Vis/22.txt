**Contour plot (3D)**

Limitations of 2D scatterplot (e.g., when there is
too much data to discern relationship)

Solution: represent a 3D surface by plotting
constant z slices (contours) in a 2D format


**(Figure Description):**

The slide presents two figures side-by-side.

* **Left Figure:** A scatter plot displays a large number of data points densely clustered in a roughly circular or elliptical region within the plot area.  The x-axis ranges from approximately 0 to 20, and the y-axis ranges similarly.  The high density of points in a specific area makes it difficult to discern individual data points and potentially obscures the underlying patterns or relationships in the data.  This is a typical example of a 2D scatter plot's limitation when dealing with a large dataset.

* **Right Figure:** A contour plot is shown, representing the same data as the left figure.  Instead of individual points, this plot uses contour lines to depict the distribution of data points in the 3D space.  The contour lines represent different levels or densities of data points.  The x-axis and y-axis ranges are similar to the left plot, but the values on the axes are not numerical values of the original data, but rather represent the ranges of the data.  The contour plot provides a more effective way to visualize the overall distribution of the data, especially when dealing with a large number of overlapping points.  The contour lines show the density of the data points in the 3D space, providing a clearer picture of the underlying relationship between the variables.

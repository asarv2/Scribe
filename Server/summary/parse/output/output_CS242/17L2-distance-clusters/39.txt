# Slide 39

**Example 1: Good Clustering**

A scatter plot is displayed on the left side of the slide.  The plot shows three distinct clusters of data points, each colored differently (red, green, and blue).  The x-axis ranges from approximately 0 to 1, and the y-axis ranges from approximately 0 to 1.  The points within each cluster are relatively close to each other, and the clusters are well-separated.  The data points are small circles.

A heatmap-like proximity matrix is displayed on the right side of the slide.  The matrix is a square grid of colored squares.  The colors vary from a light blue to a bright orange/red.  The colors represent the proximity (or distance) between data points.  The pattern of colors in the matrix clearly reflects the clustering structure.  The areas where the colors are similar (e.g., a block of bright orange) correspond to data points that are close together in the scatter plot.  The areas where the colors are different (e.g., a block of light blue) correspond to data points that are far apart in the scatter plot.  The proximity matrix is reordered to reflect the cluster assignments, which is evident in the block-like structure of the colors.

**Description:**

This slide provides an example of a "good" clustering result.  The scatter plot on the left visually demonstrates the separation of data points into distinct clusters.  The proximity matrix on the right visually confirms the quality of the clustering by showing a clear block pattern, where similar colors represent similar distances between data points within the same cluster.  The slide is part of a lecture on clustering, illustrating how a well-structured clustering should appear in both a scatter plot and a proximity matrix.  The example is intended to contrast with examples of poor clustering results shown in previous slides.

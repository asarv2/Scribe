# Slide 31

**Intelligible Models for HealthCare**

Caruana et al. 2015

In the mid-90s, Cost-Effective Healthcare (CEHC) funded a large study to evaluate the application of machine learning to predicting pneumonia risk.

**Goal:** predict the probability of death (POD) for patients with pneumonia so high-risk patients could be admitted to the hospital while low-risk patients treated as outpatients.

Most accurate models were neural nets, which outperformed traditional methods by a wide margin.

But after careful consideration, they were considered too risky for use on real patients, and logistic regression was used instead. Why?

* Rule-based system used in the study learned the rule HasAsthma(x) → LowerRisk(x)
    * i.e., patients who have a history of asthma have a lower risk of dying from pneumonia than the general population—which is counterintuitive.
    * This reflected a true pattern in the training data.
    * Patients with a history of asthma were admitted directly to the ICU with aggressive care that effectively lowered their risk of dying.
    * But causal reasoning is incorrect... asthmatics have a much higher risk if not hospitalized.
* If rule-based systems have this problem, then neural networks probably made similar mistakes... but even more difficult to interpret.

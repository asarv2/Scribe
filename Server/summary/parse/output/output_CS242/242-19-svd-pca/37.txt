# Slide 37

**Modeling Systematic Biases**

```
μ = overall mean rating
b<sub>u</sub> = mean rating for user u
b<sub>i</sub> = mean rating for movie i
```

**Description:**

This slide, part of an Intro to Data Science lecture, introduces a method for modeling systematic biases in ratings data, likely for a recommender system.  The slide defines three variables:

* **μ (mu):**  Represents the overall average rating across all users and movies.

* **b<sub>u</sub>:** Represents the average rating bias for a specific user u.  This accounts for the tendency of some users to rate items higher or lower than the average.

* **b<sub>i</sub>:** Represents the average rating bias for a specific movie i.  This accounts for the tendency of some movies to receive higher or lower ratings than the average, potentially due to genre, popularity, or other factors.

The context of this slide is to introduce a way to model and account for these biases in the rating data, which can improve the accuracy of recommender systems.  There are no figures on this slide.  The slide is likely a precursor to a discussion of how these biases can be incorporated into a model to predict ratings more accurately.

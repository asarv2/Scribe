# Slide 3

# The FOUR V's of Big Data

**Volume**
* 40 Zettabytes (43 Trillion Gigabytes) of data will be created by 2020, a 300x increase from 2005.
* 2.5 Quintillion Bytes (2.3 Trillion Gigabytes) of data are created each day.
* 6 Billion people have cell phones.
* World Population: 7 Billion
* The New York Stock Exchange captures 1 Terabyte of trade information during each trading session.
* Most companies in the U.S. have at least 100 Terabytes of data stored.
* Modern cars have close to 100 sensors that monitor items such as fuel level and tire pressure.

**Velocity**
* Analysis of streaming data.
* By 2016, it is projected there will be 18.9 Billion network connections - almost 2.5 connections per person on earth.
* By 2015, 4.4 Million IT jobs will be created globally to support big data, with 1.9 million in the United States.

**Variety**
* Different forms of data.
* 30 Billion pieces of content are shared on Facebook every month.
* By 2014, it's anticipated there will be 420 Million wearable, wireless health monitors.
* 4 Billion+ hours of video are watched on YouTube each month.
* 400 Million Tweets are sent per day by about 200 million monthly active users.

**Veracity**
* Uncertainty of data.
* 27% of respondents in one survey were unsure of how much of their data was inaccurate.
* Poor data quality costs the US economy around $3.1 Trillion a year.


**Description of the slide:**

This slide visually presents the four key characteristics of big data: Volume, Velocity, Variety, and Veracity.  It's a large infographic-style layout, with each characteristic represented in a distinct section.  Each section contains various data points and visualizations to illustrate the scale and complexity of big data.

* **Visualizations:**  The slide uses various icons, charts, and diagrams to represent the data.  For example, stacked cylinders represent the growth of data over time, and a world map shows global population.  There are also icons representing social media platforms (Facebook, YouTube), cell phones, and wearable health monitors.  These visual aids help to convey the vastness and diverse nature of big data.
* **Data Points:**  The slide provides specific numerical data points, such as the projected number of data bytes created each day, the number of people with cell phones, and the amount of data captured by the New York Stock Exchange.  These figures are meant to highlight the sheer volume of data being generated and processed.
* **Context:** The slide is designed to introduce the concept of big data and its characteristics in the context of the Intro to Data Science course.  The information presented is meant to be a high-level overview of the topic, providing a general understanding of the scale and complexity of big data.  The slide also includes a source list at the bottom, which is a common practice in academic presentations to give credit to the original sources of the data.

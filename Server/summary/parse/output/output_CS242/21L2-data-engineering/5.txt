# Slide 5

# What is Big?

Your data fits in RAM (e.g., 64GB) on a single machine?
Your data fits on disk (e.g., 8TB) on a single machine?
* Movielens 100K ratings = 3.1MB; 26M ratings = 1GB
* Total size of Wikipedia (English article text, compressed) is 16.1 GB
Your data fits in RAM/disk on a small cluster of machines? (i.e., don't need to worry about machines dying)
Your data fits in RAM/disk on a large cluster of machines? (i.e., do need to worry about machines dying)
* Facebook's Data warehouse generates 4 petabytes of data per day Facebook
* Walmart collects 2.5 petabytes unstructured data (from 1 million customers every hour)

**Description of the slide:**

This slide, part of a lecture on Intro to Data Science, explores the concept of "big data" by posing questions about the size of datasets and their implications for storage and processing.

* **Content:** The slide presents a series of questions and statements designed to illustrate the different scales of data.  It asks if the data fits in a single machine's RAM or disk, and then provides examples of data sizes (e.g., Movielens ratings, Wikipedia data).  It also introduces the concept of data clusters and the potential for machine failure in larger systems.  Finally, it gives examples of massive data generation rates from companies like Facebook and Walmart.
* **Purpose:** The slide's purpose is to help students understand the difference between manageable and unmanageable data sizes in the context of data science.  The examples are meant to illustrate the scale of data that can be encountered in real-world applications.  The questions about machine failure highlight the importance of distributed systems for handling large datasets.
* **Visuals:** There are no figures or images on this slide.  The content is entirely text-based, focusing on the textual description of data sizes and the implications of data volume.

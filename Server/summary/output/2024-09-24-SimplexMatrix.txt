\section*{Simplex in Matrix Notation}

\begin{align*}
\max \ \ & \mathbf{c}^T \mathbf{x} \\
\text{s.t.} \ \ & \mathbf{A} \mathbf{x} \le \mathbf{b} \\
& \mathbf{x} \ge \mathbf{0}
\end{align*}
$\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^n$, $\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{R}^n$ \\
$\mathbf{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$, $\mathbf{b} \in \mathbb{R}^m$

\vspace{1cm}

\begin{align*}
\max \ \ & \mathbf{c}^T \mathbf{x} \\
\text{s.t.} \ \ & \mathbf{w} = \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{A} \mathbf{x} \\
& \mathbf{x}, \mathbf{w} \ge \mathbf{0}
\end{align*}
$\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^m$

\end{document}

\vspace{1cm}

\textbf{Description:}

This slide presents two equivalent formulations of a linear programming problem in matrix notation.  The top formulation is the standard form, where the objective function is to maximize $\mathbf{c}^T \mathbf{x}$ subject to the constraints $\mathbf{A} \mathbf{x} \le \mathbf{b}$ and $\mathbf{x} \ge \mathbf{0}$.  The bottom formulation introduces a slack variable $\mathbf{w}$ to convert the inequality constraint to an equality constraint, making the problem more suitable for simplex method implementation.  The slide also defines the dimensions of the vectors and matrices involved ($\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^n$, $\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{R}^n$, $\mathbf{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$, $\mathbf{b} \in \mathbb{R}^m$, $\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^m$).  The red arrows indicate a transformation between the two formulations. This is likely the first step in a lecture series explaining the simplex method in matrix form.

\section*{Simplex Iterations in Matrix Form}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item $B \mathbf{x}_B + N \mathbf{x}_N = \mathbf{b} \implies \mathbf{x}_B = B^{-1} \mathbf{b} - B^{-1} N \mathbf{x}_N$
    \item $\mathcal{Z} = \mathbf{c}_B^T \mathbf{x}_B + \mathbf{c}_N^T \mathbf{x}_N = \mathbf{c}_B^T (B^{-1} \mathbf{b} - B^{-1} N \mathbf{x}_N) + \mathbf{c}_N^T \mathbf{x}_N$
    \item $= \mathbf{c}_B^T B^{-1} \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{c}_B^T B^{-1} N \mathbf{x}_N + \mathbf{c}_N^T \mathbf{x}_N$
    \item $= \mathbf{c}_B^T B^{-1} \mathbf{b} + (\mathbf{c}_N^T - \mathbf{c}_B^T B^{-1} N) \mathbf{x}_N$
    \item $= \overline{\mathcal{Z}} + \mathbf{c}_N^T \mathbf{x}_N$
\end{enumerate}

\end{document}

\vspace{1cm}

\textbf{Description:}

This slide presents the derivation of the objective function in matrix form during a simplex iteration.  It shows how the objective function ($\mathcal{Z}$) is expressed in terms of basic ($\mathbf{x}_B$) and non-basic ($\mathbf{x}_N$) variables, given the constraint equation $B \mathbf{x}_B + N \mathbf{x}_N = \mathbf{b}$.  The key steps are: (1) solving for $\mathbf{x}_B$ in terms of $\mathbf{x}_N$ and $\mathbf{b}$, (2) substituting this expression into the objective function, (3) rearranging the terms to isolate the constant term ($\overline{\mathcal{Z}}$) and the terms dependent on the non-basic variables ($\mathbf{x}_N$).  This derivation is crucial for the simplex method, as it allows for efficient calculation of the objective function value at each iteration and for determining the next variable to enter the basis.  The notation $B$, $N$, $\mathbf{c}_B$, $\mathbf{c}_N$, and $B^{-1}$ are all matrix representations used in the simplex algorithm.

\section*{Example}

\begin{align*}

\[
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & -1 & 1 & 0 & 0 \\
2 & -1 & 0 & 1 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 1
\end{bmatrix}
\]

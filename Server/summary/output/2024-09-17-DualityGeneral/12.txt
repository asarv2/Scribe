Slide 12

\textbf{Duality for General LP}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Primal Problem (P)}
    \begin{align*}
        \max \xi(x) &= c^T x \\
        \text{s.t.} \quad Ax &\le b \\
        x &\ge 0
    \end{align*}
    \item \textbf{Dual Problem (D)}
    \begin{align*}
        \min \xi(\lambda) &= \lambda^T b \\
        \text{s.t.} \quad A^T \lambda &\ge c \\
        \lambda &\ge 0
    \end{align*}
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Description:}

This slide presents the primal and dual problems for a general linear program (LP), but now explicitly showing the standard form of the dual problem. The primal problem (P) is to maximize the objective function $c^T x$ subject to the constraints $Ax \le b$ and $x \ge 0$.  The dual problem (D) is to minimize the function $\lambda^T b$ subject to the constraints $A^T \lambda \ge c$ and $\lambda \ge 0$.  This is a crucial step in the development of the duality theory for linear programming, showing the standard forms of the primal and dual problems.  The notation $c^T x$ represents the dot product of the vectors $c$ and $x$.  The notation $A^T$ represents the transpose of the matrix $A$.  The slide is likely part of a larger presentation on duality and optimization techniques in linear programming.  The key difference from slide 11 is the inequality constraint in the dual problem.

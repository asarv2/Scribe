\section*{Equality Constraints}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Compare $\max f_0$ with constraints $\leftrightarrow$ $\max L$ with no constraints
    \item Let $x \in \mathcal{G}$, i.e. $g_j(x) \ge 0$
    \item $\xi(x) = f_0(x) \le \xi(\mu)$ for any $\tilde{x}$ satisfies constraint
    \item $\xi = \max_{x \in \mathcal{G}} \xi(x) \le \min_{\mu} \xi(\mu) = \xi^*$  (Weak Duality)
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Description:}

This slide continues the discussion of equality constraints in linear programming, building on the previous slides' introduction of the Lagrangian and the comparison between optimization with and without constraints.  It formally states the **Weak Duality Theorem**.  The inequality $\xi(x) \le \xi(\mu)$ is restated, emphasizing that the maximum value of the objective function ($\xi(x)$) subject to the constraints ($\mathcal{G}$) is always less than or equal to the minimum value of the Lagrangian function ($\xi(\mu)$) without constraints.  The notation $\xi^*$ represents the minimum value of $\xi(\mu)$.  The statement "Weak Duality" is explicitly included, highlighting the fundamental property of duality in optimization.  This slide is likely part of a larger presentation on duality and optimization techniques in linear programming, demonstrating the fundamental duality relationship between primal and dual problems.

\begin{align*}
\tilde{Z}_0 &= \left( \begin{array}{c} 3 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{array} \right) = (R^T N)^T C_B - C_N, \quad X_N = \left( \begin{array}{c} x_2 \\ x_4 \\ x_6 \end{array} \right) \\
\xi &= 13 - 3x_2 - 1x_4 - 1x_6 \\
X_B &= \left( \begin{array}{c} x_3 \\ x_1 \\ x_5 \end{array} \right) = \left( \begin{array}{ccc} 1 & x_2 & 3x_4 - 2x_6 \\ 2 & -2x_2 & -2x_4 + x_6 \\ 1 & 5x_2 & 2x_4 \end{array} \right) \\
\tilde{X}_B &= \left( \begin{array}{c} 1 \\ 2 \\ 1 \end{array} \right) = \tilde{R} \tilde{B} = \left( \begin{array}{ccc} -1 & 3 & -2 \\ -2 & -2 & -2 \\ 5 & 2 & 0 \end{array} \right)
\end{align*}

\end{document}

\vspace{1cm}

\textbf{Description:}

This slide continues the sensitivity analysis of the linear programming problem. It shows the optimal solution in a more detailed form, including the vector notation for the basic variables ($X_B$) and non-basic variables ($X_N$).  The equation $\tilde{Z}_0 = (R^T N)^T C_B - C_N$ likely represents the calculation of the objective function value in terms of the basic and non-basic variables.  The matrix $\tilde{R}$ and the vector $\tilde{B}$ are likely part of the simplex method's tableau representation, used to determine the optimal solution and its sensitivity to changes in the problem parameters.  The slide also shows the optimal solution values for the basic variables ($x_3$, $x_1$, $x_5$) in terms of the non-basic variables ($x_2$, $x_4$, $x_6$).  The notation and calculations are advanced and assume familiarity with the simplex method and matrix operations.

\textbf{Description:}

This slide presents the conditions for the optimal solution to remain optimal when the objective function coefficients are changed.  The equations show the relationships between the basic variables ($X_B$), non-basic variables ($X_N$), and the change in the objective function coefficients ($\Delta C_B$, $\Delta C_N$).  The condition $\tilde{Z}_N^* + \Delta \tilde{Z}_N \ge 0$ is crucial; it ensures that the reduced costs of the non-basic variables remain non-negative, which is a necessary condition for the current optimal solution to remain optimal.  The notation uses matrix operations, specifically the inverse of the basis matrix ($B^{-1}$) and the transpose ($^T$).  This slide is a continuation of the sensitivity analysis, focusing on the conditions for the optimality of the solution to remain valid under changes in the objective function.

These parameters represent potential changes in the right-hand side (RHS) values of the constraints.  This formulation is typical of a parametric analysis in linear programming, where the constraint values are allowed to vary, and the impact on the optimal solution is investigated.  The question "what if" suggests an exploration of how different values of $\Delta b_i$ affect the optimal solution.

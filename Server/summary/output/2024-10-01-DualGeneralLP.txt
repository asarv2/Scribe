Slide 1

\textbf{Dual of General LP Problem}

\newline
\textit{(EVJ p.107 / #66, Chapter 9, p 152)}

\newline
\textbf{Recall (P)}
\begin{align*}
\max \ \vec{s} &= \vec{c} \vec{x} \\
\text{s.t.} \quad \vec{A} \vec{x} &\le \vec{b} \\
\qquad \vec{x} &\ge \vec{0}
\end{align*}

\newline
\textbf{(D)}
\begin{align*}
\min \ \vec{s} &= \vec{b} \vec{y} \\
\text{s.t.} \quad \vec{A}^T \vec{y} &\ge \vec{c} \\
\qquad \vec{y} &\ge \vec{0}
\end{align*}

\newline
\textbf{Derivation:}
Take linear combination of the constraints.
\newline
$y$ - Lagrange Multiplier

\newline
$(\vec{y}^T \vec{A}) \vec{x} \le \vec{y}^T \vec{b} \implies (\vec{y}^T \vec{A}) \vec{x} \le \vec{y}^T \vec{b}$

\newline
$\vec{c}^T \vec{x} \le (\vec{y}^T \vec{A}) \vec{x} \le \vec{y}^T \vec{b}$


---

\textbf{Description:}

This slide introduces the dual of a general linear programming (LP) problem.  It shows the primal problem (P) and its corresponding dual problem (D).  The slide highlights the relationship between the two problems, noting that the dual problem is derived by taking a linear combination of the constraints of the primal problem.  The notation uses vectors and matrices, typical of linear programming formulations.  The slide also includes a reference to a textbook and page numbers, indicating the source of the material.  The red arrows and text "Derivation:" and "Take linear combination of the constraints" indicate the derivation process.  The notation $y$ - Lagrange Multiplier is a key concept in the derivation.  The slide number is 1 of 7, suggesting this is the first of a series of slides on linear programming duality.


Slide 2

\textbf{General LP}

\newline
\textbf{(M1)}
\begin{align*}
\max \ \vec{c}^T \vec{x} \\
\text{s.t.} \quad \vec{A} \vec{x} &\le \vec{b} \\
\qquad \vec{x} &\le \vec{u}
\end{align*}
\newline
\textit{(not necessarily $\vec{x} \ge \vec{0}$)}

\newline
\begin{align*}
\max \ \vec{c}^T (\vec{x}^+ - \vec{x}^-) \\
\text{s.t.} \quad \vec{A} (\vec{x}^+ - \vec{x}^-) &\le \vec{b} \\
\qquad \vec{x}^+, \vec{x}^- &\ge \vec{0} \\
\qquad \vec{x} = \vec{x}^+ - \vec{x}^-
\end{align*}

---

\textbf{Description:}

This slide continues the discussion of linear programming duality, specifically focusing on the case where the primal problem's constraints do not necessarily require non-negativity of the variables ($\vec{x} \ge \vec{0}$).  It introduces a reformulation of the primal problem (M1) by splitting the variables into positive and negative components ($\vec{x}^+$ and $\vec{x}^-$).  The slide shows how the original constraints are maintained, and the new variables are constrained to be non-negative.  The notation is consistent with the previous slide, using vectors and matrices.  The arrow indicates a transformation or reformulation of the primal problem.  The slide number is 2 of 7, indicating its place in a larger presentation.


Slide 3

\begin{align*}
\vec{y}^T \vec{A} (\vec{x}^+ - \vec{x}^-) &\le \vec{y}^T \vec{b} + \vec{z}^T \vec{u} \\
\vec{z}^T (\vec{x}^+ - \vec{x}^-) &\le \vec{z}^T \vec{u} \\
(\vec{y}^T \vec{A} + \vec{z}^T) \vec{x}^+ + (-\vec{y}^T \vec{A} - \vec{z}^T) \vec{x}^- &\le \vec{y}^T \vec{b} + \vec{z}^T \vec{u} \\
\implies \vec{c}^T &\le (\vec{y}^T \vec{A} + \vec{z}^T) \\
\end{align*}

\newline
\textbf{(D)}
\begin{align*}
\min \ \vec{b}^T \vec{y} + \vec{u}^T \vec{z} \\
\text{s.t.} \quad \vec{A}^T \vec{y} + \vec{z} &\ge \vec{c} \\
\qquad \vec{y}, \vec{z} &\ge \vec{0}
\end{align*}

---

\textbf{Description:}

This slide continues the derivation of the dual problem from the primal problem (M1), which is a general linear programming problem that doesn't necessarily require non-negativity constraints on the variables.  It shows the intermediate steps in manipulating the inequalities to arrive at the final form of the dual problem (D).  The slide demonstrates how the introduction of slack variables ($\vec{z}$) allows for the formulation of the dual problem in a standard form.  The notation is consistent with the previous slides, using vectors and matrices.  The slide number is 3 of 7, indicating its place in a larger presentation.  The derivation shows how the dual problem is constructed from the primal problem's constraints and objective function.


Slide 4

\textbf{M2}
\begin{align*}
\max \ \vec{c}^T \vec{x} \\
\text{s.t.} \quad \vec{A} \vec{x} \le \vec{b} \\
\qquad \vec{x} \le \vec{u}
\end{align*}

\newline
$\max \ \mathcal{S}(\vec{x}) = \vec{c}^T \vec{x}$
\newline
\textit{s.t.}
\begin{align*}
\vec{A} \vec{x} - \vec{b} &\le \vec{0} \\
\vec{x} - \vec{u} &\le \vec{0}
\end{align*}

\newline
\textbf{Lagrangian function}
\newline
$\mathcal{L}(\vec{x}, \vec{y}, \vec{z}) = \vec{c}^T \vec{x} - \vec{y}^T (\vec{A} \vec{x} - \vec{b}) - \vec{z}^T (\vec{x} - \vec{u})$
\newline
$\vec{y} \ge \vec{0}$
\newline
$\vec{z} \ge \vec{0}$

---

\textbf{Description:}

This slide continues the derivation of the dual problem by introducing the Lagrangian function.  It shows the primal problem (M2) again, emphasizing the constraints.  The Lagrangian function $\mathcal{L}(\vec{x}, \vec{y}, \vec{z})$ is presented, which incorporates the objective function and the constraints of the primal problem.  The Lagrangian function is a key step in the derivation of the dual problem, as it allows for the combination of the objective function and constraints into a single expression.  The slide number is 4 of 7, indicating its place in a larger presentation.  The notation is consistent with the previous slides, using vectors and matrices.  The introduction of the Lagrangian function is a crucial step in the process of deriving the dual problem.


Slide 5

Let $\vec{x}$ is (P) - feasible.
\newline
$\mathcal{S}(\vec{x}) = \vec{c}^T \vec{x}$
\newline
$\le \vec{c}^T \vec{x} - \vec{y}^T (\vec{A} \vec{x} - \vec{b}) - \vec{z}^T (\vec{x} - \vec{u})$
\newline
$\vec{y} \ge \vec{0}, \vec{z} \ge \vec{0}$
\newline
$\le \max_{\vec{x}} (\vec{c}^T \vec{x} - \vec{y}^T (\vec{A} \vec{x} - \vec{b}) - \vec{z}^T (\vec{x} - \vec{u}))$
\newline
$= \mathcal{S}(\vec{y}, \vec{z})$
\newline
Weak Duality
\newline
$\vec{x}$: P - feasible $\implies \mathcal{S}(\vec{x}) \le \mathcal{S}(\vec{y}, \vec{z})$
\newline
$\max \mathcal{S}(\vec{x}) \le \min \mathcal{S}(\vec{y}, \vec{z})$
\newline
$\vec{y}, \vec{z} \ge \vec{0}$

---

\textbf{Description:}

This slide presents the proof of weak duality in linear programming.  It starts by assuming a feasible solution $\vec{x}$ for the primal problem (P).  The slide then shows how the objective function value for the primal problem ($\mathcal{S}(\vec{x})$) is less than or equal to the objective function value for the dual problem ($\mathcal{S}(\vec{y}, \vec{z})$).  This inequality is the statement of weak duality.  The slide number is 5 of 7, indicating its place in a larger presentation.  The notation is consistent with the previous slides, using vectors and matrices.  The proof demonstrates the fundamental relationship between the optimal values of the primal and dual problems.


Slide 6

\begin{align*}
\label{eq:1}
(D) \quad \min_{\vec{y}, \vec{z} \ge \vec{0}} \ \mathcal{S}(\vec{y}, \vec{z}) = \vec{y}^T \vec{b} + \vec{z}^T \vec{u}
\end{align*}

\begin{align*}
\label{eq:2}
= \min_{\vec{y}, \vec{z} \ge \vec{0}} (\vec{c}^T \vec{x} - \vec{y}^T (\vec{A} \vec{x} - \vec{b}) - \vec{z}^T (\vec{x} - \vec{u}))
\end{align*}

\begin{align*}
\label{eq:3}
= \min_{\vec{y}, \vec{z} \ge \vec{0}} (\vec{c}^T \vec{x} - \vec{y}^T \vec{A} \vec{x} + \vec{y}^T \vec{b} - \vec{z}^T \vec{x} + \vec{z}^T \vec{u})
\end{align*}

\begin{align*}
\label{eq:4}
= \min_{\vec{y}, \vec{z} \ge \vec{0}} (\vec{c}^T - \vec{y}^T \vec{A} - \vec{z}^T) \vec{x} + \vec{y}^T \vec{b} + \vec{z}^T \vec{u}
\end{align*}

\begin{align*}
\label{eq:5}
\implies \vec{c}^T - \vec{y}^T \vec{A} - \vec{z}^T = \vec{0}
\end{align*}

\begin{align*}
\label{eq:6}
\implies \vec{A}^T \vec{y} + \vec{z} = \vec{c}
\end{align*}

\textbf{Description:}

This slide continues the derivation of the dual problem by simplifying the Lagrangian function.  It shows the steps to arrive at the final form of the dual problem's objective function and constraints.  The slide demonstrates how the Lagrangian function is used to derive the dual problem's constraints, specifically $\vec{A}^T \vec{y} + \vec{z} = \vec{c}$.  The slide number is 6 of 7, indicating its place in a larger presentation.  The notation is consistent with the previous slides, using vectors and matrices.  The derivation shows how the dual problem's constraints are derived from the primal problem's constraints and objective function.  The final result is the standard form of the dual problem.

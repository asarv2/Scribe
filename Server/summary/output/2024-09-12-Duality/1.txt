Slide 1

\textbf{Primal and Dual Problems}

\begin{itemize}
    \item[(P)]
    \begin{align*}
        \max \ \sum(x) &= c^T x \\
        \text{subject to} \quad Ax &\le b \\
        x &\ge 0
    \end{align*}
    \item[(D)]
    \begin{align*}
        \min \ \sum(y) &= b^T y \\
        \text{subject to} \quad A^T y &\ge c \\
        y &\ge 0
    \end{align*}
    \begin{center}
        $\iff$
    \end{center}
    \begin{align*}
        \max \ -\sum(y) &= -b^T y \\
        \text{subject to} \quad (A^T)y &\le c \\
        y &\ge 0
    \end{align*}
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Description:}

This slide introduces the primal and dual problems in linear programming.  It shows the standard forms of both problems.  The primal problem (P) seeks to maximize a linear function ($c^T x$) subject to linear inequality constraints ($Ax \le b$) and non-negativity constraints ($x \ge 0$). The dual problem (D) seeks to minimize a different linear function ($b^T y$) subject to different linear inequality constraints ($A^T y \ge c$) and non-negativity constraints ($y \ge 0$).  Importantly, the slide highlights the equivalence between the dual problem and a maximization problem with the opposite objective function and reversed inequality constraints.  The arrow between the two dual problem formulations indicates the transformation. This is a fundamental concept in linear programming, as solving one problem can often provide information about the solution to the other.  The slide is likely the first step in a lecture series on duality in linear programming.

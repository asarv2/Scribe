Slide 7

\textbf{Neg. Transpose Property is Preserved in each Simplex Step}

[V.J] p.64

\begin{itemize}
    \item[(P)]
    \begin{align*}
        \zeta &= 9 - 5x_1 + 4x_2 - 3w_2 \\
        w_1 &= 1 - x_1 - 4x_2 + x_3 \\
        x_3 &= 3 - 3x_1 + x_2 - w_2
    \end{align*}
    \begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|}
    \hline
    $\zeta$ & $x_1$ & $x_2$ & $x_3$ & $w_2$ \\ \hline
    $w_1$ & $-1$ & $-4$ & $1$ & $0$ \\ \hline
    $x_3$ & $-3$ & $1$ & $0$ & $-1$ \\ \hline
    \end{tabular}
    $x_2$ enters, $w_1$ leaves
    \item[(D)]
    \begin{align*}
        -\zeta &= -9 - 1y_1 - 3y_2 + 5z_1 \\
        z_1 &= 5 + y_1 + 4y_2 - z_2 \\
        z_2 &= -4 + 4y_1 - y_2 + z_3
    \end{align*}
    \begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|}
    \hline
    $-\zeta$ & $y_1$ & $y_2$ & $z_1$ & $z_3$ \\ \hline
    $z_1$ & $1$ & $1$ & $1$ & $0$ \\ \hline
    $z_2$ & $4$ & $-1$ & $0$ & $1$ \\ \hline
    \end{tabular}
    $z_2$ leaves, $y_1$ enters
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Description:}

This slide continues the demonstration of the negative transpose property in simplex method iterations for linear programming.  It shows two consecutive iterations of the simplex method, one for the primal problem (P) and one for the dual problem (D).  The tables represent the updated tableau after each iteration.  The slide illustrates how the coefficients in the dual problem are obtained by taking the negative transpose of the primal problem's coefficient matrix.  The step shown is a simplex iteration, where $x_2$ enters the basis in the primal problem and $w_1$ leaves, and in the dual problem, $y_1$ enters the basis and $z_2$ leaves.  The slide emphasizes that the negative transpose property is maintained throughout the simplex iterations.  The "greatest increase rule" is implied for selecting the entering variable in the primal problem.

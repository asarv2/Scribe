Slide 2

\textbf{General LP}

\newline
\textbf{(M1)}
\begin{align*}
\max \ \vec{c}^T \vec{x} \\
\text{s.t.} \quad \vec{A} \vec{x} &\le \vec{b} \\
\qquad \vec{x} &\le \vec{u}
\end{align*}
\newline
\textit{(not necessarily $\vec{x} \ge \vec{0}$)}

\newline
\begin{align*}
\max \ \vec{c}^T (\vec{x}^+ - \vec{x}^-) \\
\text{s.t.} \quad \vec{A} (\vec{x}^+ - \vec{x}^-) &\le \vec{b} \\
\qquad \vec{x}^+, \vec{x}^- &\ge \vec{0} \\
\qquad \vec{x} = \vec{x}^+ - \vec{x}^-
\end{align*}

---

\textbf{Description:}

This slide continues the discussion of linear programming duality, specifically focusing on the case where the primal problem's constraints do not necessarily require non-negativity of the variables ($\vec{x} \ge \vec{0}$).  It introduces a reformulation of the primal problem (M1) by splitting the variables into positive and negative components ($\vec{x}^+$ and $\vec{x}^-$).  The slide shows how the original constraints are maintained, and the new variables are constrained to be non-negative.  The notation is consistent with the previous slide, using vectors and matrices.  The arrow indicates a transformation or reformulation of the primal problem.

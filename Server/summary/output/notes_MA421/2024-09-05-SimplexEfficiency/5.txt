\section*{Worst Case Example: Klee-Minty (1970)}

\subsection*{[LcJ]}

Using the largest-coefficient rule, we construct the following sequence of dictionaries.
The initial dictionary:

\begin{align*}
x_4 &= 1 - x_1 \\
x_5 &= 100 - 20x_1 - x_2 \\
x_6 &= 10,000 - 200x_1 - 10x_2 - x_3
\end{align*}
$2^3 - 1$ steps

$\bar{x}_1 = x_1$, $\bar{x}_2 = 0.01x_2$, $\bar{x}_3 = 0.0001x_3$

converts the Klee-Minty problem (4.3) into the form

\begin{align*}
maximize \quad 100\bar{x}_1 + 1,000\bar{x}_2 + 10,000\bar{x}_3 \\
subject \ to \quad \bar{x}_1 &\le 1 \\
\quad 20\bar{x}_1 + 100\bar{x}_2 &\le 100 \\
\quad 200\bar{x}_1 + 2,000\bar{x}_2 + 10,000\bar{x}_3 &\le 10,000 \\
\bar{x}_1, \bar{x}_2, \bar{x}_3 &\ge 0.
\end{align*}

In the first dictionary associated with this new version of (4.3), the nonbasic variable $\bar{x}_3$ appears most attractive, and so the simplex method reaches the optimal solution in only one iteration.

\textbf{Description:}

This slide continues the Klee-Minty example, demonstrating how scaling the variables can affect the Simplex method's performance.  It shows a transformation of the original problem (4.3) into a new form with scaled variables ($\bar{x}_1$, $\bar{x}_2$, $\bar{x}_3$).  The transformed problem is presented with a new objective function and constraints.  The key point is that, with this scaling, the Simplex method using the largest-coefficient rule now finds the optimal solution in only one iteration.  This highlights the sensitivity of the Simplex method to the scaling of the variables and the potential for different scaling to lead to vastly different performance characteristics.

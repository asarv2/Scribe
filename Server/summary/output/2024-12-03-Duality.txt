\textbf{Duality}

\begin{align*}
\label{eq:1}
(P) \quad &\text{max} \quad c^T x \\
&\text{s.t.} \quad Ax \le b \\
& \quad \quad x \ge 0
\end{align*}
\begin{align*}
\label{eq:2}
(D) \quad &\text{min} \quad b^T y \\
&\text{s.t.} \quad A^T y \ge c \\
& \quad \quad y \ge 0
\end{align*}

\vspace{0.5cm}

$y^T A x \le y^T b$
$c^T x \le y^T A x \le y^T b$
$c^T \le y^T A$

$(x \ge 0)$

\vspace{0.5cm}

\textbf{Description:}

This slide introduces the concept of duality in linear programming.  It shows the primal problem (P) and its corresponding dual problem (D).  The primal problem (P) seeks to maximize a linear objective function subject to linear constraints (Ax ≤ b and x ≥ 0). The dual problem (D) seeks to minimize a different linear objective function subject to different constraints (A<sup>T</sup>y ≥ c and y ≥ 0).  The slide highlights the relationship between the primal and dual problems, specifically how the objective function coefficients and constraint coefficients are transposed and reversed in inequality signs.  The notation uses standard linear programming symbols, with $c$, $x$, $b$, and $A$ representing the objective function coefficients, decision variables, resource vector, and constraint matrix, respectively.  The $y$ vector represents the dual variables.  The slide also shows the important relationship between the primal and dual objective functions, $c^T x \le y^T A x \le y^T b$, and $c^T \le y^T A$.  The notation $(x \ge 0)$ and $(y \ge 0)$ indicates the non-negativity constraints for the primal and dual variables, respectively.  The arrows and red boxes visually separate the primal and dual problems, and the dual problem is presented below the primal problem. This is the first slide of a presentation on duality, likely part of a linear programming course.

\textbf{Description:}

This is a crucial property for determining the optimal solutions to both the primal and dual problems.  The notation uses the same variables as the previous slide, with $z_j$ representing the slack variable for the $j^{th}$ constraint in the primal problem and $w_i$ representing the slack variable for the $i^{th}$ constraint in the dual problem.

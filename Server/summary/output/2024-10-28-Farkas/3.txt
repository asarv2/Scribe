\textbf{[mg] Farkas Lemma (Other Versions)}

6.4.1 Proposition (Farkas lemma). Let $A$ be a real matrix with $m$ rows
and $n$ columns, and let $b \in \mathbb{R}^m$ be a vector. Then exactly one of the following
two possibilities occurs:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item[(F1)] There exists a vector $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$ satisfying $Ax = b$ and $x \ge 0$.
    \item[(F2)] There exists a vector $y \in \mathbb{R}^m$ such that $y^T A \ge 0^T$ and $y^T b < 0$.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Fredholm Alternatives}

$Ax = b$

(F1) $Ax = b$ has a solution
(F2) There is $y$ s.t. $y^T A = 0$ and $y^T b \neq 0$

\textbf{Description:}

This slide continues the discussion of Farkas' Lemma, presenting yet another equivalent formulation.  It introduces the concept of "Fredholm Alternatives."  The slide explicitly states the two possibilities (F1 and F2) in the context of the equation $Ax = b$.  (F1) asserts that if a solution $x$ exists satisfying $Ax = b$ and $x \ge 0$, then (F2) cannot hold.  Conversely, if (F1) does not hold, then (F2) must hold.  This version emphasizes the relationship between the existence of a solution to $Ax = b$ and the existence of a specific vector $y$ satisfying certain conditions.  The notation $y^T A$ and $y^T b$ represents the transpose of $y$ multiplied by $A$ and $b$ respectively.

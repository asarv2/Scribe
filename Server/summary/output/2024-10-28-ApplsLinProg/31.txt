\textbf{Sparse Solutions of Linear System}

\newline
\textit{[EMG] p. 167}

\newline
Sparse solution of underdetermined system of linear equations

\newline
Given an $m \times n$ matrix $A$ with $m < n$, a vector $b \in \mathbb{R}^m$, and an integer $r$, find an $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$ such that $Ax = b$ and $|supp(x)| \le r$

\newline
if one exists.

\newline
($|supp(x)| = \# \{i: x_i \ne 0\}$)

\newline
Recovery of Signals with noise:

\newline
$w \in \mathbb{R}^k \implies$ encode $\implies z = Q w$

\newline
$z \implies$ transmit $\implies z_2 = z + x$ (noise: only a few zeros)

\newline
Given $z_2$, how to recover $z$?

\newline
$z_2 = Q w + x$

\newline
$A z_2 = A Q w + A x$


\textbf{Description:}

This slide continues the discussion of sparse solutions to underdetermined linear systems, but now introduces the concept of signal recovery with noise.  The problem is to recover a sparse signal $w$ from a noisy observation $z_2$.  The signal $w$ is encoded into a vector $z$ using a matrix $Q$.  The transmission of $z$ introduces noise, represented by the vector $x$, which is also sparse.  The goal is to recover the original signal $z$ from the noisy observation $z_2$.  The slide shows the relationship between the original signal, the encoding matrix, the noise, and the observed signal.  This is a common problem in signal processing and data recovery, where a sparse signal is corrupted by noise, and the goal is to recover the original signal from the noisy observation.

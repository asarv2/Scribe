Slide 14

\textbf{Portfolio Selection (Markowitz Model)}

\newline
\textit{[LV] ch. 14}

\newline
$\max \mu \sum_{j} x_j ER_j - \epsilon E \left( \sum_{j} x_j \tilde{R_j} \right)^2$

\newline
s.t.
\newline
$\sum_{j} x_j = 1$, $x_j \ge 0$

\newline
$E \left( \sum_{j} x_j \tilde{R_j} \right)^2 = \sum_{i} \sum_{j} x_i x_j \sigma_{ij}$

\newline
(variance)

\newline
$\sigma_{ij} = E(\tilde{R_i} \tilde{R_j})$


\textbf{Description:}

This slide further clarifies the calculation of portfolio variance in the Markowitz portfolio selection model.  It explicitly defines the covariance ($\sigma_{ij}$) between the returns of assets $i$ and $j$ as the expected value of the product of the fluctuations ($\tilde{R_i}$ and $\tilde{R_j}$) of the returns.  This is a crucial step in the model, as the variance of a portfolio is calculated as a weighted sum of the covariances between all pairs of assets in the portfolio.  The slide is likely part of a lecture or presentation explaining how to use linear programming to optimize investment portfolios, considering both return and risk, and how to calculate the variance of a portfolio from historical data.

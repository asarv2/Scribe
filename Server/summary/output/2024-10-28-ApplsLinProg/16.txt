Slide 16

\textbf{Portfolio Selection (Markowitz Model)}

\newline
\textit{[LV] ch. 14}

\newline
$\max \mu \sum_{j} x_j \overline{r_j} - \frac{\epsilon}{2} \sum_{i} \sum_{j} x_i x_j \sigma_{ij}$

\newline
s.t.
\newline
$\sum_{j} x_j = 1$, $x_j \ge 0$

\newline
\textbf{Quadratic Programming}

\newline
$\min c^T x + \frac{1}{2} x^T Q x$

\newline
s.t.
\newline
$Ax \le b$
\newline
$x \ge 0$


\textbf{Description:}

This slide presents the standard form of a quadratic programming problem, which is the type of problem that arises when solving the Markowitz portfolio selection model.  The objective function is to minimize a quadratic function of the form $c^T x + \frac{1}{2} x^T Q x$, where $c$ is a vector of constants, $x$ is a vector of variables, and $Q$ is a symmetric matrix.  The constraints are linear inequalities ($Ax \le b$) and non-negativity constraints ($x \ge 0$).  This slide is likely part of a lecture or presentation explaining how to use quadratic programming to solve the Markowitz portfolio selection model, which is a crucial step in optimizing investment portfolios.

Slide 14

\textbf{Portfolio Selection (Markowitz Model)}

\newline
\textit{[LV] ch. 14}

\newline
$\max \mu \sum_{j} x_j \overline{r_j} - \frac{\epsilon}{2} \sum_{i} \sum_{j} x_i x_j \sigma_{ij}$

\newline
s.t.
\newline
$\sum_{j} x_j = 1$, $x_j \ge 0$

\newline
(Quadratic function)


\textbf{Description:}

This slide presents the final, simplified linear programming formulation of the Markowitz portfolio selection model.  The objective function now explicitly maximizes the expected return ($\mu \sum_{j} x_j \overline{r_j}$) minus a portion of the portfolio variance ($\frac{\epsilon}{2} \sum_{i} \sum_{j} x_i x_j \sigma_{ij}$).  The constraints ensure that the total investment is 100% ($\sum_{j} x_j = 1$) and that the proportions of investment in each asset are non-negative ($x_j \ge 0$).  The objective function is now a quadratic function, which is a key characteristic of this particular formulation.  The slide is likely part of a lecture or presentation explaining how to use linear programming to optimize investment portfolios, considering both return and risk, and how to calculate the variance of a portfolio from historical data.

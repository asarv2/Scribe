Slide 12

\textbf{Portfolio Selection (Markowitz Model)}

\newline
\textit{[LV] ch. 13}

\newline
$\max \mu \sum_{j} X_j R_j - \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^T \left| \sum_{j} X_j (R_j(t) - \overline{r_j}) \right|$

\newline
s.t.
\newline
$\sum_{j} X_j = 1$, $X_j \ge 0$
\newline
($\overline{r_j} = \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^T R_j(t)$)

\newline
$\max \mu \sum_{j} X_j R_j - \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^T Y_t$

\newline
s.t.
\newline
$-\gamma(t) \le \sum_{j} X_j (R_j(t) - \overline{r_j}) \le \gamma(t)$
\newline
$\sum_{j} X_j = 1$, $X_j \ge 0$


\textbf{Description:}

This slide presents a linear programming (LP) formulation of the Markowitz portfolio selection model.  The objective function now explicitly includes a risk term, aiming to maximize the expected return ($\mu \sum_{j} X_j R_j$) while limiting the risk (represented by the sum of $Y_t$ terms).  The constraints ensure that the total investment is 100% ($\sum_{j} X_j = 1$) and that the proportions of investment in each asset are non-negative ($X_j \ge 0$).  The new constraints, $-\gamma(t) \le \sum_{j} X_j (R_j(t) - \overline{r_j}) \le \gamma(t)$,  control the risk by limiting the deviation of the portfolio return from its expected value in each historical period $t$.  This formulation is a key step in solving the portfolio selection problem using linear programming techniques.

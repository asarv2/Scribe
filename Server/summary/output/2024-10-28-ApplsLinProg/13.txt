Slide 13

\textbf{Portfolio Selection (Markowitz Model)}

\newline
\textit{[LV] ch. 14}

\newline
$\max \mu \sum_{j} X_j ER_j - \epsilon E \left( \sum_{j} X_j \tilde{R_j} \right)^2$

\newline
s.t.
\newline
$\sum_{j} X_j = 1$, $X_j \ge 0$

\newline
$E \left( \sum_{j} X_j \tilde{R_j} \right)^2 = \sum_{i} \sum_{j} X_i X_j E( \tilde{R_i} \tilde{R_j}) = \sum_{i} \sum_{j} X_i X_j \sigma_{ij}$

\newline
(variance)


\textbf{Description:}

This slide further refines the Markowitz portfolio selection model, focusing on the calculation of portfolio variance.  The objective function now explicitly includes the variance of the portfolio return, aiming to maximize the expected return ($\mu \sum_{j} X_j ER_j$) while minimizing the portfolio's variance ($E \left( \sum_{j} X_j \tilde{R_j} \right)^2$).  The variance is calculated as the sum of the covariances between each pair of assets, weighted by the proportion of the portfolio invested in each asset.  This slide is a crucial step in the Markowitz portfolio selection model, providing the necessary calculations to quantify expected return and risk based on historical data and covariances.  The notation $\sigma_{ij}$ likely represents the covariance between the returns of assets $i$ and $j$.

\textbf{Cutting Stock Problem}

\newline
\textit{[EMG] p. 267}

\newline
A paper mill manufactures rolls of paper of a standard width 3 meters. But customers want to buy paper rolls of shorter width, and the mill has to cut such rolls from the 3 m rolls. One 3 m roll can be cut, for instance, into two rolls 93 cm wide, one roll of width 108 cm, and a rest of 6 cm (which goes to waste).

\newline
Let us consider an order of

\begin{itemize}
\item 97 rolls of width 135 cm,
\item 610 rolls of width 108 cm,
\item 395 rolls of width 93 cm, and
\item 211 rolls of width 42 cm.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Description:}

This slide continues the cutting stock problem from the previous slide.  It provides a visual example of how a 3-meter roll of paper might be cut to satisfy an order for smaller rolls of different widths.  The diagram shows a 3-meter roll (represented by a rectangle) being cut into smaller pieces of 135 cm, 108 cm, and 42 cm.  The remaining 150 cm is marked as wasted.  This example illustrates the problem of finding the optimal cutting pattern to minimize the total waste while satisfying the customer's order.  The slide is likely part of a lecture or presentation explaining how to use integer programming to solve cutting stock problems.

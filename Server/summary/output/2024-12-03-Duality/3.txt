\textbf{THE GRAPHIC METHOD}

\vspace{0.5cm}

The geometric interpretation of linear programming leads to a geometric procedure for solving
LP problems with two variables. For illustration, let us consider the problem

\vspace{0.5cm}

maximize $\quad x_1 + x_2$

\vspace{0.5cm}

subject to $\quad 2x_1 + x_2 \le 14$
\vspace{0.5cm}
$\quad -x_1 + 2x_2 \le 8$
\vspace{0.5cm}
$\quad 2x_1 - x_2 \le 10$
\vspace{0.5cm}
$\quad x_1, x_2 \ge 0$.

\vspace{0.5cm}

Its region of feasibility is shown in Figure 17.7, together with the line representing all the points

\vspace{0.5cm}

$x_1 + x_2 = 2$

\vspace{0.5cm}

\textbf{Description:}

This slide introduces the graphical method for solving linear programming problems with two variables.  It presents a specific example problem, where the objective is to maximize $x_1 + x_2$ subject to a set of linear constraints.  The constraints define a feasible region in the $x_1$-$x_2$ plane.  The slide shows a graph (Figure 17.7) depicting this feasible region. The shaded area represents the region of feasibility, which is a polygon.  The lines represent the boundaries of the constraints.  The line $x_1 + x_2 = 2$ is also shown on the graph.  This line represents the objective function, and the goal is to find the point within the feasible region that maximizes the objective function.  This slide is part of a linear programming lecture, demonstrating the graphical method for solving such problems.  The figure is a graphical representation of the feasible region for the given constraints. The optimal solution will be one of the vertices of the feasible region.

Slide 5

This slide presents an example of a network flow problem, likely from a linear programming context.  It shows a directed graph representing a transportation network, with nodes representing cities (San Francisco (SF), Denver (D), Houston (H), Atlanta (A), and New York City (NYC)) and edges representing possible routes between them.  The numbers on the edges represent the capacity (or maximum number of seats available) for each route.  The diagram is accompanied by a table (Table 22.1) that shows the seat availability between pairs of cities.  The table provides the capacity values for the edges in the graph.  The goal of the problem is likely to find the maximum flow (number of passengers) that can be transported from San Francisco to New York City, subject to the capacity constraints of the various routes.  This is a practical application of the max-flow min-cut theorem discussed in previous slides.

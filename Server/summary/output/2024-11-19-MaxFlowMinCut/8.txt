\textbf{Proof of Max Flow = Min Cut}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Let $x_{ij}^*$ be a (optimal) max flow
    \item Let $C^*$ be those nodes that some more flow can be pushed from $s$.
    \item $t \notin C^*$ (By default, $s \in C^*$)
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Claim}
Flow ($C^*$ to $C^{*c}$) = $K(C^*)$
$\sum_{i \in C^*, j \notin C^*} x_{ij}^* = \sum_{i \in C^*, j \notin C^*} u_{ij}$

\textbf{Description:}

This slide presents the proof of the max-flow min-cut theorem.  It outlines the steps in the proof.  The first bullet points define the optimal maximum flow ($x_{ij}^*$) and the set of nodes ($C^*$) that can receive additional flow from the source ($s$).  The second bullet point states that the sink ($t$) is not in the set $C^*$.  The claim states that the flow from the set $C^*$ to its complement ($C^{*c}$) is equal to the capacity of the cut $C^*$, which is expressed as a summation of the capacities of the edges crossing the cut.  This is a crucial step in the formal proof of the theorem.  The notation $x_{ij}^*$ represents the optimal flow values, and $u_{ij}$ represents the capacities of the edges.  The sets $C^*$ and $C^{*c}$ are subsets of the nodes in the network.

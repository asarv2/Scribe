Slide 11

\textbf{Branch-and-Bound}

\textit{Discussion of Depth-First Search}

It is better going deep - depth search
before going wide - breadth search

There are three reasons why depth-first search is generally the preferred order in
which to fathom the enumeration tree. The first is based on the observation that most
integer solutions lie deep in the tree. There are two advantages to finding integer feasi-
ble solutions early. The first is simply the fact that it is better to have a feasible solution
than nothing in case one wishes to abort the solution process early. But more impor-
tantly, identifying a feasible integer solution can result in subsequent nodes of the
enumeration tree being made into leaves simply because the optimal objective func-
tion associated with that node is lower than the best-so-far integer solution. Making
such nodes into leaves is called pruning the tree and can account for tremendous gains
in efficiency.


\textbf{Description:}

This slide discusses the rationale behind using depth-first search in the Branch and Bound algorithm for integer programming.  It argues that most integer solutions are found deeper in the enumeration tree, making a depth-first approach more efficient.  The slide emphasizes two key advantages of finding feasible integer solutions early:  1) the ability to terminate the search early if a satisfactory solution is found, and 2) the potential for "pruning" the search tree by eliminating branches that cannot lead to a better solution than one already found.  The text highlights the concept of pruning as a significant efficiency gain in the algorithm. \textit{Discussion of Depth-First Search (cont.)}

The third reason to favor depth-first search is perhaps the most important. It is based
on the observation that as one moves deeper in the enumeration tree, each subsequent
linear programming problem is obtained from the preceding one by simply adding (or
refining) an upper/lower bound on one specific variable. To see why this is an advan-
tage, consider, for example, problem $P_2$, which is a refinement of $P_0$. The optimal
dictionary for problem $P_0$ is recorded as

$\qquad \xi = 205 \frac{5}{3} - \frac{5}{3} w_1 - \frac{1}{3} w_2$

$\qquad x_1 = \frac{5}{3} - \frac{1}{3} w_1 + \frac{7}{3} w_2$

$\qquad x_2 = 10 + \frac{1}{3} w_1 - 10 w_2$

Problem $P_2$ is obtained from $P_0$ by adding the constraint that $x_1 \ge 2$. Introducing a
variable, $g_1$, to stand for the difference between $x_1$ and this lower bound and using the
dictionary above to write $x_1$ in terms of the nonbasic variables, we get

$\qquad g_1 = x_1 - 2 = \frac{1}{3} - \frac{1}{3} w_1 + \frac{7}{3} w_2$.

Slide 28

Concept of a Dictionary of Variables

Initially: ($x_1, x_2, ..., x_n$, $w_1, w_2, ..., w_m$)
$n$ non-basic vars, $m$ basic variables

($x_1, x_2, ..., x_n$, $x_{n+1}, x_{n+2}, ..., x_{n+m}$)

$\begin{pmatrix}
x_{n+1} \\
x_{n+2} \\
... \\
x_{n+m}
\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}
b_1 \\
b_2 \\
... \\
b_m
\end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix}
a_{11} & a_{12} & ... & a_{1n} \\
a_{21} & a_{22} & ... & a_{2n} \\
... & ... & ... & ... \\
a_{m1} & a_{m2} & ... & a_{mn}
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}
x_1 \\
x_2 \\
... \\
x_n
\end{pmatrix}$

$\mathcal{Z} = c_0 + c_1x_1 + c_2x_2 + ... + c_nx_n$

Interchange one basic and one non-basic variable.

\textbf{Description:}

This slide reiterates the concept of a dictionary in linear programming, specifically within the context of the Simplex method.  It emphasizes the structure of the dictionary as a system of equations, where basic variables are expressed in terms of non-basic variables.  The notation is more general than previous slides, representing a problem with $n$ original variables ($x_1, ..., x_n$) and $m$ constraints.  The coefficients ($a_{ij}$) represent the relationships between the variables in the constraints, and the $c_i$ values are coefficients of the objective function.  The key takeaway is the concept of an interchange between basic and non-basic variables, which is the pivot operation in the Simplex method.  The slide is focused on the structure and organization of the variables within the dictionary, preparing for the iterative process of the Simplex method.

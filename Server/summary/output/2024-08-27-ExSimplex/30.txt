Slide 29

Concept of a Dictionary of Variables

(1) In each dictionary, setting $n$ non-basic variables to zero corresponds to a vertex in a polyhedron.
(2) Total number of dictionaries = $\frac{(n+m)!}{n!m!} = (n+m)!$

(Note: not all vertices are feasible)

\textbf{Description:}

This slide discusses the relationship between dictionaries and vertices in a polyhedron, a key concept in the Simplex method for linear programming.  It states that each dictionary corresponds to a vertex in the feasible region defined by the constraints.  The formula $\frac{(n+m)!}{n!m!}$ represents the total number of possible dictionaries, which is a combinatorial calculation.  The important note is that not all vertices in the polyhedron are necessarily feasible solutions to the linear programming problem.  The context suggests this is a theoretical underpinning for the Simplex method, explaining the relationship between the algebraic representation (the dictionary) and the geometric representation (the vertices of the feasible region) of the problem.

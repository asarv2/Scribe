\section*{Simplex in Matrix Notation}

\begin{align*}
\max \ \ &\mathbf{z}(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{c}^T \mathbf{x} \\
\text{s.t.} \ \ & \mathbf{A} \mathbf{x} + \mathbf{w} = \mathbf{b} \\
& \mathbf{x}, \mathbf{w} \ge \mathbf{0}
\end{align*}
$m \times (n+m)$  $\begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{A} & \mathbf{I} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{x} \\ \mathbf{w} \end{bmatrix} = \mathbf{b}$
$\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^n$, $\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{R}^n$, $\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^m$
$\mathbf{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$, $\mathbf{b} \in \mathbb{R}^m$

\vspace{1cm}

\begin{align*}
\max \ \ &\mathbf{z}(\mathbf{x}) = \overline{\mathbf{z}} + \mathbf{c}^T \mathbf{x} \\
\text{s.t.} \ \ &\overline{\mathbf{A}} \mathbf{x} + \overline{\mathbf{w}} = \overline{\mathbf{b}} \\
& \mathbf{x}, \overline{\mathbf{w}} \ge \mathbf{0}
\end{align*}
$m \times (n+m)$  $\begin{bmatrix} \overline{\mathbf{A}} & \mathbf{I} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{x} \\ \overline{\mathbf{w}} \end{bmatrix} = \overline{\mathbf{b}}$
$\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^n$, $\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{R}^n$, $\overline{\mathbf{w}} \in \mathbb{R}^m$
$\overline{\mathbf{A}} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$, $\overline{\mathbf{b}} \in \mathbb{R}^m$

\textbf{Description:}

This slide further develops the matrix representation of a linear programming problem, specifically for the simplex method.  It shows two formulations. The top formulation presents the standard form of the problem, where the constraints are expressed as equalities using slack variables ($\mathbf{w}$).  The bottom formulation, marked for simplex iterations, introduces overlined variables ($\overline{\mathbf{z}}$, $\overline{\mathbf{w}}$, $\overline{\mathbf{b}}$), suggesting a transformation of the problem into a form suitable for the iterative steps of the simplex algorithm.  The matrix notation $\begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{A} & \mathbf{I} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{x} \\ \mathbf{w} \end{bmatrix} = \mathbf{b}$ and its counterpart for the second formulation highlight the augmented matrix representation of the constraints.  The dimensions of the vectors and matrices are explicitly stated, emphasizing the linear algebraic structure of the problem.  This slide is likely part of a lecture explaining the simplex method in matrix form, showing how the problem is set up for iterative calculations.

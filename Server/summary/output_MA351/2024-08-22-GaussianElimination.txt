\textbf{Gaussian Elimination - Solving Linear Systems}

$m$ equations in $n$ unknowns ($m \times n$)

\begin{align*}
\begin{cases}
a_{11} X_1 + a_{12} X_2 + \dots + a_{1n} X_n = b_1 & \text{- (1)} \\
a_{21} X_1 + a_{22} X_2 + \dots + a_{2n} X_n = b_2 & \text{- (2)} \\
a_{31} X_1 + a_{32} X_2 + \dots + a_{3n} X_n = b_3 & \text{- (3)} \\
\vdots & \\
a_{i1} X_1 + \dots + a_{ij} X_j + \dots + a_{in} X_n = b_i & \text{- (i)} \\
\vdots & \\
a_{m1} X_1 + a_{m2} X_2 + \dots + a_{mn} X_n = b_m & \text{- (m)}
\end{cases}
\end{align*}

\textit{Description:}

This slide introduces the concept of solving a system of linear equations using Gaussian elimination.  It shows a general form of $m$ equations with $n$ unknowns.  Each equation is a linear combination of the unknowns, where $a_{ij}$ are the coefficients and $b_i$ are the constants.  The notation $(m \times n)$ indicates the dimensions of the system of equations.  The numbers (1), (2), (3), ..., (i), ..., (m) are used to label each equation.  This is a standard format for representing a system of linear equations, which is a fundamental concept in linear algebra and often used in various applications, including linear programming.  The slide is the first of a series of 20 slides, likely detailing the Gaussian elimination method for solving such systems.

(1) Interchange 2 rows (equations)

\begin{itemize}
    \item $( \leftrightarrow R_i \leftrightarrow R_j )$

\begin{itemize}

\textit{Description:}

This slide details the three elementary row operations that can be performed on a matrix (in this context, the augmented matrix representing a system of linear equations).  These operations are fundamental to Gaussian elimination, a method for solving systems of linear equations.  The operations are: (1) swapping two rows, (2) multiplying a row by a non-zero constant, and (3) adding a multiple of one row to another.  These operations are crucial because they do not change the solution set of the system of equations. The use of the notation $R_i$ and $R_j$ to represent rows is standard in linear algebra.

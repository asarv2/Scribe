Row Echelon Form (REF) (to facilitate backward substitution)

$X_1$ is eliminated

$\begin{bmatrix}
1 & * & \dots & * & | & * \\
0 & * & \dots & * & | & * \\
\vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots & | & \vdots \\
0 & 0 & \dots & * & | & *
\end{bmatrix}$

(m-1) x (n-1) system.  REPEAT!

\textit{Description:}

This slide continues the explanation of transforming a matrix into Row Echelon Form (REF).  It visually depicts a matrix in REF, where the first non-zero element in each row (the leading entry) is 1, and all elements below the leading entry in each column are 0.  The slide highlights the elimination of the variable $X_1$.  The asterisk (*) represents arbitrary numbers.  The notation "(m-1) x (n-1) system" indicates that after eliminating $X_1$, the remaining system has one less row and one less column.  The instruction "REPEAT!" signifies that the process of obtaining REF is iterative, and the same steps are applied to the remaining (m-1) x (n-1) sub-matrix to eliminate the next variable.  This is a crucial step in Gaussian elimination, which is used to solve systems of linear equations.  The slide is part of a larger presentation likely covering Gaussian elimination and its application to linear programming problems.

\section*{Vectors in $R^n$}

$\vec{u} = \begin{pmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ \vdots \\ x_n \end{pmatrix}, \quad \vec{v} = \begin{pmatrix} y_1 \\ y_2 \\ \vdots \\ y_n \end{pmatrix} \in R^n$

\section*{Scalar Multiplication}

$\alpha \in R$,

$\alpha \vec{u} = \alpha \begin{pmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ \vdots \\ x_n \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \alpha x_1 \\ \alpha x_2 \\ \vdots \\ \alpha x_n \end{pmatrix}$

--- Description ---

This slide continues the discussion of vectors, focusing on scalar multiplication.  It defines scalar multiplication as multiplying each component of a vector by a scalar value ($\alpha$).  The example shows a vector $\vec{u}$ in a 2-dimensional space.  The red arrow represents the result of multiplying $\vec{u}$ by a scalar $\alpha$.  The figure illustrates that scalar multiplication scales the vector $\vec{u}$ by a factor of $\alpha$.  If $\alpha > 1$, the vector is stretched; if $0 < \alpha < 1$, the vector is shrunk; and if $\alpha < 0$, the vector is flipped and stretched/shrunk.  The slide number is 2 of 14, indicating its place in a sequence of linear programming lectures.  The context suggests that this is a foundational concept for later discussions in linear programming.

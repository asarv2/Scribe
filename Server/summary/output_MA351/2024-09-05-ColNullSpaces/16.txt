\textbf{Feasible Region and Linear Combinations}

\vspace{0.5cm}

\begin{itemize}
    \item The feasible region in a linear programming problem is the set of all points satisfying the constraints.
    \item These points are linear combinations of the columns of the constraint matrix $A$, where the coefficients are the decision variables.
    \item The objective function is maximized at one of the vertices of the feasible region.
\end{itemize}

\end{document}

\vspace{0.5cm}

\textbf{Description:}

This slide connects the geometric concept of the feasible region in linear programming to the idea of linear combinations.  It states that the feasible region is the set of all points satisfying the constraints, which are linear combinations of the columns of the constraint matrix $A$.  The coefficients in these linear combinations are the decision variables.  Importantly, the optimal solution (maximizing the objective function) is found at one of the vertices of this feasible region.  This slide likely follows the previous ones, which introduced the simplex method, pivot operations, and the geometric interpretation of $Ax = b$.  It ties together the algebraic and geometric aspects of linear programming.

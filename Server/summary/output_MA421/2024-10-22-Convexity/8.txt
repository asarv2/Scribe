\section*{Convex Analysis [V] Chapter 10}

\subsection*{Theorem 10.2}
$S \subseteq \mathbb{R}^m$, $\text{Conv}(S) = \{ \text{collection of all convex combinations of finitely many points from } S \}$

\textbf{Proof:}
Let $H = \{ \text{collection of all convex combinations of finitely many points from } S \}$.
Need to show $\text{Conv}(S) \subseteq H$ and $H \subseteq \text{Conv}(S)$.

(1) $\text{Conv}(S) \subseteq H$
\textit{Proof:}
Let $z \in \text{Conv}(S)$.  By definition of $\text{Conv}(S)$, there exists a convex set $C$ such that $S \subseteq C$ and $z \in C$.  Since $C$ is convex and $S \subseteq C$, any convex combination of points in $S$ must also be in $C$.  Thus, $z \in H$.

(2) $H \subseteq \text{Conv}(S)$
\textit{Proof:}
Let $z \in H$.  Then $z$ is a convex combination of finitely many points from $S$.  By Theorem 10.1, any convex combination of points in $S$ is in $\text{Conv}(S)$.  Therefore, $z \in \text{Conv}(S)$.

\textbf{Caratheodory Theorem}
If $z \in \text{Conv}(S)$, then $z$ can be written as a convex combination of at most $m+1$ points from $S$.

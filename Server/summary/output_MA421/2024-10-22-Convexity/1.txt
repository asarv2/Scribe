\section*{Convex Analysis [V] Chapter 10}

\subsection*{Def 1}
$S \subseteq \mathbb{R}^m$ is convex if
for any $z_1, z_2 \in S \implies \lambda z_1 + (1-\lambda) z_2 \in S$
$0 \le \lambda \le 1$

\subsection*{Def 2}
Convex combination of $z_1, z_2, \dots, z_n \in \mathbb{R}^m$
$\sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_i z_i$
$\lambda_i \ge 0$
$\sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_i = 1$

\textbf{Description:}

This is the first slide of a three-part lecture on Convex Analysis, specifically focusing on the definition of convex sets and convex combinations.  The slide defines a convex set (Def 1) as one where any combination of two points within the set, weighted by a factor between 0 and 1, also lies within the set.  This is illustrated with a simple line segment in $\mathbb{R}^2$.  Definition 2 (Def 2) introduces the concept of a convex combination, which is a weighted sum of points where the weights sum to 1 and are all non-negative.  The slide visually represents convex combinations with examples of triangles in $\mathbb{R}^2$, showing how the combination of vertices results in points within the convex hull of the set.  The notation and figures are typical of a linear programming or optimization lecture, illustrating the geometric interpretation of convexity.  The handwritten style suggests a classroom setting.
